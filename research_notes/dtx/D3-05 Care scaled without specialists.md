# D3-05 Care scaled without specialists (agent: Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-13)

## Digest
(All IDs are D3-05-F##. Items tagged SNIPPET were not opened.)
1. IHCI keeps the protocol simple (3 to 4 steps, amlodipine first), gives free 30-day refills, and logs patients in the Simple app with overdue lists and nurse calls (F01-F03). Of 281,209 starters, 36% had no visit in Jan-Mar 2022 (F04).
2. Who changes the dose in practice: in Bathinda, paramedical staff refill when BP is controlled and uncontrolled patients are sent to a doctor (F08). No order letting a CHO change a dose was found (F30).
3. Why Punjab patients stopped coming: no sense of illness 24.8%, distance 22.2%, no reminder or guidance 15.2% (F07; 157 people, phone sample).
4. Scale: 75 million on standard care by Dec 2025 (F10, programme deck, THEIR CLAIM).
5. Tamil Nadu doorstep scheme: 1.86 crore beneficiaries in 4 years, 92.6 lakh hypertensives given drugs (F14). It is a refill service; no dose change at the door was found (F15).
6. CARRS: coordinators plus software, composite control 18.2% vs 8.7%, systolic 4 mmHg lower, more mild lows (F18).
7. Lesotho 2026: lay workers titrating one fixed-dose pill by app, 58% vs 48% controlled, OR 1.52, p=0.046 (F24).
8. South Africa IMPACT-BP: home cuff, worker delivers drugs, nurse decides remotely by app; systolic 7.9 to 9.1 mmHg lower at 6 months (F27).
9. Nigeria (no control group): control 21.7% to 55.9%; single-pill share 16.3% to 65.2%; centres with 30-day stock 27% to 96% (F22).
10. Kaiser: 43.6% to 80.4% controlled 2001-2009 with registry, guideline, single pill, medical-assistant BP visits (F20, SNIPPET).
11. Three-month refills for controlled patients: Uganda cohort, 96.4% retained at 12 months, control 73.5% to 78.3% (F36, no control group).
12. PAHO HEARTS: 33 countries, about 10,000 facilities; roadmap names multi-month prescriptions and nurse dose adjustment (F28).
Surprises: (a) the Tamil Nadu doorstep scheme shows no dose change at home; (b) ECHO India has no published effect on chronic disease control (F33); (c) two reports of IMPACT-BP give different control rates (F27).
Against the starting leads: lead 2 (a doctor or health worker approves each change) matches Bathinda practice, but Lesotho, Nigeria and IMPACT-BP had non-doctors change doses by app or standing order.

### Q4. CARRS (India and Pakistan)
- D3-05-F18 | CARRS trial: 10 outpatient diabetes clinics in India and Pakistan, 1,146 randomised (575 vs 571), type 2 diabetes with HbA1c 8% or more and systolic 140+ or LDL 130+; median follow-up 28 months; 89.6% completed. Care coordinators (non-physician): contacted patients at least every 3 months to arrange visits, phoned at least monthly on self-care, diet, exercise, tobacco, drugs; entered data into decision-support software and reviewed its printed prompts with the physician; 2.5 days training; caseload 50 to 80. Software gave individual prompts from guideline algorithms, prefer low-cost generics; physicians could accept or reject with a reason. Primary composite (HbA1c <7% plus BP <130/80 and/or LDL target): 18.2% vs 8.7% (abstract; results text says 8.1%), RR 2.24 (1.71 to 2.92). HbA1c fell 0.50 points more, systolic 4.04 mmHg more. Mild hypoglycaemia episodes 133 vs 21. Urban specialist-clinic setting. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6561084 | pub 2019 | OPENED | CITED
- D3-05-F19 | CARRS long term (search summary): first microvascular complications 32% lower at 6.5 years; gains seen only at sites that kept the intervention going. | https://theprint.in/ (press release) | SNIPPET

### Q8. What these programmes changed that software alone could not (facts only)
- Free or revolving-fund drug stock: Nigeria 30-day stock 27% to 96% of centres, drugs free for 17 months (F22); IHCI free drugs at all clinics (F02); China free or discounted drugs and free home cuffs (F21).
- A standing order or protocol that lets a non-doctor start or change drugs: Nigeria CHEWs (F23, SNIPPET); Lesotho lay workers (F24); China village doctors (F42); PAHO nurse dose adjustment (F28).
- Health workers carrying medicine and the cuff to the patient: IMPACT-BP (F27); Tamil Nadu (F14).
- Pay linked to control: China village doctors (F21, SNIPPET).
- Supervision and audit by a physician: China (F21); IHCI Senior Treatment Supervisors (F06).
- A person who calls and books the next visit: CARRS coordinators (F18); IHCI nurse calls (F03).
- Overdue lists: IHCI Simple app (F03). The list only works when staff act on it.
- Null result for decision support alone: mWellcare, nurse app, no systolic gain (D1-01-F46).
