## H. Tab: funded abroad

### 1. What this tab shows, and how to read it

This tab lists 32 companies and products abroad, most funded or launched in 2025-2026, in chronic care, AI clinicians, new sensors and frontier-lab health tools, with the Indian equivalent and one idea to borrow for each [D2-06-F1, D2-06-F15]. Funding figures marked "(search summary only)" were not opened at source, "YC mirror" means a secondary copy of the Y Combinator directory, and every traction number is THEIR CLAIM, not checked against a trial. A fund that "took part" put money into a round another investor led, a weaker signal than a fund that "led".

### 2. The closest relatives to our loop

These are the four funded companies nearest to our product: a home reading drives a protocol-based medicine change that a clinician approves. Plainly: the research found no funded startup in 2025 or 2026 whose funding page describes home blood-pressure dose steps by protocol, with or without a clinician signing. Cadence is the nearest: its funding page says only that its AI agents "support" medicine changes [D2-06-F1], but its own August 2026 page describes protocol-bound titration software that is in a US regulator's pilot and not cleared [D3-06-F31].

#### Cadence (US)

- **What.** Remote vital signs for older adults between visits; supervised AI agents flag risk and support medicine changes under the partner medical group's protocols; clinicians keep judgement calls; health systems pay. How far the AI decides alone is not stated [D2-06-F1].
- **Round.** $100M Series C, June 2026 (pages differ by a day, 23 or 24 Jun), led by Spark Capital; Thrive Capital, General Catalyst, Coatue, Duke Health and others took part [D2-06-F1]. No valuation is stated on the pages the claims check opened [D2-06-F1].
- **Regulator pilot.** On 13 Aug 2026 Cadence said its HypertensionOS, prescription software for clinician-supervised, protocol-bound initiation and titration of blood-pressure medicines, was selected for the FDA's TEMPO pilot; the company says this "does not constitute FDA approval or clearance" (THEIR CLAIM) [D3-06-F31].
- **Reports (THEIR CLAIM, Jun 2026).** 100,000+ active patients; 55% of alerts (vital-sign alerts in general, not medicine changes) resolved with no human adjustment; blood-pressure control up 70% relative; admissions down 27% [D2-06-F2]. Independently, the US review body PHTI (Oct 2024) named Cadence among programmes where a team adjusts medicine, the only kind with clinically meaningful falls in systolic pressure [D1-05-F1].
- **India differs.** No Medicare-type payer; 79.9% of required posts in four specialities were unfilled at rural community health centres in March 2023 [D1-03-F16], so the approver is a general doctor or health worker, not a pharmacist.
- **Borrow.** Report the share of readings needing no clinician touch, and clinician minutes per patient, as in the HyperLink pharmacist trial [D1-01-F04].

#### Closed Loop Medicine, WeDosify (UK, launched in the US)

- **What.** Personalised dose options for GLP-1 weight-loss medicines, shown to clinicians, from tolerability, goals, clinical data and response. Built on 29 studies and 15,000+ patient profiles with PK/PD modelling (how a drug moves through the body and acts). FDA status not stated [D2-06-F24].
- **Round.** NOT FOUND. US launch announced 18 Sep 2025 [D2-06-F24].
- **Status, Nov 2026.** Closed Loop Medicine Ltd has been in members' voluntary liquidation (a solvent winding-up by shareholders) since 28 Apr 2026 [D3-06-F07]. Its website now points to Morchella Medical, a 2026 company that licenses the patents and dosing models (THEIR CLAIM) [D3-06-F08].
- **Reports (THEIR CLAIM).** 91% of surveyed GLP-1 patients would stay on treatment longer with support [D2-06-F25].
- **Evidence.** Its one blood-pressure trial (UK, published 2024, authors from the company and a university) titrated amlodipine through an app in 205 adults over 14 weeks, with no comparison group; systolic pressure fell 11 mmHg [D3-06-F09].
- **India differs.** Generic semaglutide launched 20-21 Mar 2026 as key patents expired [D1-04-F10]; Healthify and Tracky sit at depth 3 with no proof of a dose algorithm [D1-04-F26, D1-04-F27].
- **Borrow.** The closest published depth-4 pattern to our engine: protocol as data, options to the clinician, response fed back. The step rule itself can be the product.

#### Counsel Health (US)

- **What.** An "AI native medical group": doctors in front, AI behind; prescribes and orders labs; the AI gets more autonomy as clinicians validate it [D2-06-F11, D2-06-F51].
- **Round.** Series A, 16 Oct 2025, co-led by a16z and Google Ventures; amount not stated [D2-06-F11].
- **Reports.** "Strong market adoption", no figures (THEIR CLAIM) [D2-06-F11].
- **India differs.** Telemedicine rules and the regulatory class of a dose-proposing tool are open (part F2).
- **Borrow.** Log every clinician accept, edit or reject; it is the safety record and future training data.

#### 9amHealth (US)

- **What.** Virtual specialists and clinical pharmacists manage medicines for obesity, diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol, with home lab tests; employers, plans and PBMs (pharmacy benefit managers) pay. Who changes doses is not stated [D2-06-F22].
- **Round.** $26M Series B, 13 May 2026, led by Define Ventures; total raised about $35M or $55.2M (search summary only) [D2-06-F22].
- **Reports (THEIR CLAIM).** "$50 million in projected" savings at two Fortune 100 employers, not realised; no blood-pressure outcome [D2-06-F23].
- **India differs.** Whether clinical pharmacists work in Indian primary care was not checked.
- **Borrow.** A "titration desk" between app and doctor [D1-01-F04].

**Also close, not a funded startup.** Omron, the Japanese cuff maker, runs Hypertension Plus (later Viso) in the UK: it collates home readings and drafts a medication plan that the clinician accepts or modifies (search summary only; THEIR CLAIM) [D3-06-F35].

**So what for us (OUR VIEW).** The pattern exists abroad inside broad virtual clinics and for weight-loss medicines, but nobody funded sells it as a blood-pressure product for a public programme run by health workers. The gap is real, and it also means no investor has priced it, so the pitch must carry its own trial evidence (part D).

**So what for us (OUR VIEW).** The labs are taking the "explain my numbers" job, so a reading app with a chat on top is a weak product. They stop before the regulated step, a medicine change approved by a named clinician, which is where our loop sits. Writing readings to Health Connect keeps our data open to whatever assistant the patient uses.

### 6. Patterns

OUR VIEW throughout, each point tied to table rows.

- **Money goes to AI clinician time and virtual clinics.** The largest rounds are Function $298M, Hippocratic AI $126M, Cadence $100M and Nourish $100M [D2-06-F42, D2-06-F20, D2-06-F1, D2-06-F5]; only Cadence describes medicine changes.
- **Blood-pressure money buys sensors, not decisions.** Biobeat, Aktiia and Vital Signals raised $50M, $42M and $15M+ at depth 1 [D2-06-F36, D2-06-F37, D2-06-F38], while the independent review found benefit only where a team adjusted medicine [D1-05-F1].
- **Who pays.** Health systems pay Cadence and led Wellinks's round [D2-06-F1, D2-06-F35]. Employers and plans pay Twin Health, Nourish, 9amHealth and Ilant [D2-06-F3, D2-06-F5, D2-06-F22, D2-06-F26]. Consumers pay only for tests and companions, Function at $365 a year and Bevel at $6 a month [D2-06-F43, D2-06-F33]; none of those changes a dose. This matches the wider record that scaled firms are paid by employers and insurers [D1-05-F12, D1-05-F13, D1-05-F15, D1-05-F16].
- **Safety is sold as a staged process.** Hippocratic trains each agent before release, Counsel widens AI autonomy as clinicians validate it, Tucuvi claims Class IIb device status [D2-06-F20, D2-06-F11, D2-06-F10].
- **Missing.** No funded home blood-pressure dose-step service; no funded heart-failure or kidney startup surfaced; nothing built for a public health system or health workers. Every outcome in the table is THEIR CLAIM.

### 7. Eight ideas to borrow for a home blood-pressure step loop

OUR VIEW, most useful first.

1. Protocol held as data, options with reasons shown to the clinician, response fed back (Closed Loop Medicine) [D2-06-F24].
2. Before any step up, a call in the patient's language asks whether the tablet was taken, as the Gujarat protocol requires (Tucuvi) [D2-06-F10, D1-06-F14].
3. Report readings closed with no clinician touch and clinician minutes per patient (Cadence) [D2-06-F2].
4. Log every accept, edit and reject as the safety and training record (Counsel Health) [D2-06-F11].
5. A one-page staged safety ladder: supervised first, wider authority after testing (Hippocratic AI) [D2-06-F20].
6. Alert on drift of the 7-day mean, not single readings (Prana) [D2-06-F13].
7. One reading schema (value, time, device, quality) for any cuff or watch (Junction) [D2-06-F30].
8. Cuffless or 24-hour readings as an input or one-off check only; dose decisions stay on cuff readings (Aktiia, Biobeat) [D2-06-F37, D2-06-F36].
