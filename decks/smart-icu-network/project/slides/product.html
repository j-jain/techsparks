Interface is a mock-up. Monitor reading: arXiv 2025 · Open ICU software: CARE · CDSCO: software proposes, never acts

Say: This is one bed. The monitor the hospital already has stays exactly where it is. We add a camera on the ceiling that looks at the monitor and the bed, so it reads any brand of monitor without rewiring anything, and it blurs faces on the device. A small clip-on mic takes voice notes and counts alarms nobody answered. A tablet on a pole at the bedside shows the nurse the checklist and the countdown to the next check, and lets the on-site doctor approve a step in one tap. One server with a battery backup sits in the ICU, so everything runs even when the internet drops. The network and power points go in while the block is being built. On the right is what people see: the bedside tablet showing the next step, where it came from, and the timer; and the specialist’s phone, which only lights up for an exception, here a hard stop because breathing is slowing. What is where (all OUR DESIGN; idea check sections 4 and 6): 1. Ceiling camera, one per bed, aimed at the monitor and the bed area. Reads heart rate, SpO2 and BP off the screen (preprint: above 98.9% accuracy on 6,498 images, D6-02-F18). Faces blurred on the device; only events and the frames attached to a proposal are stored. 2. The existing multi-parameter monitor (the block guideline lists one per ICU bed, D6-01-F01). No integration or rewiring needed. 3. Clip-on microphone: voice notes in local languages, and a count of alarms left ringing for the audit. No raw audio is stored. 4. Bedside tablet: nurse checklist and countdown timers; the on-site doctor’s one-tap approval of routine steps. One per two beds in the cost model (slide 7). 5. Ward server with UPS: runs the protocol engine and the audit log locally, so the bed keeps working offline; SMS is the fallback to reach the specialist. One per ICU. 6. Network and power points, camera mounts and the server space: written into the block’s drawings as a tele-ready build spec, because a retrofit costs more. Screens (mock-ups, no real patient, no doses shown): - Bedside tablet: the next step proposed from the poisoning protocol, the protocol line it came from, a countdown to the next check, checklist ticks, and Approve / Change / Call specialist. - Specialist’s phone: opens only for exceptions. Shows the breathing trend, two monitor frames from the bed camera, and the hard stop; Approve transfer or Video call. The on-site doctor confirms the order. - Regulation: the software proposes, never acts; it never drives a pump (CDSCO Class C reading, D2-04-F03).
