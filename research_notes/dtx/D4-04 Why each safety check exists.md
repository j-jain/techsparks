# D4-04 Why each safety check exists (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-14)

## Digest

The 12 facts that matter most:

1. Creatinine rises on an ARB because blocking angiotensin II relaxes the efferent arteriole, so filtration pressure falls; the rise comes in the first 2 weeks and settles by 2 to 4 weeks (D4-04-F26, F27).
2. Accepted rise: under 30% creatinine (or 25% eGFR fall) continue and recheck; 30% or more look for dehydration or NSAIDs, then stop or reduce; no trial tested the cut-off (F21, F23, F27).
3. Rises below 30% still carry graded risk: death IRR 1.15 at 10-19%, 1.35 at 20-29% (UK, 122,363 people) (F22).
4. Recheck creatinine and potassium 1 to 2 weeks (NICE, UKKA) or 2 to 4 weeks (KDIGO) after starting or raising an ARB; the Indian IHCI sheets ask for no recheck (F23, F27, F34, F57).
5. Potassium: caution above 5.0 before starting; 5.5-5.9 recheck within 3 days; 6.0-6.4 within 1 day and stop the RAS blocker; 6.5 or more admit. Potassium sets the heart cell resting potential; the ECG can look normal (F24, F34, F35).
6. RAS blockers in the 2nd and 3rd trimester stop fetal urine, so the amniotic sac drains: lung hypoplasia, skull and limb deformities, death (F02, F54, F56). Stop within 2 working days; use labetalol, nifedipine or methyldopa (F29, F30).
7. The first-trimester malformation signal (RR 2.71) shrinks to OR 1.14 against untreated hypertensive mothers (F31, F32).
8. Thiazide hyponatraemia: 2-year incidence 3.5-3.8%; HR about 4 in the first 30 days, 1.3 after a year; worse with age and low weight (F58, F61).
9. Chlorthalidone gives more hypokalaemia than HCTZ (6.0% vs 4.4%), no outcome gain; thiazide cardiac arrest risk rises with dose (F33, F37).
10. Amlodipine edema: 3.0% at 5 mg, 10.8% at 10 mg; arteriolar-only dilation raises capillary pressure; adding a RAS blocker cuts it 38% (F11, F65, F66).
11. Orthostatic hypotension affects about 22% of community elders (Kerala 9.6%), falls OR 1.73; hip fractures rise 43% in the 45 days after starting any antihypertensive; home self-testing works (kappa 0.90) (F39, F40, F42, F48, F50).
12. 180/110 without symptoms: 0-0.5% MACE at 7 days; emergency means organ damage; chronic hypertension shifts brain autoregulation upward, so crashing pressure is the danger (F68, F70, F72).

Surprises: US labels give no numeric creatinine or potassium thresholds (F04, F06, F15); thiazide hyponatraemia is far commoner than labels say (F58); intensive targets do not raise orthostatic hypotension (F44), but the weeks after starting do (F48).

## What this suggests for the product

- Suggestion: schedule a creatinine and potassium test 1 to 2 weeks after telmisartan starts and after 40 to 80 mg, and block the next step-up until it is back (F23, F27, F34, F57).
- Suggestion: encode the creatinine rule as a trend (percent change from the pre-telmisartan value), not pass or fail: under 30% continue and recheck; 30% or more goes to the doctor with a dehydration and NSAID checklist (F21, F22, F23, F27).
- Suggestion: encode potassium bands with recheck windows (5.5-5.9 in 3 days, 6.0-6.4 in 1 day, 6.5 urgent), and ask about potassium-based "low sodium" salt and painkillers before telmisartan (F07, F28, F34).
- Suggestion: a pregnancy and contraception question every cycle for women of childbearing age on or about to start telmisartan or chlorthalidone; "pregnant" triggers a stop-within-2-working-days alert and a drafted switch for the doctor (F01, F29, F30, F57).
- Suggestion: for patients 60 and over, add a sit-then-stand reading pair (1 and 3 minutes) plus a dizziness and falls question in the 45 days after each step; a 20/10 drop flags the doctor (F38, F41, F48, F50).
- Suggestion: sodium test within the first month of chlorthalidone, prioritised for older, thin patients and the hot season (F58, F61, F62).
- Suggestion: ask about ankle swelling after the amlodipine 10 mg step and send it to the doctor as a reason to move to the telmisartan step rather than stop (F11, F65, F66).
- Suggestion: a reading of 180/110 or more triggers a rested repeat and a symptom checklist (chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, vision, weakness): symptoms go to same-day care; no symptoms go to urgent doctor review, never extra doses to bring it down fast (F68, F70, F71, F72).
