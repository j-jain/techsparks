# D6-01 Smart ICU: the need, the Critical Care Blocks and the staffing gap (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-16)

Why this note exists: the user's new idea combines Cloudphysician (remote ICU) with Cadence (protocol loops) for rural hospitals, entering "before or during construction". This note checks the need side: what is being built, who will staff it, and what patients these ICUs will see. Players and evidence are in D6-02; protocols, rules and money in D6-03.

Fact IDs are D6-01-Fxx. OPENED means the page, PDF or abstract was read; SNIPPET means only a search summary was seen. Earlier IDs (D5-01, D5-02, D2-04) are reused where they apply.

## Digest

1. **The building wave is real and mostly unbuilt.**
   - Guideline target: 602 districts (D6-01-F01).
   - A Dec 2025 Lok Sabha reply puts approvals at 621 Critical Care Blocks and Rs 18,799.21 crore for FY 2021-22 to 2025-26 (D6-01-F03, SNIPPET).
   - An IMPRI analysis (Jul 2026) says Critical Care Blocks show "only 3% completion" (D6-01-F04). A current-affairs page gives "10 out of 394 units operational", with no source named (D6-01-F05).
2. **The government's own design budgets for remote consults, but little money.**
   - The official guideline lists "teleconsultation" inside the non-HR recurring cost: Rs 26.6 lakh a year for a 50-bed block and Rs 50 lakh a year for a 100-bed block. The same line also covers drugs, oxygen, maintenance and consumables (D6-01-F02).
3. **Staffing is the bottleneck.**
   - Norms: specialists round the clock and one nurse per ICU bed (D6-01-F02).
   - The central HR money runs only to FY 2025-26; after that the state pays (D6-01-F02).
   - The guideline budgets a specialist at about Rs 1 lakh a month (OUR ARITHMETIC from D6-01-F02).
   - India had 8,350 critical care specialists against a need of more than 50,000 (IMA via Reuters, 2016; D6-01-F09).
   - Machines already bought have sat idle for lack of trained staff (D6-01-F11, F12).
4. **The patients.**
   - The CCB guideline names the top conditions needing critical care: heart attack, stroke, COPD, ARDS, poisoning and septic shock (D6-01-F01).
   - In INDICAPS-II (132 ICUs, mostly larger hospitals), 29.3% of patients had sepsis or septic shock during the stay, and poisoning was only 1.5% (D6-01-F08).
   - Snakebite kills about 58,000 a year, 94% rural, 77% outside hospital (D6-01-F14).
   - A district-level ICU case mix was NOT FOUND.
5. **ICU deaths are high and rose between two national snapshots:** 18.1% (2010-11) to 23.4% (2018-19). The authors warn that the two samples differ (D6-01-F08).

## Findings

### What is being built: PM-ABHIM Critical Care Blocks

- D6-01-F01. MoHFW / NHSRC, "Guidelines on Critical Care Hospital Block, PM-ABHIM 2022" (preface dated 16 Mar 2022).
  - **Who gets one:** support for 602 districts. 102 districts with more than 20 lakh people get 50-100 beds, by existing hospital size. 274 districts with 5-20 lakh people get 50 beds. 226 districts with government medical colleges get a 50-bed block.
  - **Conditions:** "The top five conditions requiring critical care are Acute Myocardial Infraction [sic], Cardiao-vascular [sic] Stroke, COPD, Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome, Poisoning, Septic Shocks etc. (IHME, ICMR, Global Burden of Disease, 2019)."
  - **Bed mix:** a 100-bed block has 20 ICU and 20 HDU beds (each including 4 paediatric), 30 isolation beds, 12 isolation rooms, 4 dialysis, 6 MCH and 10 emergency. A 50-bed block has 10 ICU beds (including 2 paediatric) and a step-down unit; the 50-bed column is garbled in the text layer, so other counts are not quoted.
  - **Equipment:** a "multi para monitor with central station" is listed for every ICU bed: 20, 12 and 16 units for the 100, 75 and 50-bed blocks.
  - https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/Guidelines%20on%20Critical%20Care%20Hospital%20Block-PM-ABHIM_2022%20-%2003-11-2022.pdf (2022). OPENED (text extracted from the PDF).
- D6-01-F02. Same guideline, staffing and money.
  - **HR norms:** "Specialists - round the clock for OT, ICU, Emergency"; GDMO "1 for 10 beds - critical care area"; nurses "1:1 ICU; 1:2 Step Down Unit; 1:6 wards".
  - **Who pays:** HR support "only up to the scheme period, i.e. up to FY 25-26 and after that, states would be responsible", and "only contractual/outsourced Human Resource".
  - **Unit cost:** a 100-bed block costs Rs 44.50 Cr up front (construction Rs 30.55 Cr, equipment Rs 13.95 Cr) and Rs 7.912 Cr a year to run. A 50-bed block costs Rs 23.75 Cr up front (construction Rs 16.63 Cr, equipment Rs 7.12 Cr) and Rs 4.592 Cr a year.
  - **Annexure 3, 100 beds:** Specialists-7, Rs 84,00,000. GDMO-19, Rs 1,36,80,000. Nurses-129 ("at least 43 round the clock"), Rs 4,64,40,000. Non-HR recurring Rs 50,00,000, "including Equipment maintenance, Oxygen refilling, teleconsultation, drugs, consumables".
  - **Annexure 3, 50 beds:** Specialists-5 ("at least one round the clock"), Rs 60,00,000. GDMO-12, Rs 86,40,000. Nurses-66, Rs 2,37,60,000. Non-HR recurring Rs 26,60,000.
  - **Indicative pay (OUR ARITHMETIC):** Rs 84 lakh / 7 = Rs 12 lakh a specialist a year, about Rs 1 lakh a month. Rs 2.376 Cr / 66 = Rs 3.6 lakh a nurse a year, about Rs 30,000 a month. The guideline says the rates "are only indicative".

### ICU beds and the people to run them

- D6-01-F07. ICU beds in public facilities: a Dec 2024 Lok Sabha answer reports 37,834 ICU beds, including 9,873 paediatric ICU beds, counted in medical colleges, district hospitals and sub-divisional hospitals. Per the summary, adult ICU beds in those facilities total 20,953. https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2987466/1/AU4360_wkfa0t.pdf (2024). SNIPPET (sansad.in refused the connection). India had 2.3 ICU beds per 100,000 people in 2017 (Think Global Health, citing an Asian comparison). https://www.thinkglobalhealth.org/article/10-bed-icu-brings-intensive-care-units-indias-rural-towns-and-villages (date per page). OPENED.
- D6-01-F08. Divatia JV et al., INDICAPS-II, Indian J Crit Care Med 2021.
  - **Sample:** point-prevalence on four days (Aug 2018 to Apr 2019); 4,669 adults analysed in 132 ICUs.
  - **Deaths:** ICU mortality 23.4% (1,092), made up of deaths 15.8% and terminal discharges 7.6%. Hospital mortality 25.3%.
  - **Admissions:** medical 85.5%. Sepsis or septic shock during the stay 29.3% (1,368); septic shock 12.6%. Poisoning or overdose 1.5% (68), including 36 organophosphorus or organochlorine, 9 corrosive and 4 snakebite. Confirmed tropical infection 2.9%, of which dengue 1.99%.
  - **Sample bias:** 36.4% of ICUs were in hospitals with 500+ beds, which carried 47.5% of patients.
  - **Comparison with INDICAPS-I (2010-11, 120 ICUs, 4,038 patients):** ICU mortality 18.1% then 23.4%. The authors say direct comparison "may not be appropriate" because ICUs and definitions differed.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8645819/ (2021). OPENED.
- D6-01-F09. Reuters via Onmanorama, 8 Feb 2016, citing the Indian Medical Association: India needs "more than 50,000 critical care specialists" but has 8,350.
  - The same article: about 3,000 eICU beds in India. Apollo's Hyderabad eICU monitored 200 patients in six states, and Fortis Delhi tracked 350.
  - Hospitals charged "$10 to $30 a day" to monitor a patient remotely, against about $200 a day for critical care in a small-city hospital.
  - https://www.onmanorama.com/business/news/hospitals-doctors-icu-who-indian-medical-association-village-remote-areas-india.html (2016). OPENED. Old; the 50,000 method is not stated.

### What the patients are

- D6-01-F14. Suraweera W et al., "Trends in snakebite deaths in India from 2000 to 2019", eLife 2020;9:e54076. Data: 2,833 snakebite deaths among 611,483 verbal autopsies (Million Death Study, 2001-2014).
  - **Burden:** about 1.2 million deaths in 2000-2019, about 58,000 a year; 94% rural; 77% outside hospital.
  - **Where:** about 70% of deaths fell in eight states (Bihar, Jharkhand, MP, Odisha, UP, Andhra Pradesh including Telangana, Rajasthan, Gujarat).
  - **Treatment:** facility studies found antivenom cut deaths by over 90%. Government hospital reporting captured only about 10% of expected hospital snakebite deaths.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7340498/ (2020). OPENED.
- D6-01-F15. Snakebite at a sub-district hospital (Akhnoor, J&K): 96 envenomated patients; 39.58% got no formal first aid and 48.96% arrived after at least 6 hours. https://www.ijmedicine.com/index.php/ijam/article/view/2877 (date per source). SNIPPET.
- D6-01-F16. Pesticide suicides:
  - NCRB data: pesticides were used in 441,918 reported suicides in 1995-2015, 90.3% of them in 11 states.
  - NCRB 2024 counted 7,123 pesticide suicide deaths in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, described as one in four of India's total (The South First).
  - Implied national total: about 28,000 in 2024 (OUR ARITHMETIC, 7,123 x 4).
  - Under-reporting is noted.
  - https://thesouthfirst.com/health/a-cheap-herbicide-was-behind-nearly-1-in-4-suicide-deaths-in-2024-it-is-still-on-sale/ (2025); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7031890 (date per source). SNIPPET.
- D6-01-F17. Transfers:
  - **Neonates (BMJ Open 2021):** at a North Indian tertiary centre, 1,013 neonates arrived by referral and 305 (30.1%) died; 52% of the deaths came within 24 hours of arrival. The authors called for pre-referral stabilisation and dedicated transport teams.
  - **Under-fives:** transport longer than one hour carried an OR of 7.75 for death within 48 hours (another Indian study).
  - **Trauma (eastern India):** a transfer study notes there are "no guidelines governing the transfer of injured patients in India".
  - https://doaj.org/article/c40f039c5d2e43998643b54ed3cbd482 ; https://www.iapsmupuk.org/journal/index.php/IJCH/article/view/334 (dates per source). SNIPPET.
  - No adult critical-care transfer study was found.

## So what for the idea (OUR VIEW)

- The user's "enter during construction" instinct matches a funded national programme that is mostly unbuilt (D6-01-F03, F04).
- Each 50-bed block needs at least one specialist round the clock (D6-01-F02). The guideline pays about Rs 1 lakh a month per specialist (OUR ARITHMETIC), and the country is short of intensivists (D6-01-F09). So most blocks will open with GDMOs and nurses, not intensivists. That is the gap a protocol-plus-remote-specialist service fills.
- The money is thin.
  - The whole non-HR line, teleconsultation included, is Rs 26.6-50 lakh a year per block (D6-01-F02).
  - A service that replaces part of the specialist line (Rs 60-84 lakh a year) is easier to fund than one added on top. OUR VIEW
- The first protocol packs should follow the guideline's own list: sepsis, respiratory failure, heart attack and stroke, and poisoning (D6-01-F01, F08).
  - Snakebite and pesticide poisoning are rural and seasonal; they matter most in the eight high-burden states (D6-01-F14, F16).
