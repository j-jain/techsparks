### 4. Field voices on the Simple app and on what makes control improve

Found with the content search `"Simple app" hypertension India` (3 posts) and `"India Hypertension Control Initiative" Simple app` (1 post, the same as the second below).

- **National NCD consultant, UNICEF India (profile line), about Apr 2026.** Says the old response was to counsel patients and send reminders. From district clinics in Maharashtra and primary health centres in Pune and Gurugram (projects named PRANAA and SCREEN) the author lists the real failures: machines uncalibrated for months so readings could not be trusted, and a drug stocked out in week three with nothing to flag it. Credits a standard protocol, steady drug supply and the Simple app, which turned a missed visit into something visible. States control rose 48 percentage points in primary care and 23 in secondary care (POST CLAIM; study not named in the text read).
- **Medical doctor, public health specialist, WHO consultant (profile line), about Oct 2026.** Says programme counts of registrations and visits tell only part of the story; what matters is continuation, control and missed follow-up. Notes an observational New England Journal of Medicine study of electronic records and diabetes care, then the mWellcare trial in Circulation, which found no added improvement over enhanced usual care. Says ease of use matters and begins to describe a study of the Simple app (cut off).
- **Chief executive of INSTACARE, Chennai, about Aug 2026.** Promotes long-term condition support at home by a visiting team, for diabetes, hypertension and obesity.

Read across (OUR VIEW): people who run these programmes point to reading quality, drug supply and the missed visit, the same places the main triage puts the product. None of the three posts describes dose changes made from home readings.
