Say: Most people assume a camera and a remote doctor fix the ICU. The evidence on the left says they don’t. The biggest randomised trial gave 30 ICUs a remote specialist reviewing patients every day, over 17,000 patients, and deaths didn’t change. Pool 26 studies and over two million patients and the benefit is still unproven. The evidence on the right is different: when the next step is done on time, deaths fall. When doctors answered a sepsis alert within three hours, deaths fell by about a fifth. When a poisoning antidote was doubled every five minutes, patients were stable in 24 minutes instead of 152, and deaths fell by almost two thirds. We build the right side: the next step, on a timer, approved in one tap. How to read the slide: grey box = only watching; orange box = acting on time. The big symbol or number on each line is the effect on deaths: “=” no change, “?” unproven, “−19%” and “−64%” relative drops in deaths. Sources and caveats: - Remote ICU specialist: TELESCOPE trial, JAMA 2024. Cluster randomised, 30 ICUs in Brazil, 17,024 adults. Daily remote rounds led by an intensivist, plus audit and feedback. ICU stay 8.1 days with tele-ICU vs 7.1 without (P = .24); no secondary outcome differed; ICU deaths RR 1.09 (0.94 to 1.26). A summary adds that 74% of the remote team’s recommendations were accepted (search summary only). D6-02-F12, F15. - 26 studies pooled: Pscheidl et al., PLOS Digital Health 2025. Systematic review of 26 studies (3 randomised), about 2,164,508 patients. No meta-analysis because the studies differ too much; certainty for ICU deaths is very low and effects on patient outcomes are “highly uncertain”. D6-02-F15. (An older meta-analysis found benefit only in ICUs doing worse than predicted before tele-ICU: OR 0.64 there, 0.98 elsewhere. D6-02-F14.) - Sepsis alert: TREWS study, Nature Medicine 2022. 5 hospitals, 590,736 patients monitored, 6,877 sepsis patients flagged before antibiotics. When a clinician confirmed the alert within 3 hours, in-hospital deaths were 18.7% lower in relative terms (3.3 points absolute). Observational; several authors tied to the vendor; percentages from news summaries (search summary only). D6-02-F16. - Antidote: atropine trial, Bangladesh (J Med Toxicol 2012). 156 patients; doubling the dose every 5 minutes plus an infusion against usual bolus doses. Time to stable 23.90 vs 151.74 minutes. Deaths 8.0% vs 22.5%, a 64% relative drop (OUR ARITHMETIC: 1 − 8.0/22.5). One hospital, open-label. D6-03-F02. - The four studies differ in design and setting, so the slide shows direction, not a head-to-head comparison. - Also consistent: a tele-ICU that raised adherence to standard steps saw hospital deaths fall from 13.6% to 11.8% (Lilly, JAMA 2011, single centre; D6-02-F13). At home, only blood-pressure programmes that changed treatment moved blood pressure (PHTI review; D1-05-F1).
