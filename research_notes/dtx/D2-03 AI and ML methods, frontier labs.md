## Digest
Twelve facts for tonight:
1. PHYSIC (JAMA 2023, crossover, n=280): people differ in which BP drug works; choosing per person could add 4.4 mmHg on average; ACE vs ARB and thiazide vs amlodipine are interchangeable (D2-03-F12).
2. A one-time "responder" label from a short run-in did not predict 12-month response to potassium salt (Hypertension 2026) (F13). Personal response needs repeated periods.
3. Indian CDS trials split: CARRS positive (RR 2.24 composite) (F14); mWellcare null for SBP and HbA1c (F15); SMARThealth India null for BP, broken by fluctuating BP readings (F17); IMPACT Diabetes small positive (F18).
4. Twin Health Indian RCT: home BP monitoring guided drug changes, SBP -7.6 vs -3.2 mmHg, but secondary and company-linked (F19).
5. Adaptive vs static: DIAMANTE RL texts beat random texts on steps (F10); JITAI vs non-adaptive momentary interventions not different in sleep (F06); mental-health JITAIs g=0.15 (F04).
6. RL dosing: RL-DITR n=16 single-arm, glucose 11.1 to 8.6 mmol/L (F11). No RCT of RL dosing found.
7. LLM dose errors: protocol-constrained GPT-4 still 1.0% sentinel errors (F38); ChatGPT and Gemini dangerous anticoagulant advice in 12-20% of vignettes (F39).
8. OpenAPS safety rules give a template: bounded actions, revert to standing plan on missing data, never act on one reading (F46).
9. WHO SMART has no general hypertension DAK; the BP DAK is pregnancy-only and non-commercial (F50, F51). CQL engine and CDS Hooks are open (F52, F53).
10. ATHENA-HTN: 26 decision points gave 3,120 output combinations; 100 tests covered 1% (F55).
11. Open data: R `bp` package has 757 Ghanaian home-BP patients (F61); simglucose covers type 1 only (F49).
12. Indian speech: Sarvam STT ₹0.49 per minute (F58); IndicTrans2 and IndicConformer MIT (F59, F60); Bhashini voice 22 languages (F57).

Surprises: AMIE disease-management paper is Nature 2026 (not 2025) (F29). No completed RCT of an LLM coach with a BP or HbA1c outcome found (F44). The fully automated AI DPP app matched human coaches (F42).

Contradicts starting leads: lead 1 partly: CDS that drafts prescriptions did not beat enhanced usual care in mWellcare and SMARThealth India (F15, F17), so decision support alone is not the effective ingredient.

### Q1. Adaptive interventions: JITAIs and micro-randomised trials (MRTs)

- D2-03-F01. Definition, MRT method: JITAIs are "time-varying adaptive interventions" adapted "weekly, daily, or even many times a day"; the MRT randomises each decision point and is analysed with the weighted and centred least-squares (WCLS) estimator of "causal excursion effects", runnable in standard R. Psychological Methods 2022, PMID 35025583, doi 10.1037/met0000283. OPENED (EPMC). CITED.
- D2-03-F02. HeartSteps V1 MRT (Klasnja et al., Ann Behav Med 2019). Design: 6-week MRT; n=44 adults; US. Up to 5 decision points a day, randomised: suggestion or not, walking or anti-sedentary. Outcome: 30-min step count after randomisation. Any suggestion vs none: +14% (35 steps over 253 average), p=.06. Initially +66% (167 steps, p<.01), effect faded over time. Walking suggestion: +24% (59 steps, p=.02). Anti-sedentary suggestions: no detectable effect. PMID 30192907, doi 10.1093/abm/kay067, 2019. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limit: small, short, proximal outcome only.
- D2-03-F03. HeartSteps V2: a Thompson-sampling style RL algorithm "being used in HeartSteps V2 to decide, five times per day, whether to deliver a context-tailored activity suggestion", designed from V1 data (Liao et al., Proc ACM IMWUT 2020). PMID 34527853, doi 10.1145/3381007. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. HeartSteps II is a "yearlong micro-randomized trial" (PMID 40979180, 2025, secondary analysis, n=10 in that paper). NOT FOUND: a primary outcome paper for HeartSteps V2/V3 with a between-arm effect.
- D2-03-F04. JITAI meta-analysis, mental health (BMJ Mental Health 2025): 23 studies, n=2,563; small between-group effect g=0.15 (95% CI 0.05 to 0.26, p=0.003); risk of bias moderate to high. PMID 41027677. OPENED (EPMC). CITED.
- D2-03-F05. JITAI meta-analysis, behaviour (Digital Health 2023): 21 effect sizes, 592 participants, all pre-post designs; g=0.77 (0.32 to 1.22). Authors: "high-quality random[ised trials]" still needed. Design principles did not moderate effects. PMID 37521518. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limit: no controlled comparisons, so it does not show adaptive beats static.
- D2-03-F06. JITAI/EMI sleep meta-analysis (Sleep Medicine 2026): 11 RCTs (8 JITAIs); insomnia severity SMD -0.87 (-1.32 to -0.42); subgroup tests JITAI vs non-adaptive EMI "not statistically significant for any outcome". PMID 42994167, 2026. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. This is direct evidence that adaptive versions did not beat static momentary versions in that field.
- D2-03-F07. Monitoring of online RL in JITAIs: guidelines with red/yellow/green issue severity and pre-specified "fallback methods"; in two case studies monitoring "detected real-time issues" and fallbacks "safeguarded participants". NPJ Digital Medicine 2026, PMID 42082685, doi 10.1038/s41746-026-02669-4. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Directly usable as a design pattern for a dose loop (fallback to the fixed protocol).

### Q2. Bandits and RL in mobile health: deployed systems

- D2-03-F08. Oralytics (Trella et al., AAAI 2025): online RL algorithm deciding when to send tooth-brushing prompts; deployed in a registered US clinical trial with marginalised patients; paper reports design decisions and a re-sampling analysis, not clinical outcome; "A second phase (randomized control trial) ... planned to start in spring 2025". PMID 40452862, doi 10.1609/aaai.v39i28.35143. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. NOT FOUND: Oralytics phase 2 outcome results.
- D2-03-F09. Replicable bandits (Statistical Science 2025): standard estimators "can be inconsistent and fail to be replicable" on data from adaptive (bandit) trials; proposes "replicable bandit algorithm". PMID 41536936. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limit for any team claiming an RL arm proves effect.
- D2-03-F10. DIAMANTE (Aguilera et al., AHRQ final report 2025; NCT03490253). Design: 3-arm RCT, US safety-net clinics plus online, English and Spanish, diabetes plus depression. Arms: RL-chosen texts vs uniformly random texts vs weekly mood message. n=168 analysed. Adaptive arm +3.6 steps/day per day of intervention (95% CI 2.45 to 4.78; P<.001); post-intervention step increase 19% (P<0.001) vs 1.6% (random) and 3.9% (control). Follow-up 6 months. Funder AHRQ. PMID 42862923. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Note: one of the few head-to-head tests of adaptive (RL) vs static random delivery of the same content; outcome is steps, not HbA1c in this abstract. NOT FOUND in the abstract: HbA1c or PHQ-8 results.

### Q3. Dose-setting algorithms

- D2-03-F11. RL-DITR (Wang et al., Nature Medicine 2023): model-based RL for insulin regimens in type 2 diabetes, China. Development MAE 1.10 ± 0.03 U; inpatient validation MAE 1.18 ± 0.09 U, better than junior and intermediate physicians on blinded review; single-arm, patient-blinded proof-of-concept trial n=16: mean daily capillary glucose 11.1 (±3.6) to 8.6 (±2.4) mmol/L (P<0.01); no severe hypoglycaemia. NCT05409391. PMID 37710000, doi 10.1038/s41591-023-02552-9. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limits: inpatients, n=16, no control arm. Code or weights: NOT FOUND (not checked on GitHub; see NOT SEARCHED).
- D2-03-F12. PHYSIC (Sundström et al., JAMA 2023): randomised, double-blind, repeated crossover; Sweden; n=280 randomised, 270 analysed, 1,468 treatment periods (median 56 days); grade 1 hypertension; lisinopril, candesartan, hydrochlorothiazide, amlodipine. Outcome: ambulatory daytime systolic BP. Between-person response heterogeneity P<.001 for ACE/ARB vs thiazide and ACE/ARB vs amlodipine; large differences excluded for lisinopril vs candesartan and HCTZ vs amlodipine. Personalised choice could give "an additional 4.4 mm Hg-lower systolic blood pressure" on average. NCT02774460. PMID 37039792, doi 10.1001/jama.2023.3322. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Lead confirmed.
- D2-03-F13. Counter-evidence on apparent responders: China Salt Substitute Study run-in, n=608; run-in labelled 477 "sensitive" and 131 "resistant", but 12-month effects were -2.2 vs -7.2 mmHg with no difference (P=0.068); "no contribution of apparent run-in sensitivity". Hypertension 2026, PMID 41263071. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Relevance: a single home-reading response to one step is mostly within-person noise; personal-response models need repeated periods (as PHYSIC did).

- D2-03-F46. OpenAPS reference design safety rules (https://openaps.org/reference-design/, undated, OPENED): dosing only by temporary basal rates so that if the system stops, the pump reverts to normal basal; "OpenAPS oref0 cannot issue insulin boluses"; max temp basal capped by the lowest of several limits including "4x the current scheduled basal rate"; conflicting or missing data leads back to the pre-programmed basal; low-glucose suspend; max IOB default zero; each oref1 super-micro-bolus limited to "1/3 of the insulin known to be required". CITED. oref0 code: MIT licence (https://github.com/openaps/oref0, OPENED; README: "not a 'set and forget' system"). Loop (https://github.com/LoopKit/Loop, OPENED): open-source iPhone automated insulin app, "highly experimental", "not approved for therapy"; licence file not read. These are the clearest open examples of the design rule: bounded actions, fail to the standing plan, never act on one reading.
- D2-03-F47. FDA-cleared insulin dose calculator apps, product code NDC (openFDA 510(k) API, OPENED 2026-11-13): My Dose Coach (Sanofi, K163099, 22 Mar 2017); Insulia Diabetes Management Companion (Voluntis, K170669, 19 Jun 2017); Insulia Bolus Companion (K232451, 12 Dec 2023); iSage Rx (Amalgam Rx, K242066, 21 Aug 2024); EndoTool SubQ (K142918, 2015) and EndoTool IV 3.1 (K241088, Dec 2024); also BlueStar CGM insulin dose calculator (Welldoc, K222888, 11 Aug 2023, code QRX). CITED. Their internal titration rules were not opened (NOT SEARCHED).
- D2-03-F48. Warfarin dosing algorithm (IWPC, NEJM 2009): derived on 4,043 patients, validated on 1,009; pharmacogenetic algorithm identified more patients needing ≤21 mg/week (49.4% vs 33.3%) and ≥49 mg/week (24.8% vs 7.2%) than the clinical algorithm (both P<0.001). PMID 19228618. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Initial-dose estimator, not a feedback titrator.
- D2-03-F49. Safe learning and evaluation tools: "Guidelines for reinforcement learning in healthcare" (Gottesman et al., Nature Medicine 2019, PMID 30617332; title only opened, abstract not returned). Open Bandit Pipeline (`pip install obp`, Apache 2.0; DM, IPW, SNIPW, DR, Switch, MRDR, DRos and others; https://github.com/st-tech/zr-obp, OPENED). simglucose (`pip install simglucose`, MIT; UVA/Padova 2008 type 1 simulator with 30 virtual patients; gym and gymnasium interface; research only; https://github.com/jxx123/simglucose, OPENED). CITED. No type 2 or blood-pressure simulator found (see NOT FOUND).

### Q4 (part). Computerised decision support in India and South Asia: trial results

- D2-03-F14. CARRS (Ali et al., Ann Intern Med 2016): pragmatic RCT, India and Pakistan diabetes clinics, n=1,146; non-physician care coordinators plus decision-support EHR vs usual care; median 28 months. Composite goal 18.2% vs 8.1%, RR 2.24 (1.71 to 2.92); HbA1c -0.50% (-0.69 to -0.32); systolic BP -4.04 mmHg (-5.85 to -2.22). Limit: urban specialist clinics. Funders NHLBI and UnitedHealth Group. PMID 27398874. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. POSITIVE.
- D2-03-F15. mWellcare (Prabhakaran et al., Circulation 2019): cluster RCT, 40 community health centres, India, n=3,698 (3,324 completed), 12 months; nurse-run mHealth EHR plus electronic decision support for 5 conditions vs enhanced usual care. Systolic BP difference -0.98 (95% CI -4.64 to 2.67); HbA1c +0.11 (-0.24 to 0.45). Within-arm SBP fell 13.65 vs 12.66 mmHg. "We did not find an incremental benefit". NCT02480062. PMID 30586732. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. NULL.
- D2-03-F16. mPower Heart (Ajay et al., JAHA 2016): Himachal Pradesh, 5 CHCs, pre-post (no control); nurse enters data, phone CDSS generates a prescription "vetted by a physician"; n=6,797 enrolled; 18-month change SBP -14.6 mmHg (-15.3 to -13.8), FPG -50.0 mg/dL. PMID 28003248. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limit: uncontrolled; mWellcare (F15) shows usual-care arms fall similarly.
- D2-03-F17. SMARThealth India (Peiris et al., PLoS One 2019): stepped-wedge cluster RCT, 18 PHC clusters, rural Andhra Pradesh; ASHA tablet risk assessment plus doctor CDSS. SBP target 41.2% vs 39.2%, adjusted OR 1.01 (0.76 to 1.35). Only 37.2% of evaluation cohort exposed because of discordant risk scores driven by "fluctuating BP values" and seasonal variation. In concordant high-risk subgroup, BP medicine use 54.3% vs 47.9% (OR 1.22) but no BP effect. CTRI/2013/06/003753. PMID 30913216. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. NULL on BP. Lesson for the build: reading quality and BP variability broke the trial.
- D2-03-F18. IMPACT Diabetes (Sci Rep 2025): cluster RCT, 8 PHCs, 16 villages, two Indian states; ASHA CDSS-supported care under physician supervision, 9 months; n=418 with diabetes. HbA1c fall ≥0.5%: 21.8% vs 10.3% (p<0.05); physician visits 85.0% vs 29.8%; adherence 63.0% vs 43.1%. PMID 41120559, doi 10.1038/s41598-025-20728-w. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limit: small, "preliminary".

### Q5. Digital twins and personal response models (metabolic)

- D2-03-F19. Twin Health, hypertension in T2D (JACC Advances 2024): secondary analysis of an Indian RCT, n=319 with T2D (digital twin 233, standard care 86; unequal allocation). "A Home Blood Pressure Monitoring system guided anti-HTN medication adjustments." Only 59 (18.4%) on BP drugs. 1-year SBP -7.6 vs -3.2 mmHg (P<0.007); DBP -4.3 vs -2.2 (P=0.046); 68.2% of DT group remained off BP drugs vs none in SC; in the HTN subgroup, normotension 40.9% vs 6.7%. PMID 39372467, doi 10.1016/j.jacadv.2024.101172. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Limits: secondary analysis, small HTN subgroup, company-linked trial (funder not in abstract). India evidence. Closest published analogue to "home BP drives drug changes" with an ML layer; the ML piece is diet prediction, the BP drug changes are protocol-guided.
- D2-03-F20. Twin Health real-world 1-year (Sci Rep 2024): retrospective, commercial programme, 1,985 enrolled, 1,853 completed; HbA1c -1.8% (SD 1.7); 89.0% below 7%; diabetes drugs 1.9 to 0.5. PMID 39461977. OPENED (EPMC). THEIR CLAIM in effect (company data, no control).
- D2-03-F21. Method: ML platform predicts each person's post-meal glucose response from CGM, diet logs and other inputs and "dynamically adjusts dietary recommendations" (Front Endocrinol 2024, PMID 39634180). OPENED (EPMC). CITED. No open code or model found (NOT FOUND).
- D2-03-F22. Interim RCT report at 4 weeks (J Endocr Soc 2021 abstract): n=23 analysed of target 300; 8/23 (35%) off diabetes drugs with HbA1c reversal. OPENED (EPMC). CITED. Very early, small.
- D2-03-F23. GluFormer (Segal lab; arXiv 2408.11876, 2024): generative CGM foundation model, >10 million CGM measurements from 10,812 adults mostly without diabetes; generalises to 19 external cohorts (n=6,044, 5 countries, 8 CGM devices); in 580 adults with 12-year follow-up, top quartile captured 66% of new diabetes vs 7% in bottom quartile; 69% of cardiovascular deaths in top quartile, none in bottom. Multimodal version can "simulate outcomes of dietary interventions". OPENED (arXiv abstract). CITED. Journal version: NOT FOUND in Europe PMC by title. Weights: NOT SEARCHED.
- D2-03-F24. Other CGM foundation models: CGM-LSM (arXiv 2412.09727, 2024), pretrained on 1.6 million CGM records, 48.51% lower RMSE at 1-hour horizon vs prior methods; GlucoFM (arXiv 2605.30865, May 2026), 109,066 hours from 477 subjects, +4.1 PR-AUC points over best CGM-specific foundation model. OPENED (arXiv abstracts). CITED.
