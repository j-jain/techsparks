# D1-03 Burden and access gap (agent: Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-13)

## Digest

The 12 facts that matter most, then surprises and contradictions.

1. Diabetes in India: 11.4% of adults 20+ (ICMR-INDIAB-17, 113,043 people, 2008-2020), prediabetes 15.3%, hypertension 35.5% (D1-03-F01). Press count: 101 million, 136 million, 315 million (F02, SNIPPET). IDF Atlas 2025 says 89.8 million adults with diabetes in 2024, 43% undiagnosed (F28). Two sources, both recorded.
2. Hypertension cascade, NFHS-5 district paper: awareness 46.0%, treated 18.7%, controlled 32.9% (F05). Denominators need checking before quoting. Preprint on 1.69 million adults: about 1 in 3 diagnosed, under 1 in 5 treated, 1 in 11 controlled (F07).
3. LASI adults 45+: hypertension 45.9%, 55.7% diagnosed, 38.9% on drugs, 31.7% controlled (F25).
4. Diabetes control in Tamil Nadu household survey: 9.8% controlled, hypertension 35.4%, with over 95% of diagnosed on treatment (F09).
5. IHCI: 21,895 patients in 4 states, 51% returned for follow-up; control 26.3% at registration, 59.8% at follow-up among those who returned (F10). Protocol is amlodipine, telmisartan or losartan, chlorthalidone or hydrochlorothiazide (F11). Community survey in 9 districts: 21.2% controlled in 2023-24, a gain of 6.7 points (F12).
6. 75 by 25: 42.01 million hypertension plus 25.27 million diabetes patients on treatment by Mar 2025, 89.7% of 75 million (F14, SNIPPET). PIB 12 Aug 2026: 41.5 crore screened for hypertension, 41.3 crore for diabetes (F20, OPENED).
7. Specialists at rural CHCs: 4,413 of 21,964 required in post, shortfall 79.9% (March 2023) (F16). Doctor ratio 1:811 includes AYUSH and assumes 80% available (F40).
8. Specialist counts: only a 2017 committee figure: about 4,000 cardiologists, 1,200 nephrologists, 650 endocrinologists (F18). Government told Parliament speciality-wise data is not held. No current counts found.
9. Consultation length India about 2 minutes (2015), 2.3 minutes (2013) (F24).
10. Out-of-pocket: 39.4% of health spending in 2021-22, 43.41% in 2022-23 (F23).
11. Digital: 622 million smartphone owners and 915 million internet users (Nielsen, F41); 96.43 crore ABHA IDs, 49-50 crore eSanjeevani consultations (F19, F21); women own phones less: NFHS-6 63.6% (rural 57.4%) (F42).
12. Global: 1.4 billion with hypertension, 23% controlled, two-thirds in LMICs (F32); 830 million with diabetes, 59% of those 30+ untreated (F33). Japan: about 43 million hypertensive, 29.4% aged 65+ (F47, F48).

Surprises:
- Tamil Nadu: over 95% of diagnosed patients are on treatment, yet only 9.8% of diabetics are controlled (F09). Treatment access is not the same as control.
- IHCI: half of registered patients did not return, yet control more than doubled among returners (F10). Return rate is the gap.
- NFHS-6 (2023-24) fact sheets exist; sugar and BP numbers rose (F22) but I only have secondary reports.

Conflicts with starting leads and each other:
- Hypertension 35.5% (INDIAB) vs 22.6% or 21-24% (NFHS-5) vs 45.9% (LASI, age 45+). Diabetes 101 million (INDIAB) vs 89.8 million (IDF 2024).
- Control among those treated vs among all: 73.7% (F06) vs 31.7% (F25) vs 10% (F26) vs 1 in 11 (F07). Definitions differ.
- Lead "315 million hypertension" confirmed only through press (SNIPPET). Lead "BMJ Open 2017" confirmed (OPENED). Lead "Lancet Regional Health SE Asia low control paper" NOT FOUND by name.
- Lead 2 (prescriber time is the binding limit) has support: 2 minutes per visit, 80% specialist gap, 51% IHCI return.
