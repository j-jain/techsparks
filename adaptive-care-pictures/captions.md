# Captions: home blood-pressure step loop pictures

- `10-bp-patient-chat.png`: The patient sends a photo of the cuff screen in a chat, confirms the reading and that she took her medicine, and the next day gets the doctor-approved new dose in Hindi. Mock-up with example data.
- `11-bp-doctor-queue.png`: The doctor's queue of proposals, each with the 7-day home average, the current step, the proposed action and Approve or Review. Mock-up with example data.
- `12-bp-doctor-detail.png`: One proposal opened, with valid and rejected home readings, medicine taken, the protocol path, the reasons for the step and the safety checks. Mock-up with example data.
- `13-reading-quality-clip.png`: A kit-built clip on the cuff checks arm position, rest and stillness, so a badly taken reading is repeated and never changes a dose.
- `14-system-map.png`: The loop from home reading to engine to clinic approval and back home, with protocols loaded as data files.
- `15-same-loop-new-protocol.png`: The same loop can run insulin, heart-failure and warfarin rules by changing only the home signal and the protocol file.
- `20-what-lowers-pressure.png`: Home readings or reminders alone barely lower blood pressure, while trials where the readings lead to a medicine change show falls of 3.5 to 9.7 mmHg against usual care.
- `21-share-controlled.png`: In the United States, rural China and India, programmes that let someone step the dose roughly double the share of patients with blood pressure under control.
- `22-india-cascade.png`: In the first nine districts of India's hypertension programme, control rose from 14.5% to 21.2% between 2018-19 and 2023-24, about one in five.
- `23-market-size.png`: The home blood-pressure step loop market in India, from ₹7,494 crore a year in total down to ₹2.1 crore by year 3, with the note that the US$100 million-a-year test fails on India alone.
- `24-option-scores.png`: Seven adaptive-care options scored on the same six weighted tests, with the home blood-pressure step loop first at 4.35 out of 5.
