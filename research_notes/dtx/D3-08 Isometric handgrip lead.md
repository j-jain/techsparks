# D3-08 Isometric handgrip lead (agent: Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-13)

Lead tested: isometric handgrip training lowers blood pressure; a cheap load-cell device could deliver and dose it at home with home readings as feedback. Source tags: CITED = abstract or page opened (PubMed/Europe PMC abstract text, PMC full text, FDA letter, ClinicalTrials.gov record, guideline PDF). Where only an abstract was opened, the fact says "abstract". SNIPPET = search snippet only. No ranking, no conclusions.

## Digest

Twelve facts that matter for tonight:

1. D3-08-F01: The 2023 BJSM network meta-analysis (270 trials, 15,827 people) gave isometric training -8.24/-4.00 mmHg, the largest of five modes; its confidence intervals could not be read (full text blocked). CITED (abstract).
2. D3-08-F04: Handgrip only, people with hypertension, 7 trials (2023 meta-analysis): -6.7 mmHg systolic (95% CI -10.3 to -3.4), -4.5 diastolic (-7.3 to -1.7). Prediction interval for diastolic crosses zero (-10.2 to +1.2). CITED.
3. D3-08-F05: Largest handgrip-specific pool, 31 studies, 905 people (2026): -5.38 systolic (-6.91 to -3.85), -2.71 diastolic; I-squared 86% and 90%. CITED.
4. D3-08-F07: Ambulatory pressure is weaker than clinic pressure: 24-h systolic -1.31 (-3.84 to +1.22) in a 2024 handgrip meta-analysis. CITED.
5. D3-08-F10: Sham-controlled trial of 75 hypertensive adults (Pagonas 2017): handgrip at 30% grip force gave no significant change; 24-h systolic fell only in aerobic exercise. CITED.
6. D3-08-F11: Home-based trial, 48 adults, 20 weeks (Denmark 2023): systolic difference 8.12 mmHg in favour of usual care (95% CI 0.24 to 16.01); no home-pressure differences. CITED.
7. D3-08-F13: Indian home-based trial, 40 adults, 8 weeks: systolic -8.75 mmHg (CI -6.51 to -10.39), but only a "partial preliminary findings" report with implausibly narrow intervals. CITED (abstract).
8. D3-08-F16: Usual protocol: 4 contractions of 2 min at 30% of maximum grip force, 1 to 4 min rests, 3 sessions a week, 8 to 12 weeks. CITED.
9. D3-08-F19: The effect fades after stopping: gone within 10 days in one trial, 5 weeks in another; one leg-extension trial kept it 8 weeks. CITED (review text).
10. D3-08-F21: ESC 2024 lists "dynamic or isometric resistance training" (Class 1, as a complement to aerobic exercise); the AHA/ACC 2025 table says "aerobic exercise and/or resistance training" with no isometric wording. CITED (guideline-at-a-glance PDF).
11. D3-08-F27: Zona Plus, the main device sold for this: FDA warning letter Dec 2019 says it was marketed "without marketing clearance or approval"; a 2009 510(k) was not found substantially equivalent. Pivotal sham-controlled trial terminated 2022; new pivotal trial recruiting. CITED.
12. D3-08-F31: Low-cost HX711 load-cell amplifier: Pearson r 0.947 to 0.992 against a commercial conditioner and a handheld dynamometer, inter-day ICC 0.958 (Sensors 2020, forearm push, not grip). CITED (abstract + PMC).

Surprises:
- The same research group that wrote the 2023 network meta-analysis co-authored a 2026 ambulatory-pressure network meta-analysis that says data were "insufficient" for isometric training; a second 2026 BJSM network meta-analysis (24-h pressure) calls isometric evidence "uncertain".
- A 2026 network meta-analysis of 284 trials ranks aquatic exercise first, not isometric (F03).
- An FDA warning letter exists for the best-known handgrip device (F27); retailer "FDA cleared" claims are not supported by that record.

Contradicts the lead: the effect is real in the clinic-pressure pooled data but is smaller than the headline -8.24 in newer, bigger pools; null results exist in sham-controlled and home trials; ambulatory effect is small or absent; no cleared device; Indian guideline wording not found.

## Findings

### Q1. The 2023 BJSM network meta-analysis, and later work

- D3-08-F01 Edwards JJ, Deenmamode AHP, Griffiths M, Arnold O, Cooper NJ, Wiles JD, O'Driscoll JM. Br J Sports Med 2023;57(20):1317-1326. Systematic review and network meta-analysis; PubMed, Cochrane, Web of Science; randomised trials 1990 to Feb 2023, exercise of 2 weeks or more against a non-exercise control. 270 trials, 15,827 people. Pairwise changes in resting systolic/diastolic pressure (mmHg): isometric -8.24/-4.00; combined -6.04/-2.54; dynamic resistance -4.55/-3.04; aerobic -4.49/-2.53; high-intensity interval -4.08/-2.50 (all p<0.001). Network ranking for systolic (SUCRA): isometric 98.3%, combined 75.7%, dynamic resistance 46.1%, aerobic 40.5%, interval 39.4%. Isometric wall squat top sub-mode for systolic (SUCRA 90.4%). Authors: results "should inform future exercise guideline recommendations". Competing interests: none declared. Published 25 Jul 2023. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37491419/
  - Confidence intervals and the paper's own stated limitations: NOT FOUND (full text on bjsm.bmj.com returned 403; the Canterbury repository page shows only the abstract; not in PMC). A reader of the abstract sees no interval for any estimate.
  - The headline figure pools all isometric modes (handgrip, wall squat, leg extension), not handgrip alone.
- D3-08-F02 Mallmann Schneider V et al, Br J Sports Med 2026;60(13):983-990 (published 6 Jul 2026). Network meta-analysis, adults with hypertension, 24-hour ambulatory pressure, 31 trials, 67 arms, 1,345 people. 24-h systolic against control (mean difference, 95% credible interval): combined -6.18 (-11.45 to -1.21), aerobic -4.73 (-7.53 to -2.01), interval -5.71 (-11.31 to -0.002). Isometric not listed as significant. Authors: "Evidence for dynamic and isometric resistance training remains uncertain." CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42120187/
- D3-08-F03a Anderson VA, Kambo K, Bruce-Low S, Galbraith A, O'Driscoll JM, Edwards JJ. J Hypertens 2026;44(7):1087-1096 (same group as F01). Bayesian network meta-analysis, 24-h systolic ambulatory pressure; 25 studies, 1,096 people, 16 in the network. Aerobic -4.77, interval -6.86, resistance -2.25 mmHg. "Data were insufficient to analyse comparative effects for CT or IET" (combined or isometric). No significant differences between modes. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42159444/
- D3-08-F03 Yuan Y et al, Maturitas 2026 (published 16 Oct 2026): Bayesian network meta-analysis, 284 trials, 18,750 people with prehypertension or hypertension. Largest systolic fall: aquatic exercise -10.28 (95% CrI -13.05 to -7.49); largest diastolic fall: mind-body -4.69. Isometric listed as "favourable" effect estimates; modelled isometric maximum -13.52 systolic (CrI -23.22 to -3.86) at 750 MET-minutes a week. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42887271/
- D3-08-F03b Xin X et al, J Am Heart Assoc 2026 (14 May): network meta-analysis, 105 trials, prehypertension and hypertension. Combined training -12.05 and interval training -10.97 systolic; "isometric exercise training, and resistance training showed relatively weaker effects". CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42132182/
- D3-08-F03c Etayo-Urtasun P et al, Hypertens Res 2026: 48 studies, 2,085 adults on stable blood-pressure medicine; exercise gave office systolic -7.9 mmHg (95% CI -10.3 to -5.6), I-squared 78%, certainty low; 24-h ambulatory systolic not significant; "No exercise modality (aerobic, resistance, combined, high-intensity interval, or isometric training) was significantly superior to another". CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42642628/
- The 2024 expert review by the same Canterbury group (Edwards, Coleman, Ritti-Dias, Farah, Stensel, Lucas, Millar, Gordon, Schultz, Wiles, O'Driscoll; Sports Med 2024, PMC11239608) states the field's own limits: "The majority of published IET research also fails to effectively control for non-specific factors, such as the placebo effect." and notes lack of large-scale studies compared with drug trials. CITED (PMC full text).

### Q2. Handgrip specifically; people with hypertension; medicines; sham trials; duration

- D3-08-F04 Oliveira PC, Silva MR, Lehnen AM, Waclawovsky G. J Hum Hypertens 2023;37:844-853. Handgrip only, adults with hypertension, no comorbid conditions; 9 trials (7 chronic training). Training: systolic -6.7 mmHg (95% CI -10.3 to -3.4); diastolic -4.5 (-7.3 to -1.7); 95% prediction interval systolic -10.9 to -2.5, diastolic -10.2 to +1.2. A single session: no effect on pressure. GRADE and RoB2 used. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36379974/
- D3-08-F05 Lin CW, Jun IB, Igbo V et al. J Clin Hypertens 2026;28(4):e70246. Handgrip, normotensive and hypertensive, 31 studies, 905 people, searched to June 2025. Evidence rated "moderate". CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41922197/
- D3-08-F06 Liu R et al. BMC Public Health 2026;26:2775 (15 Jun 2026): handgrip, 20 trials, 626 people, includes non-randomised trials. Systolic -4.05 (95% CI -5.58 to -2.52 as reported: mean difference 4.05, CI 2.52 to 5.58), diastolic -2.26 (0.78 to 3.74). Largest effect in high-normal baseline pressure. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42289678/

Awake diastolic -2.02 (-3.89 to -0.15); 24-h systolic -1.31 (-3.84 to +1.22), awake systolic -0.93 (-3.10 to +1.23), asleep systolic -0.84 (-3.31 to +1.63): not significant. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30889048/

Awake ambulatory systolic 121.8 vs 124.2 mmHg (p=0.017) and diastolic 76.3 vs 80.8 (p=0.009) against control; 24-h and sleep pressure not different; leg extension arm no difference. Authors call the differences "modest". CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42928564/

- D3-08-F10 Sham-controlled trials (handgrip):

Positive but small; no true no-exercise arm. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28033302/

- D3-08-F12 Low-intensity Japanese trial: Nemoto Y et al, Hypertens Res 2025;48:710-719. 60 treated adults, mean age 66.9, 4 x 2 min at 15% of maximum force, 12 weeks then 12 weeks without training, home blood pressure as outcome. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39443704/

### Q3. Protocol and guidelines

- D3-08-F16 Usual handgrip protocol (2024 review table, PMC full text): 4 contractions of 2 min each, 30% of maximum voluntary contraction, 1-min rest (some trials 3 to 4 min), unilateral or alternating hands, 3 sessions a week, 8 to 12 weeks (range 4 to 20). Session time "approximately 11-20 min". Weaker protocols: 15% at 12 weeks (F12), 5 per week gave no extra effect in one comparison. CITED. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11239608/
  - Dose-response analysis: below 30% gave smaller effects; frequency above 3 days a week "associated with a diminished effect in SBP" (F05). Suggested evidence-informed protocol in F05: four sets of 2-min contractions at 30% or more, up to 3 times a week, at least 8 weeks.
  - Re-test of maximum grip each session is advised: "retesting MVC and recalculating 30% MVC, every session" (Baffour-Awuah 2023, PMC10015931; CITED). Dose is a share of the person's own maximum, so grip force must be measured.
- D3-08-F21 Guidelines:
  - ESC 2024 (as printed in the 2025 JACC guideline-at-a-glance comparison table), Class 1: "Moderate intensity aerobic exercise of ≥150 min/wk ... should be complemented with low- or moderate-intensity dynamic or isometric resistance training (2-3 times/wk) to reduce BP and CVD risk." CITED (PDF opened, text extracted; ESC wording appears in the ESC column). https://api.iscpcardio.org/storage/guidelines/___25_Hypertension_HT_AHA_ACC_guideline_glance.pdf
  - AHA/ACC 2025 (same table, Class 1): "increasing physical activity, through a structured exercise program that includes aerobic exercise and/or resistance training". The word isometric does not appear in this table. A secondary review (Curr Cardiol Rep 2026, PMC13550040) says "Isometric resistance exercise and stress-management practices ... may be reasonable adjuncts" about the 2025 guideline: SNIPPET-grade (secondary; the guideline text itself 403-blocked).
  - D3-08-F21b India: the national Standard Treatment Guideline for hypertension (MOHFW, 54-page PDF, text extracted) has no mention of isometric or handgrip exercise; its prevention line says "increasing exercise levels in the sedentary". The Kerala hypertension protocol (1 page) says "Regular exercise Moderate intensity, 30 minutes, 5 days a week", no isometric mention. CITED (PDFs opened, keyword search). https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/6591.pdf ; https://resolvetosavelives.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/kerala-a1-hypertension-protocol.pdf (both undated).
  - NOT FOUND: ISH 2020 guideline wording (full text blocked; AAFP summary mentions resistance training 2 to 3 days a week, no isometric mention, SNIPPET). NOT FOUND: Indian Guidelines on Hypertension IV 2019 and ICMR/IHCI wording. No Indian source found recommending isometric exercise. NOT FOUND: Hypertension Canada wording.
  - The 2017 Pagonas paper says isometric exercise "is currently not recommended" by American and European guidelines (as of 2017; F10).

### Q4. Safety

- D3-08-F22 Pressure during a sustained grip: at 30% of maximum grip force pressure rises 20 to 30 mmHg in normotensive and 40 to 50 mmHg in hypertensive people, heart rate 5 to 10 beats, versus 50 to 80 mmHg and 40 to 60 beats for moderate aerobic exercise (table in a 2023 evidence guide; figures taken from its reference 36). Contractions at 50% of maximum "or above" can produce pressures above 200/100 mmHg and "should be avoided in all but young, healthy people". Pressure returns near baseline in seconds after stopping. CITED (PMC full text). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015931/
- D3-08-F23 Reported harms: Hansford 2021: risk ratio for adverse events 1.12 (95% CI 0.47 to 2.68); extrapolated "one adverse event per 38,444 bouts" (the 2024 review quotes 28,428 bouts from the prehypertension subset, eight events versus one in controls). Reviewers warn about reporting bias and "limited quality". Common complaint: burning or tingling in the forearm that stops after the contraction. CITED. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34385688/ ; PMC11239608.
- D3-08-F24 Who is told to avoid it: "strongly contraindicated (although on the basis of low-quality evidence)" in connective tissue disorders such as Marfan syndrome and in thoracic aortic disease; red flags: unstable angina, uncontrolled grade II or higher hypertension, arrhythmia; screening before starting; people over 50 or with preeclampsia history should check with a doctor. Modified Delphi (13 experts completed round 1): consensus on efficacy but "some still have safety concerns". CITED (PMC11239608; PMC10015931; PubMed 35017680).
- A 2026 J Hypertens commentary is titled "Acute blood pressure responses to isometric exercise: do safety thresholds challenge clinical implementation?" (title only; no abstract). CITED (title). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42333084/
- Safety data from older adults on heart medicines or people with kidney or eye disease: NOT FOUND in the pages opened. Thai home trial (30 older adults, 8 weeks): "No complications were reported" (F14).

### Q6. Home-based and unsupervised trials

- D3-08-F11 Denmark, 20 weeks, home, null/adverse systolic result (above).
- D3-08-F13 India: Punia S, Kulandaivelan S. Physiother Res Int 2020;25:e1806 "partial preliminary findings from RCT": 40 hypertensive adults aged 30 to 45, home-based, 30% of maximum force, 4 x 2 min with 4-min rests, 8 weeks, "double-blind". Reported systolic MD -8.75 mmHg (95% CI -6.51, -10.39), diastolic -8.35, pulse rate -8.90. Preliminary report; intervals look implausibly narrow for n=40; no adherence data in abstract. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31418966/ India flag.
- D3-08-F14 Thailand: Champaiboon J et al, PLoS One 2026;21(3):e0342563. 30 hypertensive adults over 60, home, commercial spring devices at 20 to 50% of maximum, 3 times a week for 8 weeks, control did non-resistive grip. Systolic -7.33 +/- 9.63 mmHg within group; between-group -8.19 mmHg (p=0.03); no complications; satisfaction high. Small trial. CITED (abstract; open access). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41779689/
- D3-08-F15 Healthy volunteers, home, 8 weeks, daily 4 x 2 min at 30%: handgrip lowered office pressure only, not ambulatory pressure (Goessler 2018, n=60, mean age 33). CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29472030/
- Home vs supervised, same trial (Farah 2018, as tabulated in the 2024 review): home n=18, supervised n=14, control n=16, 12 weeks, 3 times weekly; pressure before to after: home 130/73 to 126/71, supervised 132/71 to 120/66 mmHg. CITED (table, PMC11239608).
- Gordon 2018 (home n=5, lab n=8, control n=9, 12 weeks): home 137.7/88.4 to 128/81.6; lab 137.6/87.1 to 128.5/84.3. Very small. CITED (same table).
- Supervision: a 2026 meta-analysis of 10 trials comparing supervised and non-supervised exercise (all modes, includes some handgrip) found supervised exercise gave a larger systolic fall: -3.46 mmHg (95% CI -5.94 to -0.97), I-squared 74%; diastolic not different. Braz J Phys Ther 2026. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42748538/
- Adherence figures (share of prescribed sessions completed) for unsupervised handgrip: NOT FOUND in the abstracts opened. Pagonas 2017 states aerobic exercise worked "even as an uncontrolled and unsupervised exercise regimen" (F10).

### Q7. Measuring grip force and low-cost load-cell accuracy

- Trials used a handheld grip dynamometer to find the maximum and then set 30% of it (F16; research-grade or simple models "as long as the dynamometer can accurately record the force generated and display maximum force", F22 source). Specific trial brands (Jamar, Takei, Camry): NOT FOUND in the pages opened.
- D3-08-F31 Gaudet S, Handrigan G. Sensors 2020 (PMID 32899264): HX711 amplifier with Arduino and load cell against a commercial signal conditioner and a Lafayette handheld dynamometer. Limb maximal isometric force (forearm push against a fixed load cell, not a grip handle): Pearson r 0.947 to 0.992; inter-day ICC 0.9582 (95% CI 0.9297 to 0.9752); intra-day ICC 0.9269 (0.8909 to 0.9533). The paper states calibration "is of critical importance". CITED (PMC full text). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7506672/
- D3-08-F32 Kanauchi Y et al, J Hand Surg Asian Pac Vol 2023;28:562-572. 80 healthy hospital workers; a commercial load-cell hand dynamometer against the Jamar. Load cell ICC above 0.772 on different days (Jamar above 0.830); one-time measure ICC above 0.858 in all groups; load cell gave "significantly lower values in all measurements" despite correlation r above 0.70. Not a home-built device. CITED (abstract). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37881817/
- D3-08-F33 Direct HX711 grip-device against Jamar validation: NOT FOUND (one search; no published study located). Accuracy against known weights for HX711 builds: the Sensors paper does not give a kg error figure in the sections read; NOT FOUND as a stated number.
- Dose-linked fact: the target is a share (30%) of the person's own maximum, held for 2 minutes. A grip force error therefore changes the dose by the same proportion; no study found linking device error to blood-pressure response. NOT FOUND.

### Q8. What can and cannot be shown after one night (facts only)

- Fall in blood pressure from handgrip: shortest trial found 4 weeks (F-Oliveira 2026, supervised, 12 sessions) and trials mostly 8 to 12 weeks; a single session did not lower pressure in people with hypertension (F04). A one-night test cannot show a chronic fall. CITED.
- Things with published reference values a team could compare to: load-cell repeatability (ICC above 0.77 on different days, 0.93 to 0.96 for HX711 limb setup, F31, F32); the need for re-measuring maximum grip each session (F16 source); the 30% target held for 2 minutes with 1-minute rests (F16); the acute pressure rise at 30% (20 to 50 mmHg, F22).
- Home pressure as feedback: in two trials home or ambulatory pressure showed smaller or null effects than clinic pressure (F07, F11, F12).
- Honest evidence tonight would be device-only: error against known weights, time inside a 30% band over 2 minutes, repeatability of maximum-grip readings. No published acceptance thresholds for these were found: NOT FOUND.
