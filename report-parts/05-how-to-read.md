## 2. How to read this report

**What was done.** Three rounds of research on 3 November 2026, ten days before the event. Round 1: ten Sonnet agents swept ten fields (health, elderly care, air/heat/water, energy and waste, manufacturing, mobility, space and defence, fraud and civic services, education and jobs, urban living) for problems Indians complain about in public, and logged 62. Round 2: five Opus agents verified, sized and designed solutions for the 15 that passed triage, one problem set at a time. Round 3: three Sonnet agents re-checked every number and link behind the eight finalists, and two Opus agents wrote the overnight build plans. The ranking, the judgement calls and this write-up are the coordinator's. The agri, produce and cold-chain space was excluded from the start, as were the author's existing ideas.

**Proof tiers.** Tier A: five or more independent public items (different authors, different dates) with real engagement — hundreds of upvotes, lakhs of views, a survey with thousands of respondents — plus at least one hard number from government or a named study. Tier B: two to four such items, or many items without a hard number. Tier C: anecdotal. Only A and B advanced past Round 1; every finalist is Tier A after Round 2.

**Scores.** Each verified problem carries six scores of 1–5 (5 = best), given by the Round 2 agent and checked by the coordinator: proof strength, market size, overnight feasibility, how unsolved the gap is, deep-tech content, and risk (5 = lowest risk). The weighted score is proof 25 % + market 20 % + feasibility 20 % + unsolved gap 15 % + deep-tech 10 % + low-risk 10 %, maximum 5.0. The weights were fixed before the research began. Two finalists (EV charging bay, GNSS integrity) were advanced over higher-scoring near-misses on explicit judgement calls, stated where they occur.

**Market figures.** TAM and SAM are bottom-up: a sourced count of affected units multiplied by a price that is either sourced or clearly marked as an assumption. The arithmetic is shown in each finalist's Round 2 note. Where a published market figure exists it is quoted and, where the agent did not believe it, that is said. Treat every ₹ crore figure as an order of magnitude, not a forecast.

**What "verified" means.** Every number and URL used in a finalist section was re-opened in Round 3 and marked CONFIRMED, CORRECTED, CONTESTED or NOT FOUND; corrections are applied in the text and listed under each finalist. The source register (`SPARK Night 2026 - source register.xlsx`) has one row per cited URL with its verification status.

**What could not be reached.** Reddit (reddit.com and old.reddit.com) was blocked for every tool in this session; upvote counts come from the Arctic Shift archive API (which captures scores near posting time and understates them) and from news coverage of viral posts. Instagram is not fetchable at all and was not used. YouTube view counts were read from watch pages or YouTube's own search pages on 3 November 2026. LocalCircles surveys are opt-in panels, not probability samples, and are reported as such.

**Feasibility.** "Overnight" means 18:00 on 13 November to 12:00 on 14 November with a team of four to six undergraduates (computer science, hardware, bio) and the event's building kits plus pre-ordered parts. Every build plan names a measurable result reachable in that window, and says when the result is on simulated or synthetic data rather than real conditions.
