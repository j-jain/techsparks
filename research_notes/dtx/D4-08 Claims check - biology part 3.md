# D4-08 Claims check - biology part 3 (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-14)

## Summary

Checked 42 fact IDs from D4-02 "How fast a dose change works": every ID in its Digest and product section, plus the other time, interval and effect-size facts. Routes: openFDA label JSON (same set IDs as the note's DailyMed links), PubMed efetch, Europe PMC full-text XML, PMC HTML, guideline PDFs (WHO 2021, ISH 2020 author copy, IHCI Punjab and MP sheets, Swedish SmPC), the ESH 2023 and NICE pages. Verdicts: CONFIRMED 39, CORRECTED 1 (F34), CONTESTED 1 (F44), NOT FOUND 1 (F37). Every label number, steady-state time, guideline quote and trial number in the note matched its source.

Findings that change a step-timing rule:
1. F37 and the product section's 93 to 96% and 58 to 78%. Lasserson's model equation is behind a paywall, so the exponential shape is an unverified assumption. The arithmetic is right on that assumption. With a hyperbolic shape, or with the paper's own titration t50 of 1.2 weeks, the day 24 to 30 window holds about 79 to 92% of the full effect, and the day 8 to 14 window about 48 to 69%. Say "about 80 to 96%" and "about 48 to 78%". The 30-day default still looks sound; a 14-day step looks weaker than the note says.
2. F34 (Ojji 2024, Nigeria). The interval the note could not find is in the PMC full text: a first visit at 2 weeks, stepping up if clinic BP was 135/85 or more, justified by citing Lasserson ("almost all" of the effect by 2 weeks); the standard arm stepped monthly. The faster arm had more early eGFR falls of over 30% at month 1 (11% vs 4%, equal by month 6) and more potassium under 3.5 at month 6 (34% vs 18%). The arms also differed in drugs (indapamide). "No stops for adverse events" is true but incomplete. A fast ARB-plus-diuretic step would need an early creatinine and potassium check.
3. F44. The primary paper is still unopened (403, and a CAPTCHA that was not bypassed). Two news reports differ on the 12-week chlorthalidone ambulatory fall: 12.11 (Healio) against 11.1/7.8 (MDedge). Week 4 holds 56 to 61% of the 12-week effect on either figure, so "no fast track after chlorthalidone" is unchanged.
4. F17 (IHCI). The numbers are right, but control is BP at the latest visit after a median 12 months on treatment, among patients retained in care (36% excluded as lost). 16.5% (Punjab) and 31.5% (Maharashtra) were already under 140/90 at registration. "70 to 76% controlled on amlodipine 5 mg" is not a 30-day response rate.
5. F36. The trials started monotherapy in drug-naive or washed-out patients. None studied a dose increase on a drug already taken, which is what the loop does. For a protocol step, the titration-arm t50 (1.2 weeks) is the closer match.
