# D4-01 Who responds to what (agent: Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-11-14)

## Digest

Twelve facts that matter most for the loop's biology:

1. Step 4 buys almost nothing: in patients uncontrolled on amlodipine 10, telmisartan 40 added 3.7 and 80 added 3.9 mm Hg systolic (D4-01-F50); 1.7 in the factorial (F49). ARBs reach 80% of maximum at half the maximum dose (F52).
2. Step 2 adds about 5 mm Hg (F49) but oedema goes from 3.0% to 10.8%, 14.6% in women (F19). Adding telmisartan to amlodipine 5 lowered BP more than amlodipine 10, with oedema 4.3% vs 27.2% (F51).
3. Adding a class roughly doubles the fall; doubling a dose gives one fifth of that (F09), about 1.5 mm Hg (F10).
4. A thiazide on top of amlodipine + ARB adds about 6 mm Hg (F53). Chlorthalidone is near its ceiling at 12.5 mg (F26).
5. Each person's best single drug beats a fixed choice by 4.4 mm Hg (F01); rotation raised monotherapy control from 39% to 73% (F04).
6. People differ between a renin-angiotensin blocker and amlodipine/thiazide, not within those pairs (F02, F04, F32).
7. High renin favours ARB/ACE inhibitor, low renin favours thiazide, but age-race predicted better; calcium blockers work at any renin (F06, F14).
8. High salt blunts ACE inhibitors more than calcium blockers (F13); 48% of hypertensives in a north-east India study were salt-sensitive (F47).
9. South Asians: no class differences in 16 mostly Indian trials (F11); larger response to an ACE inhibitor added to amlodipine in ASCOT (F07). Black Africans: the pair should contain amlodipine (F12).
10. Women and over-65s respond more to amlodipine (F39); sex does not change benefit per mm Hg (F38).
11. Pharmacogenomics is not usable: best variants about 3 mm Hg, polygenic scores null (F33 to F36).
12. IHCI: amlodipine 5 controlled 70 to 76% of returning patients (clinic BP, survivor bias); the next step added about 5 points (F45).

Surprises: telmisartan 80 adds about nothing over 40 in amlodipine non-responders (F50); PHYSIC says normal BP variation exceeds a titration step's effect (F03); NICE has no South Asian rule (F15).

Starting leads: lead 1 is qualified. Dose increases are the weakest step (F09, F10, F50); adding or switching class is where the mm Hg are.

## What this suggests for the product

- Suggestion: model each protocol step's expected fall (about 5, 4, 2 and 6 mm Hg for steps 2 to 5; F49, F50, F53). Then a "still high" verdict can be compared with what the step could deliver, not only with 135/85.
- Suggestion: flag step 4 (telmisartan 40 to 80) to the approving doctor as low-yield and lab-heavy, citing F50 and F21. Do not change the state protocol in software.
- Suggestion: log ankle swelling at step 2, especially in women (F19, F39, F51). It is the main cost of that step and a likely cause of quiet non-adherence.
- Suggestion: record salt habit and age/sex as fields from day one (F13, F39, F47). They are the cheap predictors that exist, and the loop's outcome data could later test them in Indians, where no rotation trial exists (NOT FOUND).
- Suggestion: if a patient fails a step with good adherence, offer the doctor the class-switch question (renin-angiotensin blocker vs amlodipine/thiazide). That is where between-person differences live (F02, F04).
- Suggestion: do not build pharmacogenomic or renin testing into the product (F06, F33 to F36).
- Suggestion: because a step changes BP by only 2 to 6 mm Hg, the home-reading rule must be able to detect a change that small. Hand this to D4-03 (F03, F49).

## Findings

### Q2. Crossover and rotation trials: how much better is each patient's best drug

- D4-01-F01 | PHYSIC trial: gain from personalised drug choice | Randomised double-blind repeated crossover, Sweden, single centre, 280 randomised (270 analysed, 1,468 treatment periods, 54% men, mean age 64, grade 1 hypertension, low risk). Each person took lisinopril 20 mg, candesartan 16 mg, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg and amlodipine 10 mg in random order, two classes repeated; outcome daytime ambulatory systolic BP. Personalised single-drug choice gave on average 4.4 mm Hg lower systolic BP than a fixed choice; against lisinopril (best on average at these doses) the gain was 3.1 mm Hg. Year 2023. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091169/ | source dated 2023-04-11 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F02 | PHYSIC: which pairs differ between people | Response varied between individuals (P < .001) for lisinopril vs hydrochlorothiazide, lisinopril vs amlodipine, candesartan vs hydrochlorothiazide and candesartan vs amlodipine. Large differences were excluded for lisinopril vs candesartan (both block the renin-angiotensin system) and for hydrochlorothiazide vs amlodipine. Practical reading: the useful switch is between a renin-angiotensin blocker and an amlodipine/thiazide-type drug, not within a pair. Year 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37039792/ | source dated 2023-04-11 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F03 | PHYSIC: size of the gain against dose doubling | Authors' discussion: the extra lowering from personalisation is "of a magnitude twice that achieved by doubling the dose of a first BP-lowering drug, and more than half that of adding a second drug". They also state that normal within-person BP variation is "generally much larger" than the differences achieved by titrating a drug. Each treatment period: 1-week placebo washout, 2-week dose increase, at least 4 weeks at target dose (median period 56 days). Year 2023. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091169/ | source dated 2023-04-11 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F04 | Cambridge AB/CD rotation (Dickerson) | 56 untreated young UK hypertensives (mean 161/98 mm Hg) rotated monthly through an ACE inhibitor (A), beta-blocker (B), calcium blocker (C) and diuretic (D); 36 completed all four. Monotherapy success (at or below 140/90) rose from 22/56 (39%) on the first drug to 41/56 (73%) on each person's best drug (p=0.0001). Responses to A and B were correlated (r=0.5) and to C and D (r=0.6), not across pairs. In these young patients AB responses were on average at least 50% larger than CD. Mechanism proposed: underlying types of essential hypertension (renin-dependent versus volume/salt-dependent). Year 1999. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10376615/ | source dated 1999-06-12 | OPENED | CITED

Success: diltiazem (a calcium blocker) 59%, atenolol 51%, clonidine 50%, hydrochlorothiazide 46%, captopril 42%, prazosin 42%, placebo 25%. Best drug by subgroup: diltiazem for younger and older Black men (64%), captopril for younger white men (55%), atenolol for older white men (68%). Men only; no South Asians. - D4-01-F06 | Renin profile vs age-race as predictor (VA trial re-analysis) | 1,031 men with renin profiling. ### Q5. Dose-response: doubling a dose against adding a class

- D4-01-F08 | Law and Wald 354-trial meta-analysis: half dose | 354 double-blind placebo-controlled fixed-dose trials (40,000 treated). Average placebo-adjusted reduction 9.1/5.5 mm Hg at standard dose and 7.1/4.4 mm Hg at half standard dose (20% less). Effects of different classes were additive. For each 10 mm Hg higher pretreatment BP the fall was 1.0 mm Hg systolic greater. Side effects of thiazides, beta-blockers and calcium blockers were strongly dose related; ARBs caused no excess symptoms; metabolic effects (potassium, cholesterol) negligible at half dose. Year 2003. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12829555/ | source dated 2003-06-28 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F09 | Wald 2009: combining vs doubling | Meta-analysis of 42 factorial trials (10,968 people). Adding a second class roughly doubled the fall: thiazide 7.3 alone vs 14.6 combined; calcium blocker 8.4 vs 14.3; ACE inhibitor 6.8 vs 13.9 mm Hg systolic. Observed/expected ratio for additivity 1.01 (CI 0.90 to 1.12). Doubling the dose of one drug gave about one fifth of the extra effect of adding a second class (0.22, CI 0.19 to 0.25). Year 2009. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19272490/ | source dated 2009-03 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F10 | Wang 2025 Lancet: 484-trial dose-response | 484 double-blind placebo-controlled trials, 104,176 people, mean baseline 154/100, mean follow-up 8.6 weeks. Monotherapy at standard dose lowered systolic BP 8.7 mm Hg (CI 8.2 to 9.2); each dose doubling added only 1.5 mm Hg (1.2 to 1.7). A dual combination at standard doses lowered 14.9 mm Hg (13.1 to 16.8); doubling both added 2.5 mm Hg. Each 10 mm Hg lower baseline cut monotherapy efficacy by 1.3 mm Hg. 45 of 57 standard-dose monotherapies (79%) were "low intensity" (under 10 mm Hg). Dose-response differed between and within classes (drug-level tables not opened). Year 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40885583/ | source dated 2025-09 | OPENED | CITED

- D4-01-F11 | South Asian response: systematic review (mostly Indian trials) | Brewster 2016 review (Cochrane method, searched to Nov 2015, including IndMED): 16 randomised trials with BP outcomes in 1,719 South Asian patients, median 8 weeks, mostly conducted in India, mostly drug-vs-drug (very few placebo trials), low quality (Jadad scores mostly 1 to 3). Includes Indian trials of telmisartan 40 vs enalapril, losartan 50 vs amlodipine 5 (n=163), telmisartan 80 vs amlodipine 10 (n=62). Finding: "no significant difference in blood pressure lowering effect of different drug types". By contrast, in 35 trials with 25,540 African-ancestry patients, calcium blockers and diuretics lowered BP more than ACE inhibitors and beta-blockers. Authors: little is known about the physiology of hypertension in South Asians. Year 2016. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4820501/ | source dated 2016-03 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F12 | CREOLE trial: Black Africans (Nigeria-relevant) | Randomised single-blind, six sub-Saharan countries, 728 Black patients (mean age 51, 63% women). Amlodipine 5 + hydrochlorothiazide 12.5, amlodipine 5 + perindopril 4, or perindopril 4 + hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 for 2 months, then doses doubled for 4 months. 24-hour ambulatory systolic fell 3.14 mm Hg (CI -5.90 to -0.38) more with amlodipine + thiazide and 3.00 mm Hg (-5.8 to -0.20) more with amlodipine + perindopril than with perindopril + thiazide; the two amlodipine combinations were equal (difference -0.14). Reading: in Black Africans the combination should contain amlodipine. Year 2019. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30883050/ | source dated 2019-04-18 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F13 | Salt intake changes which drug works (salt-sensitive patients) | Randomised double-blind trial, US, 391 salt-sensitive hypertensives (96 Black, 63 Hispanic, 232 white), enalapril (ACE inhibitor) vs isradipine (calcium blocker) under alternating high salt (about 315 mmol/day sodium) and low salt (about 90 mmol/day). The calcium blocker lost less effect on high salt: high-minus-low salt BP difference with isradipine was -3.6/-1.6 (Black), -6.2/-3.9 (white), -8.1/-5.3 (Hispanic) vs enalapril -9.0/-5.3, -11.8/-7.0, -11.1/-5.6. On high salt, Black patients were better controlled on isradipine; on low salt both drugs were similar in all groups. Mechanism: high salt suppresses renin, so a renin-angiotensin blocker has less to block; calcium blockers act directly on arteriolar muscle. Authors: controlling for salt sensitivity "diminishes race-related differences". Year 1998. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9576119/ | source dated 1998-05 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-01-F14 | Renin predicts thiazide response, using home BP (PEAR) | 363 US patients aged 65 or under (152 Black, 211 white), atenolol then add hydrochlorothiazide or the reverse; responses measured as home BP averages. Higher plasma renin activity predicted greater response to atenolol and lesser response to hydrochlorothiazide, as monotherapy and as add-on, independent of race and age; pretreatment BP level also predicted response. Effect sizes per unit renin not in abstract. Year 2010. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20725057/ | source dated 2010 | OPENED | CITED

### Q4. Pharmacogenomics: what is known, effect sizes, use in practice
