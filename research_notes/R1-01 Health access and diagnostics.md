## Problem 1: Counterfeit and substandard medicines (verification at the chemist / hospital pharmacy)
- Track: Bio & Health (verification device is also AI & Systems)
- Who suffers: Patients buying from local chemists (LocalCircles: 86% buy mainly from local chemists), cancer patients on high-value injectables, hospitals buying through discount middlemen. Continuous, all states (survey names Karnataka, Haryana, UP as most documented; Bihar, West Bengal, Telangana next).
- How they cope today: Trust the neighbourhood chemist; check batch number and print quality by eye; scan the QR code on top-300 brands (1mg guide says government mandated QR on the top 300 brands, undated snippet); report to CDSCO. A DesiDime commenter's complaint: there is no way for a consumer to test the ingredients, and most strips carry no usable code (desidime thread and technospot comments, undated snippets). No price paid for verification; the cost is borne as risk.
- Public proof (tier A, with the caveat above):
  - https://theprint.in/feature/64-of-indians-bought-medicine-without-a-prescription-finds-survey/3045890/ — 17 Sep 2026 — LocalCircles, 1,06,000 responses, 327 districts — 15% say they were sold counterfeit medicines in the last 12 months; 64% say chemist never/rarely asked for a prescription; only 31% can regularly get help from a qualified pharmacist; 15% got no bill.
  - https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/industry/pharma/darzalex-keytruda-imfinzi-among-588-cancer-drug-vials-seized-in-rs-9-crore-racket-17-arrested-177854 — 24 Aug 2026 — national trade news, engagement n/a — Delhi Crime Branch: 17 arrested, about Rs 9 crore, 588 filled anti-cancer vials; batch numbers and MRP removed with acetone and drugs repackaged in original cartons; sources were counterfeit, hospital-diverted and govt-supply-diverted stock. (A search summary of the Medbound Times coverage said about 100 cancer patients were traced as recipients; I did not open that page: snippet.)
  - https://www.medboundtimes.com/healthcare-corruption/fake-cancer-drugs-90-bengaluru-hospitals-pharmacy-owner-arrested — 14 Sep 2026 — trade news, engagement n/a — Bengaluru SIT: counterfeit drugs allegedly supplied to 90+ hospitals and clinics at ~50% discount; Rs 5 crore seized at a farmhouse on 18 Aug; pharmacy owner arrested, alleged mastermind absconding.
  - https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/industry/pharma/delhi-police-busts-rs-10-crore-syndicate-supplying-fake-cancer-liver-disease-drugs-4-held-170252 — May 2026 (date from search summary, snippet) — Delhi Police: Rs 10 crore syndicate, fake cancer and liver-disease drugs, 4 held. Same search summary lists earlier busts of Rs 8 crore (2022) and Rs 4 crore (2024): repeat pattern.
  - https://theprint.in/india/antifungals-sold-as-chemo-top-hospitals-staff-involved-delhi-fake-cancer-drugs-case-chargesheet/2182796 — undated (snippet, title only) — chargesheet in a Delhi fake-cancer-drug case: antifungals sold as chemo, staff of top hospitals involved.
  - https://www.desidime.com/discussions/how-to-identify-fake-medicines-in-india — undated, consumer forum thread (snippet) — consumers asking how to identify fake medicines; no engagement figure retrievable.
- Hard number: CDSCO flagged 330 samples Not of Standard Quality plus 1 spurious (Delhi) for Sept 2026 (42 from central labs, 288 from state labs); Aug 2026: 220 NSQ + 1 spurious — https://newsable.asianetnews.com/india/cdsco-reports-330-drugs-as-not-of-standard-quality-in-september-2026-articleshow-z9g3ag1 (23 Oct 2026). Spurious samples found by CDSCO/state controllers: 282 in FY2023-24 and 245 in FY2024-25 (as quoted in the ThePrint 17 Sep 2026 article above). Caveat: NSQ results are batch-specific and mostly quality failures (assay, dissolution), not counterfeits; "spurious" is the counterfeit category and is far smaller (one a month in Aug/Sept 2026).
- Who has tried: Government track-and-trace and QR mandate for top-300 brands (1mg guide, undated snippet); DAVA portal mentioned in technospot (undated snippet). Drug Controller General announced a "new code system" (Moneylife, old, snippet). NOT SEARCHED: private authentication startups and why they stalled.
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): A pharmacy-side "is this strip/vial genuine" checker: cheap multi-channel spectral sensor (e.g. AS7265x/NIR + ESP32) fingerprinting tablets against reference spectra, plus phone macro-photo ML on blister print, cap crimp and batch-code erasure marks (the Delhi gang removed batch codes with acetone).
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 4. Spectral sensor + small tablet library + classifier is a one-night bench demo; real-world generalisation and injectables are hard, so the demo should be tablets only.
- Rough market pointer: Buyers are chemists, hospital pharmacies and oncology procurement. Chemist-shop count NOT SEARCHED, so no unit x price figure. Bust sizes (Rs 4 to 10 crore per ring, repeated) show the value at risk in oncology alone.
- Risks / regulation: Device claims touch CDSCO; false negatives carry liability; spectral checks catch wrong or absent API but not dissolution failures (which make up most NSQ).

## Problem 2: Snakebite: no on-the-spot diagnosis, antivenom missing where the bite happens
- Track: Bio & Health
- Who suffers: Rural India, about 58,000 deaths/year (eLife 2020 national mortality study); 94% of deaths rural, 77% outside hospital, 8 states (Bihar, Jharkhand, MP, Odisha, UP, AP incl. Telangana, Rajasthan, Gujarat) about 70% of deaths, children under 15 about 28% of deaths.
- How they cope today: Local healer / vaidya / ojha or travel to district hospital; private hospital bills (ThePrint opinion: one family carried Rs 16 lakh debt); doctors have "no standard test to differentiate venomous from non-venomous bites on the spot" so they watch symptoms (ETV Bharat). Drishti Hindi (snippet) relays a Sirsa, Haryana case: a 27-year-old woman refused at a govt hospital and a charity hospital; family was taking her to a village vaidya when she died; civil surgeon said referral was for lack of a specialist, not lack of antivenom (accounts differ).
- Public proof (tier A, news/court-based):
  - https://elifesciences.org/articles/54076 — 7 Jul 2020 — peer-reviewed, Registrar General of India Million Death Study — about 1.2 million snakebite deaths 2000-2019, about 58,000/year.
  - https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/supreme-court-issues-notice-to-centre-on-plea-seeking-better-snakebite-treatment-and-anti-venom-access-1561103 — 14 Dec 2024 (order dated Friday 13 Dec, inferred) — Supreme Court (Justices Gavai and Viswanathan) issued notice on PIL Shailendra Mani Tripathi v. Union of India (Diary 48030/2024) citing about 58,000 deaths/year and seeking antivenom at PHCs, CHCs, district hospitals and snakebite care units.
  - https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/09/30/bes31-mh-ld-snake-bite-girl.html — 30 Sep 2025 (PTI) — Dombivli, Thane: 4-year-old girl and her 24-year-old aunt died of krait bite; family alleged the hospital had no anti-snake-venom and waited nearly an hour for a doctor/ambulance.
  - https://theprint.in/opinion/snakebites-arent-just-lethal-in-rural-india-it-means-debts-of-lakhs-to-victims-families/1041284 — 16 Jul 2022 — opinion by RESQ Charitable Trust founder — many rural hospitals lack reliable antivenom and cold storage; most doctors untrained in dosing; snakebite not a notifiable disease; Rs 16 lakh family debt.
  - https://www.downtoearth.org.in/wildlife-biodiversity/serpentine-problem-58396 — 4 Aug 2017 — Lok Sabha figures of 1,123 deaths (2013) and 1,008 (2014) vs a 2011 PLOS NTD estimate of 45,900-50,900/year; most PHCs lack cold storage for antivenom; only 4 species' venom used (Big Four, said to cause 98% of deaths).
  - https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/boy-dies-of-snakebite-as-3-hospitals-fail-to-treat-him-773713.html — undated (page returned 403; snippet) — boy died after three hospitals failed to treat him; antivenom stock reported unavailable.
  - https://www.millenniumpost.in/kolkata/deaths-due-to-snake-bites-up-in-rural-bengal-due-to-non-availability-of-drugs-255338 — 2017 per Drishti summary (snippet) — rural Bengal PHCs frequently out of antivenom, patients sent to district hospitals.
- Hard number: about 58,000 deaths/year, 77% outside hospital (eLife, 7 Jul 2020). Contradiction worth using: official figures given to Lok Sabha were 1,123 (2013) and 1,008 (2014), roughly 50x lower than study estimates (DTE, 4 Aug 2017), so the real burden is invisible to the system.
- Who has tried (status unverified unless stated):
  - Bhat Biotech, Bengaluru: 2-minute Snake Venom Rapid Test; claimed 100% accuracy in 300 patients (founder interview, not peer-reviewed); "licensed", market launch "within about two months" as of 25 Nov 2025; pitch is ASHA workers, ambulances, PHCs — https://www.etvbharat.com/en/offbeat/karnataka-researchers-develop-2-minute-snake-venom-test-kit-set-to-hit-market-soon-enn25112506045 (25 Nov 2025).
  - Indriyam Biologics, Kerala: monovalent venom-detection biosensor and lateral-flow kits, DSIR/Startup India recognised, round size undisclosed (search summary, snippet).
  - RGCB Thiruvananthapuram, DST-funded lateral-flow device for species identification, budget INR 86,31,800 (indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in, snippet).
  - ProteInno mAb Tech (NE India, nanobody kit), Shaktinetra Labs "Go Venom" wearable (press-release style, Hitavada 1 Sep 2025), Kerala/Amrita smartphone colourimetric test (Deccan Herald, research stage), T-hisss snakebite app (snippets).
  - One editorial (snippet) says no commercial venom-detection kit exists in India; the Bhat Biotech launch would change that if it happened. Foreign comparator: VenomAid (Copenhagen) seed $1.8M, not India.
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): A PHC/ASHA triage tool that tells "envenomed? which syndrome? give ASV / refer / observe": optical clot-time sensor (LED + photodiode + ESP32 on a whole-blood tube) for the haemotoxic bites, plus phone bite-site photo/symptom model and a nearest-antivenom-stock lookup; venom-specific lateral flow is the stretch goal for the bio team. (Clotting-time bedside test is my prior knowledge, not sourced in this session.)
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 3. Clot-timer + triage logic is buildable; validation on real envenomed blood is impossible overnight (use spiked/anticoagulated samples and be honest).
- Rough market pointer: Government procurement (NHM) for PHC/CHC units; PHC/CHC counts NOT SEARCHED. Burden pointer: 300,000-400,000 envenoming-related injuries/year is one researcher's sketchy estimate (DTE 2017).
- Risks / regulation: IVD licensing and ICMR/CDSCO validation; a false negative is fatal; buyer is state government.

## Problem 3: Pathology lab report errors in an unregulated lab market
- Track: Bio & Health
- Who suffers: Anyone tested at one of the roughly 100,000 labs (2014 estimate); small-town and neighbourhood labs especially; wrong creatinine/Hb/platelet values drive wrong treatment.
- How they cope today: Repeat the test at a second lab, usually after a doctor's doubt; ask a doctor to correlate with symptoms (Apollo 247 answers, snippet); consumer-commission cases. Cost: duplicate test fees; in the one documented case, a patient was nearly sent to dialysis.
- Public proof (tier B: strong surveys, but the hard number is dated):
  - https://localcircles.com/a/press/page/survey-corruption-in-pathology-labs — 22 Nov 2017 — LocalCircles, 43,000+ responses, 29,000+ citizens — 26% got wrong reports up to 3 times in 3 years, 6% more than 3 times, 25% unsure; 9% very low trust, 34% low trust; 47% say most doctors send them to a particular lab.
  - https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2020/01/04/study-claims-widespread-malpractice-in-path-labs.html — 4 Jan 2020 — LocalCircles, 48,000+ responses, 22,000+ citizens, 215 districts — 30% say a report in the last 3 years was later proven wrong, 32% unsure; 92% want labs that incentivise doctors to lose certification.
  - https://imlindia.com/blog/when-the-test-report-went-rogue-and-nearly-sent-a-healthy-patient-to-dialysis/ — 13 Aug 2025 (archive date) — law-blog write-up of a consumer-commission case: urea and creatinine reported far out of range, doctor advised dialysis, hospital and 3 other labs found normal, commission awarded compensation.
  - https://www.delhimedicalcouncil.org/images/Order1385.doc — undated (snippet) — Delhi Medical Council order finding a lab report erroneous after a second lab's test.
  - https://www.medboundtimes.com/medicine/different-lab-results-same-blood-test — undated (snippet) — explainer: labs inherit reference ranges from kit makers calibrated on other populations; patients confused by differing results.
  - https://eastmojo.com/assam/2021/06/25/assam-tinsukia-pathology-lab-under-lens-over-incorrect-report-of-covid-19-patient/ — 25 Jun 2021 — Tinsukia, Assam lab probed over an incorrect COVID report.
- Hard number: Two LocalCircles surveys, 2017 (32% wrong at least once in 3 years) and 2020 (30% "proven wrong"); both self-reported, not audited error rates. Industry/regulator-side: about 1,000 accredited labs of about 100,000 (about 1%) per Business Standard, Apr 2014, via https://www.darkdaily.com/2014/05/30/pathologists-and-medical-laboratory-scientists-in-india-call-for-active-government-regulation-in-response-to-ongoing-problems-with-quality-of-medical-laboratory-tests-530/ (30 May 2014, snippet; Business Standard page returned 403). No current govt number found.
- Who has tried: Thyrocare says all its labs are NABL-accredited (company press release on ThePrint, undated, company-reported); NABL's voluntary QAS scheme with unannounced assessments (snippet). Diagnostics startups on AI smear analysis NOT SEARCHED.
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): A low-cost independent "second reading" device: ESP32 + colour/absorbance sensor reading a few analytes (Hb, glucose, creatinine) from a drop on a calibrated card, paired with a delta/plausibility checker on uploaded reports (e.g. urea-creatinine pairs, impossible combinations).
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 3. Plausibility checker is software and fast; a calibrated colourimeter is feasible for one analyte (Hb or glucose) with the bio team's reagents; traceable accuracy is not.
- Rough market pointer: about 100,000 labs (2014) as potential QA buyers; Indian diagnostics industry quoted as "$5 billion a year" in the same 2014 piece (dated, snippet). No current figure searched.
- Risks / regulation: Labs have no incentive to buy a tool that exposes errors, so the buyer is the patient, insurer or chain; calibration traceability; liability if the checker is wrong.

## Problem 4: Antibiotics from the chemist counter and fever tests that cannot tell bacterial from viral (Widal, typhoid)
- Track: Bio & Health
- Who suffers: Anyone with fever or a cough in India; the cost shows up as AMR, wasted spend and wrong treatment.
- How they cope today: Ask the chemist; get a Widal test then antibiotics. Patients in several states reportedly pay Rs 500 to Rs 4,000 per dose of antibiotic injection after a single Widal (The Hindu, via search summary, snippet).
- Public proof (tier B: studies and one big survey, no consumer-complaint threads retrievable):
  - https://theprint.in/feature/64-of-indians-bought-medicine-without-a-prescription-finds-survey/3045890/ — 17 Sep 2026 — LocalCircles, 1,06,000 responses, 327 districts — 64% say the chemist never or rarely asked for a prescription (34% never, 30% very rarely); only 15% say chemists mostly or always insisted. (The article does not break out antibiotics.)
  - https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/widal-test-in-diagnosing-typhoid-in-india — 9 May 2024 (summarising The Hindu) — Widal widely used in India despite limited accuracy; false positives and negatives from prior antibiotics and cross-reactivity; linked to overuse of antibiotics and AMR.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4676185 — 2015 (Antimicrob Resist Infect Control) — antimicrobials obtained without a valid prescription in 66.7% (174/261) of Bangalore pharmacies visited (snippet).
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10644113 — undated (snippet) — South-central India pharmacist survey: 78% (303) dispense antibiotics over the counter.
  - https://theprint.in/health/up-to-10-4-lakh-deaths-in-india-in-2019-due-to-antibiotic-resistant-superbugs-says-lancet-report/2270351 — Lancet GRAM published Jan 2022 (China Daily mirror dated 21 Jan 2022; ThePrint date not seen) — snippet.
- Hard number: IHME/GRAM for India 2019: 2,14,461 deaths attributable to and 6,86,908 associated with drug-resistant bacteria; overall estimate 3 to 10.4 lakh depending on method (ThePrint, snippet). Also LocalCircles 64% above.
- Who has tried: NOT SEARCHED.
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): Smartphone-camera reader for a host-response lateral-flow strip (CRP-type bacterial-vs-viral cue) or a typhoid antigen strip, with an ML calibration curve and a "do not prescribe antibiotic" decision aid at the chemist/PHC.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 2. Needs bought strips and reference samples; a calibrated strip reader with a decision rule is the only part buildable overnight.
- Rough market pointer: LocalCircles says 86% of consumers buy mainly from local chemists; chemist count and strip price NOT SEARCHED.
- Risks / regulation: IVD licensing; chemist revenue conflict; Schedule H1 enforcement is the policy lever, not devices; wedge is not unique (CRP strips exist).

## Problem 5: Anaemia is everywhere, but nobody can measure it reliably in the field
- Track: Bio & Health
- Who suffers: 57% of women 15-49, 52.2% of pregnant women, 67.1% of children 6-59 months (NFHS-5, quoted by Outlook India, undated snippet); field screening done by ASHA/ANM with finger-prick devices.
- How they cope today: NFHS-5 itself used capillary samples on a portable HemoCue-type analyzer; critics say capillary testing skews results; one preprint modelling NFHS-5 found device/team bias ranging from -0.87 to +0.62 g/dL between settings (snippet).
- Public proof (tier B: official data plus public policy controversy; no citizen-complaint threads retrievable):
  - https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/fit/citizen-fit/anaemia-nfhs-6-questions-dropped-a-mistake — 29 May 2023 — Quint opinion — NFHS-6 will not measure anaemia, moving it to the smaller DABS-I venous-blood survey because capillary blood can over- or under-state Hb; loses district-level data.
  - https://www.outlookindia.com/national/why-more-than-half-the-population-of-india-remains-anaemic-despite-years-of-intervention — undated (snippet) — NFHS-5 anaemia prevalence figures above.
  - https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10556249/v1 — undated preprint (snippet) — bias in NFHS-5 Hb readings across teams/devices.
  - https://optimizeias.com/anaemia-prevalence-in-eight-states-found-to-be-overstated — undated (title only, snippet) — claim that prevalence in eight states is overstated.
  - https://info.eka.care/eka-news/anemiaphone-set-to-revolutionize-anaemia-diagnosis-in-india — undated (snippet) — AnemiaPhone (Cornell) transferred to ICMR for Anaemia Mukt Bharat, aimed at ASHA/anganwadi workers.
- Hard number: 57% of women of reproductive age anaemic (NFHS-5, via Outlook and Quint).
- Who has tried: Biosense ToucHb non-invasive device, 2011-12 coverage, no later deployment found (imedicalapps, snippet); AnemiaPhone, finger-stick strip + reader, handed to ICMR (snippet); NiADA by Monere AI, Kolkata, AI Hb from lower-eyelid images, 2024 Cureus study "satisfactory" (snippet); Gentrac Labs HbPRISM, smartphone eye-image Hb, still in clinical development (snippet). Funding not found. Crowded.
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): Phone-camera Hb estimate from conjunctiva/nail-bed with a colour-reference card for lighting and skin-tone correction, trained on a locally collected set with HemoCue ground truth from the bio team.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 3. Small dataset of volunteers is possible overnight; error bars will be wide and must be reported honestly.
- Rough market pointer: Anaemia Mukt Bharat covers school adolescents and pregnant women with point-of-care testing (snippet); screening targets and device price NOT SEARCHED.
- Risks / regulation: Accuracy within about 1 g/dL needed to be useful; skin-tone and lighting bias; SaMD/IVD regulation; at least four prior attempts.

## Problem 6: Ambulance does not come, or comes late (108 services)
- Track: AI & Systems (IoT/telemetry; weak deep-tech, logistics-heavy)
- Who suffers: Rural and small-town emergencies, obstetric and paediatric referrals, repeated in news across MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Karnataka, Maharashtra.
- How they cope today: Private vehicles, autos, bikes; families paying for private ambulances; protests with the body at the hospital.
- Public proof (tier A on item count and a CAG number, but all case items are family allegations and the audit is one state):
  - https://citizenmatters.in/when-an-ambulance-driver-stopped-for-dinner-and-never-reached-the-patient-who-died-later/ — 18 Mar 2022 — Karnataka CAG audit (period 2014-15 to 2018-19, tabled 12 Sep 2020): response above the 10-minute mandate in more than 60% of cases; 50% of trauma patients reached hospital after the golden hour; GIS analysis of 38,737 cases found dispatch ("chute") time above 10 minutes in 60%; one crew stopped for dinner, 80-minute wait, patient died.
  - https://medicaldialogues.in/state-news/chattisgarh/patient-dies-during-transfer-family-alleges-ambulance-delay-at-chhattisgarh-hospital-172661 — 12 Jun 2026 — Manendragarh, Chhattisgarh: 60-year-old died in transit after ambulance requested at about 6 PM arrived about midnight.
  - https://medicaldialogues.in/state-news/madhya-pradesh/alleged-three-hours-ambulance-delay-costs-6-month-old-his-life-inquiry-ordered-110780 — 1 May 2023 — Datia, MP: 108 took about 3 hours, infant died; MLA's ambulance was available but had no fuel.
  - https://thelogicalindian.com/died-at-the-doorstep-of-care-how-a-jammed-ambulance-exposed-madhya-pradeshs-emergency-healthcare-crisis/ — Jan 2026 per search summary (snippet) — Satna, MP: 67-year-old died after ambulance rear door jammed at hospital gate.
  - https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/07/04/ces23-od-ambulance-death.html — 4 Jul 2025 (snippet) — Odisha: patient died after ambulance broke down on the way to hospital.
  - https://www.punekarnews.in/?p=60327 and https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/mumbai-tragedy-elderly-man-suffers-heart-attack-in-andheri-dies-before-reaching-hospital-due-to-unavailability-of-ambulance — both undated (snippets) — Pune and Mumbai deaths blamed on ambulance unavailability.
- Hard number: Karnataka CAG: more than 60% of emergencies above the 10-minute response mandate (Citizen Matters, 18 Mar 2022). National response-time statistic NOT FOUND. Fleet as of Sep 2022: 2,423 ALS + 17,135 BLS ambulances, told to Lok Sabha (from a search summary; I could not confirm which page carried the sentence, probably https://www.deccanherald.com/india/too-little-too-late-emergency-services-require-urgent-care-1216452.html: snippet).
- Who has tried: NOT SEARCHED (state 108 operators and startups).
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): Ambulance health and availability telemetry: GNSS + fuel/battery/door/OBD sensors on ESP32 with a dashboard that computes dispatch ("chute") time and flags a stalled or unfuelled vehicle, which the Datia, Satna and Odisha cases show is a real failure mode.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 4 for hardware and dashboard; 1 for proving outcome impact.
- Rough market pointer: Fleet of about 19,500 ALS+BLS ambulances (2,423 + 17,135, Sep 2022) x IoT unit plus SaaS fee is my arithmetic, not a sourced market figure; buyer is state governments and 108 operators.
- Risks / regulation: State tenders, long sales cycles; incumbents already run GPS dispatch software, and the CAG found the existing software logged chute time as a flat one minute, so the gap is data integrity, not absence of GPS.
