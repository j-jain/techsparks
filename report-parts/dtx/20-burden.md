## C. Where the burden is

### C1. What this part shows

Hundreds of millions of Indians have high blood pressure or diabetes, many are on medicine, and few are controlled; in the one cohort we have, half did not come back. Specialists are scarce, visits lasted about two minutes in 2013-2015 and families pay a large share of the bill, while phones and public programmes now reach most of the country.

Why they differ, as the notes give it:
- **Blood pressure, 35.5% against 22.6%.** Different ages (20+ against 15+), years and use of the BP threshold [D1-03-F01, D1-03-F04]. LASI counts only people 45+ [D1-03-F25].

- **NFHS-6.** Random glucose is a screening test, and the findings are provisional [D1-03-F22].

OUR VIEW. So what for us: on any count, blood pressure is the largest pool.

### C3. The care cascade

![Share of adults with high blood pressure who know, are treated and are controlled, 2018-19 and 2023-24](adaptive-care-pictures/22-india-cascade.png)

*Chart: in the first nine districts of India's hypertension programme, 21.2% of adults with high blood pressure were under control in 2023-24, up from 14.5% in 2018-19 [D1-01-F10, D1-01-F11].*

A care cascade follows people with a condition through four steps: has it, knows it, is treated, is controlled. Percentages compare only when the denominator (the group the percentage is taken of) is the same, and several sources do not state it clearly.

#### High blood pressure

| Source | Aware or diagnosed | Treated | Controlled | Denominator | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NFHS-5 district paper, 2019-21 [D1-03-F05] | 46.0% (district average; range 6.1-89.6%) | 18.7% | Not used: the abstract gives 32.9% "of individuals with hypertension", which cannot sit above 18.7% treated; the preprint below gives 9.1% of all for the same 2019-21 survey [D1-03-F07] | Contested; do not read this row as one chain | Abstract opened |
| NFHS-5 preprint, 1.69 million adults, 2019-21 [D1-03-F07] | 36.7% (about 1 in 3) | 17.7% (under 1 in 5) | 9.1% (about 1 in 11) | All people with hypertension | Preprint; record opened |
| NFHS-5 and NFHS-4, ages 15-49 (Cureus 2023) [D1-03-F06] | | | 73.7% (80.8% in NFHS-4) | Only people on BP drugs, ages 15-49 | Abstract opened |
| LASI, adults 45+, 2017-19 [D1-03-F25] | 55.7% | 38.9% | 31.7% | All hypertensives for the first two; **control denominator not stated, check before use** | Abstract opened |

The same survey gives 73.7% controlled when only people aged 15-49 on drugs are counted, and 9.1% (about 1 in 11) when everyone with hypertension is counted; the ages and denominators differ, so the two do not compare [D1-03-F06, D1-03-F07]. A LASI preprint found 63.8% of measured hypertension undiagnosed, about 46 million older Indians (search summary only) [D1-03-F27].

#### Diabetes

- **Undiagnosed:** 43%, about 38.6 million, in 2024 [D1-03-F28].
- **Treated but not controlled:** in Tamil Nadu over 95% of diagnosed people were treated, yet the diabetes control rate was 9.8% (the abstract does not state the denominator) [D1-03-F09].
- **Slow insulin starts:** a mean of 7 years of diabetes before insulin in one older Indian study (search summary only) [D1-03-F30]. AIIMS Bhopal clinic records (950 patients, not a trial) classed 11.8% as insulin inertia (delay in starting or raising insulin), mean HbA1c (a blood test showing average sugar over about three months) 8.43% [D1-03-F29].

OUR VIEW. So what for us: two steps leak, diagnosis and control. The step from "treated" to "controlled" depends on the dose being raised and the medicine being taken, which is the decision our loop prepares.

### C4. Treatment is not control

Three Indian data sets show that getting people onto medicine is not enough, and that people who return do far better than those who do not.

**Tamil Nadu survey.** The state's doorstep programme, Makkalai Thedi Maruthuvam, was surveyed in 6,856 adults aged 30+ across all 38 districts (paper abstract, 2025; a South First news report gives 6,809) [D1-03-F09]. Screening reached 79.4% for diabetes and 82.6% for hypertension, and over 95% of diagnosed people were on treatment. Control rates remained low: 9.8% for diabetes and 35.4% for hypertension; the abstract does not say whether these are shares of everyone with the disease, the diagnosed or the treated [D1-03-F09].

**India Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI) cohort.** A government programme with a fixed drug protocol. The cohort registered 21,895 patients at 24 sites in Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Telangana (Jan 2018-Jun 2019), followed up Jul-Sep 2019, with no comparison group [D1-03-F10].
- Only 11,274 (51%) returned [D1-03-F10].
- Among returners, control rose from 26.3% to 59.8% [D1-03-F10].
- Control was 70.4% in primary care against 52.2% in secondary care [D1-03-F10].
- Protocol drugs: amlodipine, telmisartan or losartan, chlorthalidone or hydrochlorothiazide; 44.6% were on amlodipine 5 mg alone at follow-up [D1-03-F11].

**IHCI district surveys.** Before and after, same nine districts, no comparison districts [D1-01-F10, D1-01-F11, D1-03-F12]:

| | 2018-19 | 2023-24 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| People with hypertension | 7,047 | 7,324 | |
| Aware | 52.4% [D1-01-F10] | 58.5% [D1-03-F12] | +6.1 points (OUR ARITHMETIC) |
| Treated | 40.8% [D1-01-F10] | 44.7% [D1-03-F12] | +3.9 points (OUR ARITHMETIC) |
| Controlled | 14.5% [D1-01-F10] | 21.2% [D1-01-F11] | +6.7 points [D1-03-F12] |

**Return visits.** About 27% of registered IHCI patients did not return in Jan-Mar 2021 (search summary only) [D1-03-F13]. IHCI enrolled about 4 million patients, about 12.5% of the estimated total, by around 2022 (search summary only) [D1-03-F13]. Pooled Indian non-adherence to BP drugs is 48%, with a prediction interval of 9-90% (search summary only) [D1-03-F44].

OUR VIEW. So what for us: our loop must reach the half who do not come back, between visits.

### C5. The specialist and time gap

Few specialists work where most patients live, and doctors who see chronic patients have very little time per visit.

**Rural community health centre (CHC) specialist posts, March 2023** [D1-03-F16]:

| Specialty | Shortfall | Share of required posts |
|---|---|---|
| Surgeon | 4,578 | 83.3% [D1-03-F16] |
| Physician | 4,499 | 81.9% [D1-03-F16] |
| Paediatrician | 4,425 | 80.5% [D1-03-F16] |
| Obstetrician-gynaecologist | 4,078 | 74.2% [D1-03-F16] |
| All four | 17,551 of 21,964 (4,413 in post) | 79.9% [D1-03-F16] |

The article's rows sum to 17,580, not 17,551 [D1-03-F16]. The shortfall has stayed near 17,500 since 2014-15 [D1-03-F17].

**Specialist counts.** The only figures are from a 2017 parliamentary committee (search summary only): about 4,000 cardiologists, 1,200 nephrologists and 650 endocrinologists, against the committee's estimated needs of 88,000, 40,000 and 28,000 [D1-03-F18]. The government told Parliament it does not hold speciality-wise doctor data (news report of the reply, 13 Apr 2025) [D3-07-F22].

**Consultation length.** Indian primary-care visits lasted about 2.3 minutes (2013, stopwatch, 412 visits) and 2 minutes (2015) [D1-03-F24].

**Doctor ratio and its caveat.** The government gave 1:811 in Rajya Sabha (about 2 Dec 2025), counting 13,88,185 allopathic doctors plus 7,51,768 AYUSH practitioners (traditional medicine) and "assuming that 80 per cent" are available [D1-03-F40]. Allopathic doctors alone give a lower ratio. OUR ARITHMETIC from the note: 13,88,185 x 0.8 = about 11.1 lakh, about 0.79 per 1,000 people if the population is about 1.4 billion (an assumption, not sourced) [D1-03-F40].

OUR VIEW. So what for us: a two-minute visit has no time to read two weeks of home readings. Software should prepare the next protocol step for a general doctor or nurse to approve, with the specialist owning the protocol.

### C6. Cost and follow-up

Families pay a large share of health costs themselves, and the share rose in the latest year on record.

- **Out-of-pocket share:** 39.4% of total health expenditure in 2021-22 and 43.41% in 2022-23 (National Health Accounts released 27 May 2026, via a news report) [D1-03-F23]. In 2022-23 household spending rose by ₹26,375 crore while government spending fell by nearly ₹49,000 crore [D1-03-F23].
- **Per visit:** medicines were 61.15% of spend per outpatient spell in a Delhi report; another report says median outpatient spend is zero (search summary only) [D1-03-F49].
- **Cheaper medicines:** 18,646 Jan Aushadhi Kendras (generic medicine shops), Feb 2026, priced 50-80% below branded [D1-03-F20].
- **Diabetes spend:** about US$109.5 per person and over US$9.8 billion in total (IDF 2024) [D1-03-F28].

NOT FOUND: household cost per chronic-care visit; travel distance and lost wages; the share of chronic patients who see a doctor each year; loss to follow-up outside IHCI; how long a dose change is delayed, in months.

OUR VIEW. So what for us: avoided trips save patients money, but without a rupee figure per visit we cannot yet claim a saving.

### C10. Heart failure in India

No national count of heart failure was found, and the estimates differ widely: 1.3 to 4.6 million cases in a 2010 estimate built from disease-specific figures, whose authors say no surveillance exists (quoted in a 2024 review) [D3-07-F24], against about 8 to 10 million, about 1% of the population, in a 2016 review of local and global studies (search summary only) [D3-07-F25]. For stroke, the Global Burden of Disease study put India's prevalent cases at 9.4 million in 2021, up from 4.4 million in 1990, with over 1.25 million new cases a year (press report quoting the authors) [D3-07-F26]. The registries show patients leave hospital on too few of the drugs that work and stop them afterwards. The three guideline classes are beta blocker, RAS blocker (ACE inhibitor or ARB) and MRA.

| Registry | Who | Treatment finding | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| National Heart Failure Registry | 10,851 acute patients, 53 hospitals, 21 states | 47.5% of eligible patients received guideline drugs | 90-day death 14.2%; 1-year death 22.1% | [D1-01-F47, D1-01-F48] |
| Indian College of Cardiology registry | 5,182 acute patients, 17 centres, 2019-2021 | All three classes taken: 18.8% of men, 15.9% of women | 1-year death 18.1% | [D1-01-F49] |
| Trivandrum, Kerala | 1,205 admissions, 16 hospitals, 2013 | 25% of weak-pump patients discharged on all three | 5-year death 59% | [D1-01-F35] |
| NORIN-STEMI, Delhi | Heart-attack patients with weak pumping | MRA use fell from 69% at discharge to 6% at 1 year | | [D1-01-F50] |

The national registries differ (47.5% against about 17%) partly because one counts prescription and the other adherence [D1-01-F49]. None reports doses as a share of target [D1-01-F35, D1-01-F47, D1-01-F49].

OUR VIEW. So what for us: a clear dose gap with high death rates, but safe titration needs lab values, so heart failure is a later protocol for the same engine.

### C11. What we could not find

- Current counts of endocrinologists, cardiologists, nephrologists, chest physicians and physiotherapists.
- Doctors per 1,000 people, rural against urban.
- A heart-failure count newer than 2016; a stroke-survivor count from a primary source.
- Depression prevalence after 2015-16 from a primary source; a depression-only treatment gap.
- Share of chronic patients who see a doctor each year; loss to follow-up outside IHCI; HbA1c at insulin start in India; delay to dose change in months.
- Household cost per chronic-care visit; distance and wage-loss figures.
- Official WhatsApp India users; unique smartphone users from a regulator.
- Japan's 2023-24 hypertension control rate; a dated WHO India NCD death share.

### C12. So what for us

OUR VIEW.
- **Blood pressure first.** 35.5% of adults 20+, against 11.4% for diabetes on the same survey [D1-03-F01].
- **The leak is after treatment starts.** Over 95% of diagnosed Tamil Nadu patients are treated, yet the hypertension control rate was 35.4% (denominator not stated) [D1-03-F09]. Screening already runs at crore scale [D1-03-F20].
- **The return visit is the bottleneck.** 51% of IHCI patients returned, and among those who returned control rose from 26.3% to 59.8%, with no comparison group [D1-03-F10]. The pilot endpoint is control at 90 days among people who would not have come back.
- **Prescriber time is scarce.** About 80% of required rural CHC posts in four specialities (surgeon, physician, obstetrician-gynaecologist, paediatrician) were unfilled in March 2023 [D1-03-F16], and visits lasted about two minutes in 2013-2015 [D1-03-F24]. Software drafts the step; a clinician approves it.
- **Reach needs shared phones and health workers.** 57.4% of rural women own a phone [D1-03-F42].
