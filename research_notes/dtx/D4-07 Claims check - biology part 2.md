# D4-07 Claims check - biology part 2 (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-14)

## Summary

Checked 45 fact IDs from `D4-04 Why each safety check exists.md`: every fact named in its Digest (including the "Surprises" line) and in "What this suggests for the product", plus every threshold fact a safety rule would rest on. Routes: PubMed E-utilities `efetch` abstracts (a different route from the note's Europe PMC search links), Europe PMC full text XML for two papers, openFDA label JSON and the DailyMed v2 SPL service for the labels (the note used DailyMed drugInfo pages), and the NICE pages and KDIGO, UKKA and Resolve to Save Lives PDFs re-downloaded and read as text (same URLs; no other route exists for these). Opened 14 Nov 2026.

Verdicts: CONFIRMED 43, CORRECTED 2, CONTESTED 0, NOT FOUND 0.

Corrections and findings that change a product safety rule:

1. D4-04-F62 (CORRECTED). The North Indian "45% in the hot season" is not a rate among diuretic users. It is the share of 247 hospitalised hyponatraemia cases (any cause, adult medical-ward inpatients, case-control stage, King George's Medical University hospitals, Lucknow) that fell in April to June; October to March held 46.6% (115 of 247) and July to September 9% (21 of 247). Per month, April to June is still the busiest (OUR ARITHMETIC: 111 / 3 = 37 a month against 115 / 6 = 19 and 21 / 3 = 7), so a weak seasonal signal exists, but diuretic use was not independently associated with low sodium in this study, patients were middle-aged (mean about 50), and the note's "mainly loop diuretics likely" is wrong (thiazides were the commonest class, 45.9%, in the prevalence stage). The "sodium test within the first month of chlorthalidone ... and the hot season" suggestion should rest on F58 and F61 (first 30 days, older age, low weight, low potassium), with season at most a soft flag.
2. Creatinine rule scope (F23, F27, no verdict change). The values are right, but the rule as written in the Digest ("30% or more: look for dehydration or NSAIDs, then stop or reduce") is NICE NG203 1.6.21 wording. KDIGO PP 3.2.5 says "continue ... unless serum creatinine rises by more than 30% within 4 weeks" (strictly above 30%, within 4 weeks) and does not itself give the investigate-then-stop step; KDIGO adds that a rise under 30% does not mandate change "as long as" there is no hyperkalaemia or fluid retention. Both NICE NG203 and KDIGO apply to adults with CKD. The only one of the three that applies to every ACE inhibitor or ARB start is UKKA Guideline 2.3 (urea and electrolytes at 1 to 2 weeks and after every dose titration, grade 1A). The rule engine should cite UKKA for the general case and use "30% or more" (NICE) as the conservative cut-off.
3. Potassium bands (F34, confirmed). All bands and windows match. Two details for the rule text: the 3-day recheck reads "within 3 days, or as soon as feasible" for mild hyperkalaemia "detected unexpectedly in the community"; and stopping at K+ 6 or more applies to patients "who do not meet the criteria for treatment with patiromer or sodium zirconium cyclosilicate", not "unless on a binder". The UKKA guideline (original July 2020, updated October 2023 and July 2026) carries a review date of October 2026, which has passed.
4. First-trimester comparison (F31, F32, no verdict change). Both numbers are right but measure different outcomes: Cooper's RR 2.71 is any major malformation against no antihypertensive; Li's OR 1.14 is congenital heart defects against untreated hypertensive mothers. The like-for-like Li figure is any birth defect, first-trimester-only ACE inhibitor against hypertension controls: adjusted OR 0.97 (0.67 to 1.41) (Li 2011 Table 2). Both studies are ACE-inhibitor data, not ARB data.
5. Home orthostatic test (F50, no verdict change). Kappa 0.90 is for lying-to-standing tests in 25 people; the product suggestion is sit-then-stand. NICE 1.1.7 says to repeat lying down if a seated start is negative despite symptoms, and F44's trials (seated to standing) were flagged as missing drops.

Other precision fixes: F01 label provenance (PD-Rx Pharmaceuticals repackager listing of an Inventia Healthcare product distributed by Ascend, not Alkem). F12's 14.6% in women pools all doses; do not pair it with the 10.8% at 10 mg. F57: both IHCI sheets do mention "close clinical and biochemical monitoring" for ACE inhibitor or ARB use in chronic kidney disease, with no test or interval, so "no recheck" holds for the main pathway.
