## Problem 1: Fishermen crossing the India-Sri Lanka boundary (IMBL) and no reliable at-sea alert/communication
- Track: Space Tech & Defence (NavIC/GNSS + embedded); also Frontier Hardware
- Who suffers: Mechanised-boat fishermen from Rameswaram, Pamban, Nagapattinam, Karaikal and Pudukkottai (Palk Bay). Arrested by the Sri Lankan Navy several times a month; boats (the family's sole livelihood asset) seized and kept for months; families blockade highways and strike.
- How they cope today: Phone/handheld GPS; the Coast Guard gives free "distress alert equipment" that fishermen seldom carry (2012 quote, old); MSSRF's Fisher Friend phone app (IMBL beeper at ~5 km from the line); ISRO's NavIC Distress Alert Transmitter (DAT / DAT-SG, >20,000 in use). Fishermen associations mostly press for political fixes: release of men and boats, talks with Sri Lanka, "take back Katchatheevu".
- Public proof (tier B, provisional; news-only because Reddit/YouTube unreachable; 6 independent events on different dates, plus hard numbers):
  - https://www.adaderana.lk/news/111415/tamil-nadu-fishermen-strike-in-rameswaram-over-frequent-sri-lanka-navy-arrests- — 13 Aug 2025 — (fetched) ~10,000 fishermen on indefinite strike, 700+ mechanised boats idle at Rameswaram; 61 fishers arrested since 15 June and none released (Rameswaram fishermen associations)
  - https://ianslive.in/one-day-protest-today-by-rameswaram-fishermen-as-families-demand-release-of-12-arrested-by-sri-lankan-navy--20251224081835 — 24 Dec 2025 — (fetched) one-day strike; families blocked the national highway at Thangachimadam over 12 Rameswaram men arrested; boats called "the sole means of livelihood for hundreds of families"; demand for "a permanent and lasting solution"
  - https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/01/26/illegal-fishing-sri-lankan-navy-arrests-34-indian-fishermen.html — 26 Jan 2025 — (fetched) Sri Lankan Navy arrested 529 Indian fishermen in 2024; 34 more arrested 25-26 Jan 2025; fishermen associations urge the Union govt to act
  - https://www.indiatvnews.com/tamil-nadu/chennai-fishermen-stage-protest-after-sri-lankan-navy-arrests-17-fishermen-from-rameshwaram-2024-09-30-954648 — 30 Sep 2024 (date from URL) — (snippet) protest after 17 Rameswaram fishermen arrested and 2 boats confiscated
  - https://www.thenewsminute.com/tamil-nadu/rameswaram-fishermen-go-on-indefinite-strike-over-sri-lankan-navy-arrests — undated — (snippet) another indefinite strike; shows recurrence across years
  - https://icsf.net/?p=37149 — The Hindu article republished by ICSF, page footer says 2012 — (fetched) Coast Guard CO, Mandapam: fishermen "seldom take" free distress alert equipment, cross the IMBL knowingly ("calculated risk") though equipped with GPS; IMBL is ~16 nm from Rameswaram.   - https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/07/31/navic-satellite-navigation-crisis.html — 31 Jul 2026 — (fetched) NavIC emergency-alert messaging still works and "has helped fishermen receive cyclone warnings far from mobile towers"
- Hard number: 529 Indian fishermen arrested by Sri Lanka in 2024 — https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/01/26/illegal-fishing-sri-lankan-navy-arrests-34-indian-fishermen.html, 26 Jan 2025 (fetched). 61 arrested 15 Jun-13 Aug 2025 (Ada Derana link above, fetched). Use 529 (2024) as the anchor.
- Who has tried:
  - MSSRF "Fisher Friend" app — built on BREW with Qualcomm/Tata CDMA in 2007, later added INCOIS data; IMBL beeper at ~5 km; user counts conflict across sources: 16,000 (Qualcomm case study), >29,000 on 576 landing centres/59 districts/9 states (Springer paper), >50,000 (a 2019-era source) — https://www.qualcomm.com/content/dam/qcomm-martech/dm-assets/documents/casestudy_indiafisherfriend_feb2019.pdf and https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s40012-018-0212-1 (snippet). Alive; no evidence it ended the arrests.
  - ISRO NavIC-based DAT, first demo'd Sept/Dec 2018 (about 2,000 built, ~Rs 3,500 per unit in the 2018 PTI story; a second report said Rs 9,000 retail, Rs 1,500 for traditional fishermen) — https://business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/navic-home-grown-gps-system-to-alert-fishermen-about-natural-disasters-maritime-boundary-118090700727_1.html (snippet). DAT-SG (second generation, UHF, Bluetooth to phone, local-language app, SAGARMITRA database, two-way acknowledgement): ">20,000 in use" — https://www.drishtiias.com/hindi/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/distress-alert-transmitter-for-fishermen/print/manual, 23 Jan 2024 (fetched).
  - Coast Guard free distress alert equipment (Mandapam) — 2012 quote above.
  - Caveat on the NavIC base: only 3 NavIC satellites operational as of 31 Jul 2026 (IRNSS-1B, 1I, NVS-01); 4 are needed for standalone positioning; users keep accuracy through GPS/Galileo/GLONASS/BeiDou — The Week, 31 Jul 2026 (fetched). A Telangana Today editorial of 18 Mar 2026 says up to five satellites unusable (fetched).
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): A cheap boat-side multi-constellation geofence unit (ESP32 + multi-GNSS module + LoRa/BLE + buzzer, offline IMBL polygon, Tamil/Telugu voice alert) that also relays position to a shore/other-boat mesh when out of phone coverage, and shows distance to line plus last known safe ground, not only a beep.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 4 — all parts are off-the-shelf; the test is a walking/vehicle geofence replay on a campus polygon plus a LoRa range test.
- Rough market pointer: Rameswaram alone had 700+ mechanised boats idle in Aug 2025 (Ada Derana, fetched); at the 2018 DAT unit price of ~Rs 3,500 (snippet) that is ~Rs 25 lakh for one harbour (my arithmetic). Statewide boat count NOT SEARCHED. Real buyer is the state fisheries department or central scheme (B2G), so the market is small unless it bundles safety, insurance and subsidy proof.
- Risks / regulation: Honest red flag: the Coast Guard (2012) says crossings are knowing and economic (fishing grounds), so an alert alone may not change behaviour; products already exist (Fisher Friend, DAT-SG) and there is no source in this round showing fishermen complaining that those devices fail. Needs fisherman interviews, which this round did not do. Any satellite-messaging add-on touches ISRO/NavIC and DoT rules.

## Problem 2: GNSS jamming and spoofing near borders and airports, with no national detection or reporting system
- Track: Space Tech & Defence
- Who suffers: Airlines and pilots on approach to Amritsar, Jammu and Delhi (and, per reports, Kolkata, Mumbai, Chennai); air-traffic controllers; also anything that trusts GPS time or position (ships, drones, telecom and power timing).
- How they cope today: Fall back to ground-based navaids (ILS/VOR; Delhi RWY 10 GPS-based procedures hit while other runways unaffected), NOTAMs where interference is repeatedly reported, airline SOPs; DGCA made reporting mandatory in Nov 2023 and issued a 10-minute reporting SOP on 10 Nov 2025; AAI asked the Wireless Monitoring Organisation (WMO) to find the source.
- Public proof (tier B, provisional; 4 independent official/news items, no social):
  - https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/03/20/del31-lsq-flights-gps-interference.html — 20 Mar 2025 — (fetched) Lok Sabha written reply (MoS Civil Aviation Murlidhar Mohol): about 465 GPS interference and spoofing incidents "in the border region between November 2023 and February 2025", mostly Amritsar and Jammu
  - https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/12/01/del69-rsq-flights-gps-spoofing.html — 1 Dec 2025 — (snippet) Rajya Sabha reply by Civil Aviation Minister Ram Mohan Naidu: some flights reported GPS spoofing near IGI Delhi during GPS-based landing on RWY 10; contingency procedures activated; AAI asked WMO to identify the source (the WION fetch of the same story failed with ECONNRESET)
  - https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/11/11/del89-avi-dgca-spoofing.html — 11 Nov 2025 — (snippet) DGCA SOP dated 10 Nov 2025: pilots and ATCs must report GPS spoofing within 10 minutes
  - https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2025/12/06/gps-spoofing-the-invisible-danger-in-india-s-aerospace.html — 6 Dec 2025 — (fetched) analysis: AI302 aborted an approach to Delhi on 7 Nov; India has "no centralized reporting system"; many events logged only as "navigation failures" or "interference", hiding spoofing as the cause; no accident conclusively linked to spoofed GNSS. The same piece says "over 800 flights were delayed or diverted that day": single source, not matched by any other item I saw, treat as unverified.
  - https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/securing-india-s-skies-countering-the-threat-of-gps-spoofing-and-hybrid-warfare — undated — (snippet title only) think-tank framing as hybrid-warfare risk
- Hard number: ~465 GPS interference/spoofing incidents, Nov 2023-Feb 2025, mostly Amritsar and Jammu — Lok Sabha reply, https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/03/20/del31-lsq-flights-gps-interference.html, 20 Mar 2025 (fetched).
- Who has tried: Govt side only: DGCA circular (Nov 2023) and SOP (Nov 2025), AAI/WMO source-finding. No Indian startup with a civil GNSS-integrity product was found in this round (NOT SEARCHED, so absence is not proven). NavIC itself is degraded (3 operational, 31 Jul 2026), which makes a GNSS-independent integrity layer more, not less, relevant.
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): A low-cost GNSS integrity sensor node (multi-constellation receiver logging C/N0 and position jumps, a second-source time check, plus an SDR spectrum watcher on L1/L5) that flags jamming vs spoofing, geo-tags the event and feeds a shared map/log, with a GNSS-denied fallback (visual-inertial) demo for survey drones.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 3 — building the node and logging is easy; proving detection without radiating fake GNSS signals is hard. Use recorded public spoofing datasets or a conducted (cabled, shielded) test; do not transmit.
- Rough market pointer: Buyers are AAI, DGCA, airports, ports, telecom/power timing sites and defence; no sized figure found. The Week (31 Jul 2026, fetched) says banking, telecom and power grids rely on NavIC timing; count of sites NOT SEARCHED.
- Risks / regulation: Transmitting GNSS-like signals is illegal without licence (Indian Telegraph/WPC rules; my knowledge, not sourced here). Sells to government, slow procurement. Defensive detection only.

## Problem 3: Cheap drone detection for the Punjab border strip, jails and district police (drone drops of drugs, weapons, phones)
- Track: Space Tech & Defence (civil/dual-use, detection only; no jamming or kill)
- Who suffers: Border villages and farmers' fields along the Punjab frontier (drops land in fields), BSF and Punjab Police, prison administrations (contraband phones/drugs), and the families of addicts.
- How they cope today: BSF electronic countermeasures (several drones "presumed to have crashed" after ECM), field searches after sightings, anti-drone systems on the border, Punjab "hawai nakas", district orders making 500 m around jails a no-drone zone. Punjab Police plans second-line anti-drone installations.
- Public proof (tier B, provisional; recurring weekly news plus a hard number, no social):

  - https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/india/bsf-punjab-police-jointly-recover-suspected-pakistani-drone-on-tarn-taran-border-dji-mavic-3-classic-drone-latest-updates-2025-05-16-990508 — 16 May 2025 — (snippet) DJI Mavic 3 Classic with pistol and magazines found at Kalash, Tarn Taran; BSF says it likely fell due to electronic countermeasures

  - https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/simply-punjab/stumbling-blocks-for-jail-administration-in-punjab — undated — (snippet) prison official: "The latest threat is the easy entry of contraband using drones" (phones still the bigger headache)

- Hard number: 294 drones seized on the Punjab border in 2024 — Lok Sabha reply reported 18 Mar 2025 (fetched). Context (snippets): a different baseline of 260+ intercepted/recovered on the whole Pakistan border in 2024 vs 110 in 2023 (egov.eletsonline.com, Dec 2024), and Amit Shah's claim that detection/neutralisation in Punjab rose from 3% to 55% after laser-equipped anti-drone systems (devdiscourse / egov.eletsonline.com, Dec 2024). No data on jail-level deployments found: "I did not find reporting on anti-drone systems installed specifically at Indian prisons" (search summary).
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): A passive, low-cost detector node for consumer-drone control/video links (SDR energy and protocol fingerprinting on 2.4/5.8 GHz, since recovered drones are mostly commercial DJI Mavic 3 Classic units) fused with a MEMS-mic acoustic array and a small classifier, giving bearing and alert on a phone; detect and alert only.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 3 — acoustic plus RF classification is doable with a team-owned drone in a permitted area; range and false-alarm testing is the weak spot.
- Rough market pointer: Punjab's quoted spend is Rs 51.4 crore for 9 units, roughly Rs 5.7 crore per unit (my arithmetic, from a snippet). A detection-only unit for a jail or police station at a small fraction of that is the target; number of Punjab/other state jails NOT SEARCHED.
- Risks / regulation: Defensive and civil only; jamming or interception is a government monopoly (WPC/MHA). Most of the volume is a military/BSF buyer problem, so civil entry points are jails, district police, critical infrastructure and airports. Drones recovered are mostly commercial models, which helps RF fingerprinting but needs real drone data.

## Problem 4: Communications blackout in hill and coastal disasters (Wayanad 2024, Dharali/Himachal 2025, Kerala 2018)

- How they cope today: Ham radio operators (Wayanad collector asked for them), satellite phones for rescuers (Dharali), diesel generators on towers, airlifted telecom kit, intra-circle roaming, families trekking in to look for relatives, free data and bill extensions for affected users.

## Problem 5: Illegal sand mining and river-bed change are not being caught despite "satellite monitoring" existing on paper
- Track: Space Tech & Defence (satellite data) or Open Track
- Who suffers: State mines departments and district collectors (revenue loss, enforcement), river-bank communities and bridge owners (bank erosion), courts that must prove volumes, honest licence holders.
- How they cope today: Central Mining Surveillance System (satellite alerts, launched 2016, mainly for major minerals), 2020 Sand Mining Enforcement and Monitoring Guidelines (drones, satellite imagery, real-time tracking of transport), state CCTV at sites (TN says 38 districts' sites monitored with CCTV), one-off IIT or CPCB analyses ordered by courts, manual FIRs.
- Public proof (tier B, provisional; 4 independent court/government records; no social):
  - https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/karnataka-high-court/bhagavant-alagur-v-the-state-of-karnataka-ors-2025khc-d3770-sand-mining-satellite-based-imagery-system-1570001 — 4 Mar 2025 — (fetched) Karnataka High Court calls the surveillance system "improper", suggests satellite imagery to map river boundaries and sand bars, directs real-time monitoring with ISRO help: "It is high time that the available technology is harnessed to address these kind of issues"
  - https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/tamil-nadu/sc-asks-tamil-nadu-to-submit-data-on-illegal-sand-mining-worth-rs-4730-crore — 12 Feb 2026 (hearing 11 Feb) — (fetched) Supreme Court (CJI Surya Kant) orders TN to submit all sand-mining FIRs; ED found illegal mining worth Rs 4,730 crore but its probe stalled (not a PMLA scheduled offence); state counsel says ED assumptions rest on two-date satellite images of a river
  - https://www.miningweekly.com/article/mines-ministry-hails-indias-satellite-based-illegal-mining-monitoring-a-success-2017-05-25 — 25 May 2017 — (snippet) Mines Ministry calls its satellite-based surveillance very successful but admits minor-mineral (sand) mining is rising while major-mineral cases fall
  - https://indialegallive.com/constitutional-law-news/courts-news/swan-river-punjab-ngt-cpcb-satellite-images-illegal-sand-mining/ — undated — (snippet) NGT orders CPCB to analyse five years of satellite images of a Punjab river stretch to establish extent of illegal sand mining
  - https://tribe.article-14.com/post/as-a-satellite-confirms-what-rajasthan-officials-deny-a-court-tries-to-reverse-destruction-of-a-life-giving-river-6771bb88389cc — undated — (snippet title) satellite imagery contradicting Rajasthan officials' denial of mining
  - Also in the search summary but not tied to a specific opened page: an IIT Patna geospatial analysis confirming unauthorised sand excavation led to a Bihar Mines and Geology FIR dated 21 Aug 2025 (attribution to a URL unverified; treat as unconfirmed).
- Hard number: Rs 4,730 crore of illegal mining found by ED in Tamil Nadu (as stated by petitioner's counsel, contested by state) — The News Minute, 12 Feb 2026 (fetched). 2019-20 Punjab: 463 FIRs and 577 vehicles impounded (Lok Sabha, 2020, snippet from the miningweekly/indiakanoon results, old).
- Who has tried: Government MSS (satellite) and state CCTV systems; IIT Patna, CPCB and NGT one-off analyses. No Indian startup selling sand-mining detection was identified (NOT SEARCHED). Why it stalls, per the court record: surveillance exists but is "improper" in implementation; evidence is two-date imagery, which the state disputes; sand is a state subject (minor mineral).
- Deep-tech wedge (one line): Free Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 change detection over a named river stretch (water-line and sand-bar change, SAR backscatter/coherence over exposed bed), time-stamped, geo-registered and hash-signed into a court-ready "evidence pack", optionally validated with cheap drone photogrammetry volumes.
- Overnight feasibility (1-5): 4 — free data and Google Earth Engine/Python are enough for one stretch; validation against known sites is the hard part (scope the claim to detection of change, not tonnage).
- Rough market pointer: Buyers are 28 state mines/geology departments and district administrations; no monitoring-contract figure found. TN's Rs 4,730 crore alleged value is a loss indicator, not a software budget. State count is my own knowledge, not sourced here.
- Risks / regulation: Politically sensitive (mining mafia, enforcement risk to anyone publicising locations); sold to government; courts need admissible evidence chains; data resolution (10 m) limits small-scale extraction detection.
