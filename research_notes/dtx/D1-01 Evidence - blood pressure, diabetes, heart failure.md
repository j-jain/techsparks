# D1-01 Evidence: feedback loops in blood pressure, diabetes, heart failure (agent: Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-11-13)

## Digest

12 facts that matter most:
1. Home BP self-monitoring ALONE: -1.0 mm Hg (95% CI -3.3 to 1.2), no effect; with co-interventions including drug titration -6.1 mm Hg (D1-01-F05).
2. Self-titration by algorithm in high-risk patients: -9.2 mm Hg systolic at 12 months (D1-01-F02). Pharmacist telemonitoring titration: control 57% vs 30% (D1-01-F04).
3. Reminder app alone: adherence score up, systolic -0.5 mm Hg (NS) (D1-01-F06).
4. Village doctors titrating by protocol (China): BP <130/80 57% vs 20%; at 4 years CVD HR 0.75 (age 60+) and 0.64 (under 60); no rise in falls, hypotension, syncope or kidney events (D1-01-F09, F38).
5. India: decision-support app for nurses and doctors (mWellcare) null for BP and HbA1c (D1-01-F46). India BP control only 14.5% to 21.2% in IHCI districts (D1-01-F10, F11).
6. PHTI 2024: only "medication management" hypertension tools reach clinically meaningful falls; monitoring and coaching tools do not (D1-01-F39). Digital diabetes tools: minimal benefit, higher spending, exceptions insulin starters and ketosis (D1-01-F40).
7. Insulin dose software in T2D: d-Nav HbA1c -1.0 vs -0.3 with equal hypoglycaemia (D1-01-F13); TeleDiab-2 -1.48 vs -0.92 (D1-01-F14).
8. Closed loop: T1D +11 points time in range (D1-01-F24); T2D RCT 2IQP -0.6 HbA1c and +14 points time in range vs CGM alone (D1-01-F43).
9. CGM without algorithm in basal-insulin T2D: HbA1c -0.4 (D1-01-F42).
10. STRONG-HF rapid up-titration: death or HF readmission 15.2% vs 23.3%, RR 0.66; more non-serious adverse events (D1-01-F27).
11. HF telemonitoring: TELE-HF and BEAT-HF null (D1-01-F32, F33); TIM-HF2 positive, death HR 0.70 (D1-01-F30); pooled: passive telemonitoring alone RR 0.93 NS (D1-01-F54).
12. India HF: only 25% of HFrEF discharged on all three classes (Trivandrum) (D1-01-F35); ICC registry 16-19% adherence (D1-01-F49); MRA use 69% to 6% within a year after STEMI (D1-01-F50).

3 surprises:
- TASMINH4: telemonitoring added nothing significant over paper self-monitoring when the GP titrated (D1-01-F03).
- The CRHCP control arm got only enhanced usual care, yet the intervention also included free or discounted drugs and doctor incentives (SNIPPET, F38 note), so the effect is not the protocol alone.
- Twin Health trials (India, Cleveland) report very large effects (72.7% "remission"; 71% vs 2.4% at goal) but are company-linked, single-centre or 2:1 open-label (D1-01-F19, F56).

Against starting lead 1: mostly supported, but HERB-DH1 (lifestyle app, no drug change) still gave -2.4 to -4.3 mm Hg (D1-01-F07), and CGM alone helped in T2D (F17, F42). Against lead 2: CRHCP gave non-physicians authority to titrate, supervised; mWellcare, with prompts to clinicians, failed (F46).

## Findings

Method note: PubMed web pages returned a cookie wall to the fetch tool, so abstracts were read through the Europe PMC REST API (`resultType=core`). Each "OPENED" below means the abstract text was read in full there; URLs point to the PubMed record for the same PMID.

### Q1. Blood pressure: home monitoring WITH a change of medicine vs usual care

- D1-01-F01 | TASMINH2 (2010) | RCT, 527 patients, 24 UK general practices; BP >140/90 despite treatment. Self-monitoring + patient self-titration by pre-agreed plan + telemonitoring vs usual care. Primary: change in systolic BP. Difference 3.7 mm Hg (95% CI 0.8 to 6.6) at 6 months; 5.4 mm Hg (2.4 to 8.5; p=0.0004) at 12 months. Leg swelling more common with self-management (32% vs 22%, p=0.022), other side effects similar. Analysis of completers without imputation (91%). | Lancet, 8 Jul 2010 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20619448/ | OPENED, CITED
- D1-01-F02 | TASMIN-SR (2014) | RCT, 552 patients at high CV risk (stroke, CHD, diabetes, CKD), 59 UK practices, 2011-2013. Self-monitoring + individualised self-titration algorithm (home target 120/75) vs usual care. 12-month BP 128.2/73.8 vs 137.8/76.3; adjusted systolic difference 9.2 mm Hg (95% CI 5.7 to 12.7); diastolic 3.4 (1.8 to 5.0). Multiple imputation: 8.8 (4.9 to 12.7). "without excessive adverse events". Primary data from 81%. | JAMA, Aug 2014 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25157723/ | OPENED, CITED
- D1-01-F03 | TASMINH4 (2018) | RCT, 1,182 patients, 142 UK practices, 3 arms: self-monitoring (GP titrates from home readings), self-monitoring + telemonitoring, usual care (clinic readings). Primary: clinic systolic at 12 months. Self-monitoring -3.5 mm Hg (95% CI -5.8 to -1.2); telemonitoring -4.7 (-7.0 to -2.4) vs usual care. Telemonitoring vs plain self-monitoring: -1.2 (-3.5 to 1.2), not significant. Adverse events similar in all arms. Funder: NIHR. 85% analysed. | Lancet, 27 Feb 2018 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29499873/ | OPENED, CITED
  - Note: in TASMINH4 the clinician still made the dose change; the "loop" was home readings feeding GP titration. Telemonitoring added no significant gain over paper self-monitoring.
- D1-01-F04 | HyperLink (Margolis 2013) | Cluster RCT, 16 primary care clinics, 450 adults with uncontrolled BP, Minneapolis-St Paul, USA. Home BP telemonitor data sent to pharmacists who adjusted drugs vs usual care; 12 months intervention + 6 months off. BP controlled at both 6 and 12 months: 57.2% (95% CI 44.8 to 68.7) vs 30.0% (23.2 to 37.8), p=0.001. Systolic difference -10.7 mm Hg (-14.3 to -7.3) at 6 months, -9.7 (-13.4 to -6.0) at 12 months, -6.6 (-10.7 to -2.5) at 18 months (6 months after stopping). | JAMA, 3 Jul 2013 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23821088/ | OPENED, CITED

### Q3/Q6 independent verdicts: Peterson Health Technology Institute (PHTI)

### Q6. What separates programmes that worked from those that failed (recorded statements, no ranking)

- Tucker IPD (F05): self-monitoring alone -1.0 mm Hg (NS); with intensive support including "systematic medication titration" -6.1 mm Hg. Authors' own wording quoted in F05.
- TASMINH4 (F03): adding telemonitoring to self-monitoring when GP titrates gave no significant extra fall (-1.2, CI crosses 0).
- PHTI hypertension (F39): monitoring tools "not clinically meaningful"; medication management by dedicated virtual care teams "clinically meaningful and more rapid"; monitoring rated as possibly useful only "for providers who consistently act on monitoring data".
- PHTI diabetes (F40): non-CGM monitoring and coaching tools minimal benefit; exceptions are insulin starters and nutritional ketosis.
- HF: TELE-HF (F32, data to own clinicians, no protocol) and BEAT-HF (F33, central nurses, protocolised actions, no drug-titration mandate stated) null; TIM-HF2 (F30, structured remote management centre) positive; STRONG-HF (F27, explicit dose targets within 2 weeks) positive; pooled analysis (F54) finds passive telemonitoring alone not significant.
- India: mWellcare (F46), decision-support prompts for nurses and doctors, null; CRHCP (F09, F38), non-physicians given authority to start and titrate drugs by protocol with free/discounted drugs, large effect on BP and hard outcomes.
- Diabetes: 2IQP (F43) control arm had CGM too; the algorithm changing doses added -0.6 HbA1c and +14 points time in range. MOBILE (F42) CGM alone in basal-insulin users -0.4 HbA1c.
