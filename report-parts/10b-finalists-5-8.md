### 3.5 Counterfeit-medicine checker: a ₹8,000 content check at the hospital goods-receipt desk

**Track:** Bio & Health. **Score 3.70 — proof 4, market 3, feasibility 4, gap 4, deep-tech 4, low-risk 3.** Safe build, moderate ceiling; the bio team's strongest sellable problem.

**The problem.** India's anti-counterfeit layer checks the label, and labels are copyable. The Schedule H2 QR mandate (top-300 brands from Aug 2023; all vaccines, anti-cancer and narcotic drugs from Jul 2027; antimicrobials from Jul 2028) has never had its compliance published, and a fake Levipil pack has scanned as genuine. Meanwhile the 2026 rackets sit exactly where a label check fails: Delhi, Aug 2026 — 588 anti-cancer vials (Darzalex, Keytruda, Imfinzi), about ₹9 cr, 17 arrested, batch numbers wiped with acetone and refilled into original cartons, part of it diverted genuine stock; Bengaluru, Sep 2026 — counterfeit cancer drugs supplied to 90+ hospitals at ~50 % discount, ₹5 cr seized; Delhi, May 2026 — a ₹10 cr ring. The honest base rate is the regulator's own: 245 spurious or adulterated samples of 1,16,323 tested in FY25 (0.21 %), 282 of 1,06,150 in FY24 (0.27 %), 0.0245 % in the 2014–16 random survey; "not of standard quality" (mostly dissolution and assay failures) runs about 3 % — 330 NSQ alerts in September 2026 alone. The 25–28 % "fake share" figures (ASSOCHAM 2015, unsourced; ASPA-CRISIL 2026, a 1,639-person perception survey by the authentication-label trade body) are not to be believed. Counterfeits are rare in random retail stock and concentrated in high-value oncology and specialty injectables bought on discount.

**Public proof (Tier A).** LocalCircles, Sep 2026 (1,06,000 responses, 327 districts): 15 % say they were sold counterfeit medicine in the last 12 months; 64 % say chemists never or rarely asked for a prescription. The Bengal chemists' association (40,000+ members) says counterfeit-plus-substandard stock rose from ~10 % to ~15 % and that any discount beyond the 16–20 % retail margin "should be treated with utmost suspicion" — the trade body is the complainant. YouTube "how to identify" demand: 79 k, 48 k, 22 k (the Bengaluru racket), several 10 k+ Shorts; r/india threads at 46 and 39 upvotes (modest). CDSCO monthly NSQ alerts and the FY24/FY25 spurious counts are the hard numbers.

**Who pays.** Not the average chemist (12.4 lakh AIOCD members including distributors; a chemist profiting from discount stock has no reason to buy a checker). The buyers with liability: hospital procurement after the Bengaluru case, oncology and specialty pharmacies, organised chains, pharma brand-protection teams, state drug-control field inspectors.

**Market.** TAM if every chemist subscribed: 12.4 lakh × ₹4,800 a year ≈ ₹595 cr a year + ₹992 cr of hardware — a ceiling, not a plan. SAM: ~50,000 hospital, oncology and chain outlets × ₹12,000 a year ≈ ₹60 cr a year. Value at risk on a ₹2,45,943 cr pharma market (MAT Mar 2026): spurious at 0.21 % ≈ ₹516 cr a year; NSQ at 3.16 % ≈ ₹7,770 cr a year.

**Who has tried.** Serialisation and scratch codes (PharmaSecure, $3.9 M in 2011, 3 billion+ packs; Sproxil India, 2011, status unknown) — label-level, brand pays, copyable. Handheld spectrometers — Raman at $52–69 k, a cheap NIR at $1,539 (the only published price table, 2017); no Indian brand-spectra library, no state purchase found. GPHF Minilab (thin-layer chromatography; skilled, slow). Paper analytical devices (Notre Dame). The decisive evidence: a 2021 PLoS NTD evaluation of twelve portable devices found **91.5–100 % sensitivity for no-API or wrong-API fakes across all twelve** (including a $1,539 NIR and a paper card), but only 5.6–50 % for 50–80 %-API substandard samples on IR and Raman; a 2020 Applied Spectroscopy study found every device except the silicon-photodiode NIR class (SCiO) built regression models under 6 % error. There is no peer-reviewed study of AS7265x-class 18-channel sensors on tablets; expect them to separate gross fakes (wrong excipient, coating, dye, no API) and fail on API quantity.

**Solution designs.** (1) **Pick: a spectral fingerprint reader with a phone pack-check.** AS7343 or AS7341 (14/11 channels, in stock at ₹1,600–1,900; AS7265x is ₹7,500 and on backorder; Hamamatsu C12880MA as the upgrade) + ESP32 + white/NIR LEDs in a 3D-printed dark chamber with a fixed tablet seat; a per-brand reference library and a one-class "matches brand X batch spectra or not" model; a phone macro photo of foil print, batch-code font and erasure marks. First customer: goods-receipt at a mid-size private hospital's pharmacy, oncology first. BOM ₹6–8 k, sell ₹15 k + ₹1,000 a month per site. Moat: the Indian brand-and-batch spectra library, which grows with every scan. (2) **The bio module:** a paper-card colour spot test for the top 20 molecules plus a 15-minute "poor man's dissolution" (tablet in 0.1 N HCl at 37 °C, turbidity/colour read by the same sensor at fixed times) — the part that targets what most NSQ failures actually are. (3) Pack forensics alone — cheapest, fails against refilled genuine cartons; do not lead with it.

**Overnight plan (R3-build-02).** BOM ≈ ₹7,900 (₹15,400 if the AS7265x arrives). Library: 8–10 OTC products bought at retail — two or three paracetamol brands, cetirizine, pantoprazole, a calcium tablet, vitamin C (ibuprofen dropped: Schedule H); five tablets each, ten reads per tablet; "fakes" are wrong-API surrogates (calcium carbonate or sugar tablets of similar size and colour, one brand passed off as another, a thin coat of a different colour, a re-pressed 50 %-starch paracetamol if the bio team has a press). Report held-out sensitivity and specificity on 100–200 reads never seen in training, a brand-versus-brand confusion matrix, time per scan, and where it fails (the 50 %-API sample). The claim is "wrong product / no API", never "detects counterfeits". Biggest risk: most OTC tablets are white and a 350–1,000 nm sensor may not separate two paracetamol brands — run a bench test before 11 Nov and, if it fails, lead with the colour-and-dissolution module. Buildability 4/5.

**Gates.** Under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 only a Government Analyst's report establishes "spurious" or "NSQ", so the device is triage ("hold and send to lab"), never a verdict; whether a drug-screening device falls under the Medical Devices Rules 2017 is NOT VERIFIED; keep controlled drugs out of the library; false negatives create hospital liability, false positives anger brand owners.

**Pitch.** "Every Indian anti-counterfeit scheme checks the label and labels are copyable; published lab evidence says even a $1,500 optical device catches wrong-API fakes at 91–100 % — exactly the oncology-ring failure (antifungals sold as chemo, refilled vials) — and a ₹8,000 content check at hospital goods-receipt, backed by the first Indian brand-spectra library, closes the gap the QR mandate leaves." ₹2 lakh over three months: a library of 50 high-value brands with one hospital pharmacy, and a pre-competition bench answer to the white-tablet question.

**Corrections from the claims check.** The honest base rate is 0.21 % (FY25), not "under 0.05 %"; handhelds are not uniformly "lakh-priced" (a $1,539 NIR was in the 91.5–100 % group); part of the Delhi ring was diverted genuine stock; the "64 %" is chemists never or rarely asking, not patients buying without one; Round 2's "2018 survey, 11 spurious" and the "WHO 3 %" figure could not be traced — do not cite them.

### 3.6 Smart-meter witness: a signed, calibrated log that explains the bill and triggers the check meter the law already owes you

**Track:** Frontier Hardware (also AI & Systems). **Score 3.70 — proof 5, market 2, feasibility 5, gap 3, deep-tech 3, low-risk 3.** Easiest build and loudest proof; thinnest market.

**The problem.** 7.24 crore smart meters were installed by 30 Jun 2026 (5.73 crore under RDSS, which sanctions 19.79 crore consumer meters by March 2028). Maharashtra logged 3.92 lakh billing complaints on 1.23 crore smart meters — 3.2 % — by July 2026; UP has 78 lakh meters (70 lakh prepaid until the state ended prepaid on 8 May 2026 after villagers threw meters on the road in Jalaun and a Banda groom led his wedding procession to the DM's office); Himachal, MP and Assam have the same stories. The consumer has no instrument of their own, and the discom's answer is the discom's data. The claims check found something important: every regulator or discom test on record (APERC 2 Feb 2026, MSEDCL's "Accu-Check", an Andaman CGRF order, the Orissa High Court in 2014) found the meters accurate and blamed capacitors, catch-up after slow old meters, estimated bills or earth leakage. The pain is not "the meter is wrong"; it is "nobody can explain why my bill rose, and I cannot check".

**Public proof (Tier A).** Hindi YouTube: "Kya Smart Meter Tez Bhagta Hai" — a home test of whether the meter runs fast — 2.86 M views, 553 comments (Jun 2026); Sanskriti PCS's UP ground report 1.35 M; Viral Khan Sir 1.05 M; NewsHooks 930 k; Zee UP 693 k; SolarSquare, a US$53 M-funded solar company, now uses smart-meter bill shock as a sales funnel (382 k). The conversation lives on Hindi YouTube, not Reddit. Hard numbers: the Maharashtra Assembly reply (3 Jul 2026); UPERC's suo motu case closed on 27 Aug 2026 after ₹32.74 cr was credited to 92,231 accounts — for meter cost over-recovered on new connections (₹6,016 charged against ₹2,800 approved), not for mis-recording; Thane's MSEDCL is fitting old meters as check meters beside 20–25 % of new smart meters.

**Who pays.** Today, nobody: the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Amendment Rules 2024 oblige the licensee to install a check meter within five days of a complaint for at least three months, free, and a NABL bench test costs ₹450. So the product must win on trust, explanation and workflow, not existence. Buyers: households through RWAs (a Noida RWA federation head says "hundreds" queue at discom offices daily), consumer councils such as UPRVUP, and possibly discoms needing trust audits.

**Market.** Dispute-driven: 3.2 % × 7.24 crore = 23.2 lakh complaining households × ₹2,500 = ₹580 cr one-time (₹1,583 cr at full RDSS); SAM in UP and Maharashtra, where the disputes are loudest: ~6.4 lakh households = ₹160 cr; or a ₹500-per-quarter rental through RWAs matching the statutory three-month window. Comparators: SustLabs Ohm Assistant at ₹6,680 (an IIT Bombay spin-out with ~US$1.5 M raised, figure contested), Tuya Wi-Fi CT meters at ₹3,200–5,500, a DIY PZEM module at ₹400–600. The hardware is a commodity; the gap is calibration, tamper-evidence and the complaint pack.

**Who has tried.** Discom check meters (statutory, distrusted); NABL labs (a snapshot, no explanation); discom apps (same data as the dispute); SustLabs (appliance disaggregation, no evidence-grade log, no dispute workflow); Prayas's ESMI (400 voltage monitors in 23 states, 2015–18, never found a paying buyer — the outage ledger stays a free feature); SolarSquare circling "home energy management".

**Solution designs.** (1) A **witness check meter**: ESP32 + class-1-capable metering IC (ATM90E26/ADE7953) + split-core CT + plug-in voltage reference + RTC + ATECC608 secure element signing a hash-chained minute log of kWh, V, I, power factor and outages; it auto-builds a side-by-side "my witness vs your meter" pack for the discom and then the CGRF. (2) **Pick: the bill explainer built on (1)** — add a core-balance CT on phase + neutral to catch earth leakage (the cause a Nagpur expert blamed, and a shock hazard) and on-device NILM that labels AC, geyser, EV and pump loads, so most disputes end with "the meter is right, here is why" — which is still worth paying for. (3) Discom trust audits: randomised independent loggers for SERCs to settle complaint clusters — long sales cycle, conflicts with the discoms' position.

**Overnight plan (R3-build-01).** BOM ≈ ₹6,600 delivered: PZEM-004T in a sealed box, a plug-in reference meter, ATECC608, a 12 V leakage rig. Tests: kWh error against the reference across five loads (kettle, iron, fan, LED, laptop charger as the non-linear load), 20–30 minutes each, target ≤2 %; 20 power-strip outage cycles (detection rate, timestamp error); a tamper test (edit one of N records, verification must fail 100 %); NILM per-appliance F1 on held-out iAWE days (an IIIT-Delhi Indian-homes dataset). Ask the organisers by 7 Nov whether a sealed, electrician-wired mains box on an RCCB circuit is allowed in the hall; if not, the fallback is signed logs from a supervised lab run on 11–12 Nov, if pre-event results are permitted. Biggest risk: mains refused, which removes the live kWh-error number judges will ask for first. Buildability 4/5.

**Gates.** A consumer device is not a revenue meter under the CEA metering regulations; its log can trigger and steer the statutory test, not replace it; a court filing would need a Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam s.63 certificate; minute-level load data is personal under DPDP (local storage, deletion); the voltage tap at the distribution board needs a licensed electrician (the plug-in reference avoids it); WPC for the radio module.

**Pitch.** "Smart meters moved the trust problem from the meter reader to an algorithm the consumer cannot see; 3.2 % of Maharashtra's smart-meter homes have complained and 2.9 million people watched a home test of whether the meter runs fast — a calibrated, signed ₹2,000 witness that also explains the bill turns complaint rates into evidence either side can accept." ₹2 lakh over three months: 50 rentals through a Noida RWA federation and UPRVUP, and a measured answer to how many disputes end "meter right, here's why".

**Corrections from the claims check.** "UP refunding ₹200 cr to 5 lakh consumers" became ₹32.74 cr to 92,231 accounts, and it concerned meter price, not accuracy; "52.8 million meters" is superseded by 7.24 crore, which includes transformer and feeder meters; the "20.17 lakh complaints" are all electricity complaints to UP discoms in July 2026, not smart-meter-specific; no order relying on consumer-side logged data, and no UP expert-committee finding on accuracy, could be found.

### 3.7 GNSS integrity node: the sensor network that turns pilots' spoofing reports into evidence

**Track:** Space Tech & Defence. **Score 3.50 — proof 4, market 2, feasibility 4, gap 4, deep-tech 5, low-risk 2.** High-ceiling build; the strongest pure deep-tech demo and the thinnest civil market. Kept as the Space-track pick on judgement.

**The problem.** Indian airlines reported 2,354 GNSS interference and spoofing incidents between Nov 2023 and Dec 2025 (Lok Sabha, 12 Mar 2026), 403 of them in December 2025 alone, and 623 more in Delhi airspace in January–February 2026 — about 300 a month within 60 nm of Delhi — plus events at Mumbai, Kolkata, Amritsar, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai and Jammu. Spoofing hit GPS-based approaches to Delhi's runway 10 (Rajya Sabha, 1 Dec 2025); DGCA now requires pilots and controllers to report within ten minutes (SOP of 10 Nov 2025); AAI forwards reports to DoT's Wireless Monitoring Organisation to find the source. There is no ground sensor anywhere in the chain: reporting is pilot memory, no AAI, DIAL or DoT tender for monitoring could be found, and NavIC is down to three operational satellites. IATA names India a new hotspot as the global GPS-loss rate rose from 31 to 59 per 1,000 flights (2022–25). A June 2026 probe of a cross-border spoofing network is reported but unconfirmed.

**Public proof (Tier A on attention).** YouTube: StudyIQ 330 k, airline-pilot YouTuber Flying Beast 262 k (the one practitioner voice), Unacademy on Kolkata 234 k, Drishti IAS 33 k, India Today 20 k; four parliamentary replies on different dates; two international bodies. Caveat: those who suffer (pilots, airlines, ATC) speak through institutions, not posts. Three Round 1 lines are dropped after the claims check: "800 flights delayed" (that was an ATC message-system failure), "465 → 2,354 in two years" (border-only versus all-India, not like-for-like), and ">10 % of Delhi flights" (untraceable).

**Who pays.** AAI (a central PSU) and private airport operators such as DIAL; later ports, telecom, power and banking timing sites that depend on GNSS, and defence. Nobody pays today.

**Market.** 164 operational airports; 8 named. A node at ₹2.5 lakh + ₹60 k a year, eight per airport: TAM ₹33 cr + ₹8 cr a year (≈₹200 cr at the price of an incumbent system — Madrid's DYLEMA, nine stations, "millions of euros"); SAM for the eight named airports ≈ ₹1.6 cr + ₹38 lakh a year. The venture case needs defence, timing infrastructure or export; as a civil domestic business it is a niche.

**Who has tried.** DGCA circulars and the SOP; AAI → WMO source-finding; Accord Software's Navika-1100 (interference mitigation inside one receiver), ISRO SAC's NavIC monitoring receiver, IIT Jodhpur's anti-jam receiver project, HAL's EoI for military anti-jam GNSS — receivers, not a monitoring network; GPSPatron (Warsaw), Enaire's DYLEMA, Septentrio and u-blox integrity features abroad; GPSJam's free ADS-B map (aircraft-only, daily, cannot tell spoofing from jamming). No Indian civil GNSS-integrity network startup was found.

**Solution designs.** (1) **Pick: a "GNSS weather station" network.** The Stanford method (arXiv 2509.13600, 2026) on a sub-$400 receiver: a two-dimensional detector combining SBAS (GAGAN) C/N0 — very stable for geostationary satellites — with a calibrated received-power metric from u-blox's MON-SPAN spectrum, auto-calibrated from ~30 minutes of clean data; cross-receiver voting to localise; a network-time check for clock jumps; auto-generated reports in the DGCA ten-minute format. Benchmark to state openly: on Jammertest 2024 the method flagged interference with ≥99.5 % accuracy and classified jamming at ~95–99 %, but classified spoofing (meaconing) at only 53–67 % with SBAS and near 0 % with GPS signals — cheap receivers detect interference well and spoofing poorly. (2) An India-specific ADS-B integrity map (software only, receive-only 1090 MHz dongles). (3) Drone-side spoofing detection with a visual-inertial fallback, licensed to Indian drone makers.

**Overnight plan (R3-build-02).** BOM ≈ ₹5,000: two u-blox MAX-M10S boards (₹1,775 each, in stock) with ₹447 active antennas — the M10 interface document carries MON-SPAN, MON-RF, NAV-SAT and a spoofing-state field, every message the detector needs; the F9P upgrade adds ~₹27 k and an imported multiband antenna with high delivery risk; the RTL-SDR V4 is end-of-life and out of stock, and MON-SPAN gives a rough spectrum, so the SDR is dropped. TEXBAT raw files are 39–48 GB each (not pullable in the window), but UT Austin also publishes processed observables (~5 MB each: cleanStatic, ds2, ds3, ds7; in ds7 the spoofer injects at 110 s and the clock push starts at 150 s), which makes the spoofing-latency result near-certain; GNSS-SDR installs with `apt`. Results: false alarms over ~10 hours of live nominal rooftop data; detection latency per TEXBAT scenario; separation of foil blockage from synthetic (digital, never radiated) jamming. Biggest risk: no open-sky mounting — get rooftop permission before the event. Buildability 3/5.

**Gates.** Receive-only hardware only (a GNSS module and, if used, an RTL-SDR; never a HackRF or Pluto, which can transmit); the Telecommunications (Radio Equipment Possession Authorisation) Rules 2026 (8 Jul 2026) require authorisation to possess radio equipment unless a Rule 10 exemption applies — the exemption text for GNSS receivers could not be read, so the ₹1,000 "testing or demonstration" authorisation is the safe route; transmitting anything GNSS-like is illegal; TEXBAT is research-only (a business gate); airside security clearance and government procurement.

**Pitch.** "India logs about 300 GNSS interference reports a month around Delhi alone and has no sensor that records the radio; a $400-class receiver flags over 99 % of interference events; a dense, cheap network turns pilot reports into localised, time-stamped evidence for AAI and the Wireless Monitoring Organisation at a fraction of a nine-station European system." ₹2 lakh over three months: three nodes at Ashoka and Sonipat plus one on a DIAL or AAI rooftop, a 90-day event archive, and a calibrated GAGAN C/N0 baseline nobody has published.

**Corrections from the claims check.** The growth line, the "800 flights" and the ">10 %" are dropped; "623" is Jan–Feb 2026 (the IANS text is inconsistent); a mid-2025 AAI talk put Delhi at only ~2 % of reports (snippet); the DW Hindi video pre-dates the Delhi events and is not India-specific; "WMO" is DoT's Wireless Monitoring Organisation, not the weather body; the Stanford numbers hold.

### 3.8 EV charging-bay monitor: watch the plug, the pack and the air so societies can say yes to chargers

**Track:** Frontier Hardware & Climate. **Score 3.40 — proof 4, market 3, feasibility 3, gap 4, deep-tech 4, low-risk 2.** The most unsolved problem in the energy set and the most predictable build; the weakest proof and a buyer that depends on rules not yet written. Advanced on judgement over two higher-scoring near-misses.

**The problem.** India registered 14,01,861 electric two-wheelers in FY26 (+21.8 %) and 15.61 lakh in Jan–Sep 2026 (+62 %; 2.03 lakh in September alone). They charge in basements, stilts and bedrooms on ordinary sockets, and nobody watches a bay at 3 am. Karnataka's Council was told of 83 EV fires from 2020 to Nov 2024, rising 7 / 3 / 9 / 28 / 36 a year, 65 of them attributed to "power leakage" (undefined; the tally includes 11 showroom fires and all EV types). Noida, Jul 2026: a scooter charging on an ordinary socket shared with a water pump in a PG building — two dead, 50–150 rescued; Indore, Mar 2026: eight dead in a house fire after an electric car on charge (worsened by LPG cylinders and a digital lock). Societies respond by banning: DLF's Ultima in Gurugram removed 75 basement chargers after a fire-NOC issue; others fine ₹25,000 for charging from a domestic connection; a Nirala Estate resident's PIL to enforce the Power Ministry's society-charging guidelines is pending in the Supreme Court (notice Feb 2026). Haryana's building code (amended 5 Jun 2026) now requires one charger per five slots in group housing, with Fire Department and discom certification, and its fire rules are being drafted; BIS has proposed restricting basement charging; Maharashtra reportedly bans it. **No jurisdiction yet mandates monitoring.**

**Public proof (Tier A, the weakest).** YouTube: "EV scooter problems nobody will tell you" 912 k; BBC Hindi on why e-scooters catch fire (2022) 246 k; a 1 Nov 2026 e-bike fire explainer 136 k; battery-life anxiety 92 k; The Hindu on Indore 64 k; CNBC on Noida 6 k; the Supreme Court society-charger case 22 k. The apartment-charging impasse is better evidenced than the fires themselves. Reddit: two of three threads live, low engagement.

**Who pays.** RWAs — if and when a fire department names monitoring as a condition for restoring chargers; charging operators; OEMs. Insurers are not buyers yet (EV fire claims "in single digits", disputes turn on electrical-fault exclusions).

**Market.** 1.96 lakh cooperative housing societies (plus RWAs under other laws, uncounted) × 10 bays × ₹6,000 = ₹1,176 cr one-time (TAM); SAM: 20 % urban gated societies with basement or stilt parking ≈ 39,200 × 10 × ₹6,000 = ₹235 cr + ₹47 cr a year of monitoring. A used-battery state-of-health certificate (no national standard exists) is an unsized second line.

**Who has tried.** Bolt.Earth smart sockets (₹4,999–5,499; auto cut-off on voltage; no plug temperature, arc, pack or gas sensing — and the claims check found its page does not list the MCB/ARAI claims Round 2 repeated); OEM battery management under AIS-156 Amendment 3 (in-pack sensors, thermal-propagation test, an audio-visual warning that goes to a rider who is asleep — "the rules cannot follow the scooter home"); MakerMax (pack pressure/temperature/gas sensors, 2022; no trace since); iRasus, Flowatt (fleet analytics with BMS access); Honeywell's Li-ion Tamer (off-gas detection for enclosed battery racks; the 6.4-minute warning is vendor-reported). No open-bay early-warning test exists; the nearest is NIST's March 2026 indoor scooter test, where 76 % of smoke-alarm activations came before flaming. Nobody watches a residential bay.

**Solution designs.** (1) **Pick: a bay safety monitor** — a smart socket with high-frequency current-signature analysis for arcing and loose contacts, a thermistor at the plug terminals, an overhead IR thermopile array over the parked scooter's battery zone, VOC/CO/H2 gas sensors, a relay cut-off, a local siren plus guard-room and app alerts, and a signed event log for the fire NOC. The Karnataka cause split says the socket and cable side may prevent more fires than gas sensing; the strongest physical claim is that a loose contact overheats at normal current, so breakers and RCDs never trip. First customer: a Gurugram or Noida society that removed chargers — "restore chargers under monitoring". BOM ₹2,500–3,500, ₹6,000 + ₹100 a month per bay. (2) Charging-curve health on the socket's own data (energy per full charge, CV-tail shape, standby draw) → a battery-health report for resale. (3) Electrolyte-vapour sensing characterised by the wet-lab team (DMC/EMC vapours versus perfume, petrol, cooking confounders) — after the event, not in the hall.

**Overnight plan (R3-build-02).** BOM ≈ ₹9,860 with the AMG8833 IR array (the MLX90640 is ₹8,731 and in stock only at zbotic; MLX90614 at ₹709 is the single-pixel fallback). A 12–24 V rig only, no mains: a deliberately loosened screw terminal carrying 5–10 A (contact-resistance heating), an aluminium dummy pack with a 50 W heater pad and thermistors (black tape so the IR sensor reads it), BME688/SGP30 gas sensor with a few drops of isopropyl alcohol as the venting stand-in, a relay, a siren, the app and the signed log; one 18650 charged within spec by a TP4056 in a fire-safe bag, attended, for real CC–CV curves; SoH estimation trained on NASA PCoE / CALCE public data. Safety rules: enclosed adapters only, a hardware thermostat cut at 80 °C, no abuse of any lithium cell. Results: seconds from fault onset to alarm and to relay cut; loose-contact temperature rise and time-to-flag; false alarms over hours of normal cycling; IPA detection distance and response time; SoH MAE on held-out cells. Biggest risk: organisers refusing the heater, the IPA or the cell, which shrinks the demo to the loose-terminal test plus dataset-only battery health. Buildability 4/5; the ceiling is capped because every result is a stand-in for real thermal runaway, and the report must say so.

**Gates.** Socket, RCD and EV-charging standards (IS 1293 / IS 12640 / IS 17017; which are compulsory under BIS CRS is not verified); state fire-department and discom certification for chargers in allotted bays (Haryana); whether a monitor can substitute for sprinklers or compartmentation is undecided; product liability under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 — wording "monitoring", never "fire prevention", and insurance; DPDP minimal.

**Pitch.** "India sold 14 lakh electric two-wheelers last year and societies are pulling chargers because nobody watches a bay at 3 am — a ₹6,000 monitor that sees the plug, the pack and the air, cuts power and keeps a log a fire officer accepts converts a fire-NOC 'no' into a monitored 'yes'." ₹2 lakh over three months: ten bays in one Gurugram society that removed chargers, a 90-day near-miss log, and a conversation with Haryana Fire & Emergency Services while its rules are still being written.

**Corrections from the claims check.** Noida was a PG, not a society, and the scooter was on an ordinary socket; Indore was an electric car; Karnataka's series starts in 2020 (7 fires), includes showrooms and all EV types, and "power leakage" is undefined; the Palam fire's cause is unconfirmed; Bolt.Earth's safety claims were overstated; no jurisdiction mandates monitoring and no open-bay lead-time data exists.
