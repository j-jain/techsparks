# D7-03 Sequoia and Peak XV: companies solving the adaptive-care problems (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-16)

Scope: companies backed by Sequoia Capital (US/Europe funds, including Sequoia Capital Global Equities) and, in a separate section, by Peak XV Partners (formerly Sequoia Capital India & SE Asia, renamed 2023; deals done as "Sequoia India" are counted as Peak XV). Each company is mapped to one or more of the four threads: A (adaptive chronic care between visits), B (high-stakes dosing where specialists are rare), C (low-cost vitals and remote monitoring), D (smart ICU and hospital deterioration). Deals announced since Jan 2025 come first; older companies are kept only if still active and relevant. HongShan (ex-Sequoia China) was not covered. Each fact has an ID (D7-03-Fnn), a URL and a tag: OPENED (I read the page) or SNIPPET (search summary only). Earlier IDs (D2-06, D6-02, D1-04, R4-05) are reused and were not re-opened. Company numbers are THEIR CLAIM. Sequoia article pages carry a wrong metadata date (I saw "Nov 13, 2026"); I used the date in the article body. Backing was confirmed from a Sequoia or Peak XV page, a company release, or named-investor press coverage; where only an aggregator lists the fund, the entry says so. "Borrow / do differently" lines are OUR VIEW.

## Digest

1. **Virta Health is the clearest Sequoia company for thread A.** Patients send home readings (scale, finger-stick glucose and ketone meter, BP cuff). A remote physician or nurse practitioner changes medicines from those readings, with "rapid downward titration" of insulin and sulfonylureas. In the 1-year trial, insulin was reduced or stopped in 94% of users (non-randomised, Virta-funded). Sequoia Capital Global Equities led the $65M Series D (Dec 2020). Virta puts "100% of its fees at risk" with payers. D7-03-F01, F02, F03.
2. **Virta is also becoming the GLP-1 gatekeeper for at least one US state plan.** Maine's state employee plan requires members on GLP-1s for weight loss to enrol with Virta from 1 Jul 2026 (SNIPPET). Continuing the drug needs shared decision-making. D7-03-F05.
3. **The nearest Sequoia company to thread B is Athelas (now part of Commure), and it is a test, not a dosing engine.** Athelas Home is an FDA-cleared, CLIA-waived finger-stick analyser. It gives WBC and neutrophil % (and so ANC) in minutes for people on clozapine, chemotherapy or immunosuppressants. It was cleared for point-of-care use on 12 Feb 2026. Claims: 40,000+ patients and 1,000,000+ tests. Sequoia has backed the company since 2016 and joined Commure's $70M round at $7B (19 May 2026). D7-03-F11 to F14.
4. **HealthLeap is the newest Sequoia hospital-deterioration deal (thread D).** Sequoia co-led the $8M seed, announced with a $30M Series A on 7 Oct 2026. Each night it reads every inpatient's EHR: notes, nursing assessments, labs, vitals trends and medicine orders. Each morning it gives a ranked risk list to clinicians and dietitians. It starts with malnutrition; heart failure, pressure ulcers and hospital infections including sepsis are next. It is paid for by reimbursement it recovers, about $11,000 per qualifying stay (THEIR CLAIM). D7-03-F25 to F28.
5. **Bunkerhill Health (thread D):** Sequoia backed the seed and joined the $25M Series B (16 Jul 2026, Khosla led). A hospital brings a problem and Bunkerhill builds a background agent that flags missed cases; clinicians act on them. Claims: 15 health systems, 22 agents at UTMB, 9 FDA-cleared algorithms. D7-03-F29, F30.
6. **OpenEvidence** (Sequoia led the $75M Series A, Feb 2025) is the doctors' question-and-answer layer. It is free to clinicians, paid for by ads, and draws on licensed journals. Claims: more than 40% of US physicians use it, and annualised revenue passed $100M. It is NPI-gated, and it answers questions rather than managing anyone's dose. A $15B round in Sep 2026 is SNIPPET only. D7-03-F18, F20, F21, F22.
7. **Abby Care** (Sequoia an existing investor in its $82M Series C, Oct 2026) trains family members as accredited, paid home caregivers. RN care managers review biometrics and step in before ED visits. It is the Sequoia model closest to an ASHA-plus-family care loop. D7-03-F08, F09.
8. **Peak XV's only smart-ICU company is still Cloudphysician.** Peak XV led its $10.5M Series A (Jun 2024). I found no 2025-26 round. Peak XV's 2025-26 health deals are clinics, a pharmacy, clinical data and a consumer AI assistant (Hoola, Truemeds, BioStack, August AI), not care loops. D7-03-F48, F49, F64, F66 to F68.
9. **Twin Health (Peak XV, thread A).** It builds a "digital twin" from wearable sensors and labs, with a licensed clinical team, and is paid only when members hit milestones. Peak XV joined the $50M Series D (Dec 2023). A Form D shows $59M sold from Jun to Sep 2026; the lead was not disclosed (SNIPPET). Twin runs a Chennai operation and Indian trials. D7-03-F50 to F53; D1-04-F01 to F03.
10. **Biofourmis (Sequoia India led the $35M Series B in 2019) built the only titration product found in either portfolio.** BiovitalsHF takes wearable data, symptoms and labs and recommends heart-failure drug starts and up-titrations to clinicians. It got FDA Breakthrough designation in 2021; clearance was not found. Biofourmis merged into CopilotIQ in Oct 2024, and Peak XV was not named in the new money. D7-03-F54 to F57.
11. **Many brief candidates are not Sequoia companies.** Cadence (Spark), Ambience (Oak HC/FT and a16z), Nabla (HV Capital), Tennr (IVP), Assort (Lightspeed), Viz.ai (Tiger and Insight), Hims & Hers and Eko Health all have other backers. Dozee, Tricog, Even and HealthifyMe are not Peak XV companies. Eko Health's apparent Peak XV link is to eko.ai, the former name of Us2.ai. D7-03-F39 to F45, F69 to F72.
12. **Gap: neither fund backs remote dose management for warfarin/INR, hydroxyurea, epilepsy drugs, transplant immunosuppressants or oral chemotherapy.** Neither backs a cheap shared vitals station or contactless sensor either (Dozee is not Peak XV). Sequoia's 2025-26 health money goes to US and German hospital software and clinician knowledge. See Gaps.

## Sequoia companies

### A. Adaptive chronic care between visits

### Virta Health (US): remote, physician-led diabetes and obesity care that takes people off drugs
- **What it is:** a virtual medical clinic that treats type 2 diabetes and obesity with nutrition (carbohydrate restriction) and remote medicine changes, aiming to reduce or stop insulin, other diabetes drugs and GLP-1s.
- **Solution, concretely:** in the published trial, patients got a cellular-connected scale, a finger-stick glucose and ketone (BHB) meter, and a BP cuff if hypertensive. They logged weight and BHB daily and glucose 1-3 times a day in a web app. A remote health coach adjusted carbohydrate intake to keep BHB at 0.5-3.0 mmol/L. A remote medical provider (physician or NP) "made medication changes as indicated by the participant-reported biomarkers". The authors credit "rapid downward titration of insulin and sulfonylurea" for having no hypoglycaemia that needed assistance. The detailed protocol is in a supplement I did not read. D7-03-F03 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6104272/ | 2018 | OPENED. A Feb 2026 Virta page says deprescribing should be medically supervised, uses HbA1c as a criterion, and allows medicines to be restarted if labs worsen; it gives no thresholds. D7-03-F04 | https://www.virtahealth.com/blog/what-is-deprescribing | 2026-02-18 (updated 2026-07-21) | OPENED.
- **Who confirms:** a clinician (physician or NP) makes every drug change; the coach handles diet. Clinician-in-loop, protocol-guided.
- **How sold:** to employers, health plans and government payers (more than 100 payers in 2020). For enterprises Virta "puts 100% of its fees at risk". D7-03-F02 | same URL as F01 | OPENED.
- **Sequoia round:** $65M Series D led by Sequoia Capital Global Equities, with Caffeinated Capital, valuation above $1.1B, 2 Dec 2020. D7-03-F01 | https://www.virtahealth.com/blog/virta-health-raises-65-million-series-d-to-scale-type-2-diabetes-reversal-treatments | 2020-12-02 | OPENED. A tracker lists a Dec 2025 $30M "Series F" with Tiger Global, Sequoia and Caffeinated; I could not confirm it. D7-03-F07 | https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/remote-patient-monitoring-top-startups-fundraising | SNIPPET.
- **Traction:** trial results, intention to treat: HbA1c 7.6% to 6.3%; insulin reduced or stopped in 94% of users (40% of completers stopped insulin; the rest went from 105 to 54 units a day); sulfonylureas stopped; weight down 13.8 kg. It was non-randomised (262 vs 87 usual care) and Virta-funded (F03). 2020 claims: 60% "reversal" at one year, more than 70% lower prescription costs in year 1 (THEIR CLAIM, F02). GLP-1 claims: GLP-1 use halved among current takers (early results), and a 154-patient matched cohort found no weight or HbA1c difference after stopping GLP-1 (THEIR CLAIM). D7-03-F06 | https://www.virtahealth.com/press/virta-health-announces-first-of-its-kind-peer-reviewed-study-proving-its-approach-is-an-effective-off-ramp-from-glp-1s-for-sustained-weight-loss | SNIPPET.
- **2026 signal:** Maine's state employee plan requires members with GLP-1 prescriptions for weight loss to enrol with Virta and get the prescription from a Virta provider, effective 1 Jul 2026 (deadline 30 Oct 2026). Members can stay on the drug if patient, provider and Virta agree. D7-03-F05 | https://www.maine.gov/bhr/oeh/virtahealth | SNIPPET (page returned 403).
- **Maps to:** A (depth 4: medicines change from home readings) and B (insulin and sulfonylurea down-titration).
- **India:** none found. The Indian programmes in D1-04 (Twin, Sugar.fit, BeatO, Fitterfly) do not publish an algorithmic dose rule (D1-04 digest point 3).
- **Borrow / do differently:** borrow the pattern of daily readings, a clinician who changes doses, and "down-titrate fast to avoid hypos". This is the safest titration direction to demo, because insulin and sulfonylurea cuts lower hypo risk when diet changes. Do differently in India: ketone strips are expensive and a ketogenic diet clashes with rice and roti diets; use glucose plus weight only. The prescriber would be a medical officer working to a written protocol, not an NP, and payment cannot be "fees at risk" without a payer. A state NCD programme or PM-JAY wellness budget is the nearest equivalent.

### Abby Care (US): family members trained and paid as home caregivers, with nurse oversight
- **What it is:** a home-care company that screens, trains and certifies a patient's own family members as accredited caregivers under state Medicaid home- and community-based services (HCBS) rules.
- **Solution, concretely:** the family caregiver delivers daily care. Its software records care delivery in the home with an "immutable" audit trail, and automated checks catch false time reports. A dedicated RN care manager oversees each care plan, reviews "biometrics and status updates", and steps in when status worsens, before an ED visit. Sold to commercial, Medicare Advantage and Medicaid managed-care health plans. The article does not call the software AI. D7-03-F09 | https://hitconsultant.net/2026/10/19/abby-care-paid-family-caregiving/ | 2026-10-19 | OPENED.
- **Sequoia round:** $82M Series C led by Transformation Capital; new investors CVS Health Ventures, Frist Cressey and Pivotal; existing Khosla, Thrive and Sequoia Capital. D7-03-F08 | same URL | OPENED. Fortune dates it 15 Oct 2026, after a $35M raise in Aug 2025; reported 2025 revenue of $15M is unverified. D7-03-F10 | https://fortune.com/2026/10/15/startup-abby-care-raises-82-million-to-turn-family-members-into-paid-caregivers/ | SNIPPET.
- **Traction:** no patient, caregiver or outcome counts published (F09).
- **Maps to:** A and C (the care loop at home; the RN reviews readings). It does not titrate drugs.
- **India:** none found. Indian families already give most of the home care, unpaid.
- **Borrow / do differently:** borrow the idea of certifying the family member, giving them a simple logging app, and having one nurse watch many homes and escalate. Do differently: there is no Medicaid HCBS payer, so the nurse layer must come from a PHC/CHC nurse, an ASHA incentive line or an NGO. Keep paperwork light; the audit trail matters less than a missed-reading alert.

### B. High-stakes drug dosing where specialists are rare

### Commure, formerly Athelas (US): finger-stick blood-count monitoring for high-risk drugs, plus RPM, scribe and billing AI
- **What it is:** Commure is a health-system AI platform (ambient scribe, autonomous coding, revenue cycle). Athelas is its subsidiary and brings FDA-cleared AI diagnostics and remote monitoring. Sequoia's page lists the company as "FKA Athelas", partner Alfred Lin, partnered 2016. D7-03-F11 | https://sequoiacap.com/companies/commure | OPENED.
- **Solution, concretely (thread B):** Athelas Home is an AI hematology analyser. A finger-stick drop gives quantitative WBC and neutrophil %; ANC is calculated for neutrophil-monitoring workflows. Results come back "in minutes", at the point of care or at home. It is used for clozapine monitoring, oncology and infusion (chemotherapy), and immunosuppressive therapy. The page names psychiatrists, a PharmD and a PMHNP as users. How results reach the prescriber, pharmacy or REMS is not stated, and neither is the price. D7-03-F14 | https://www.commure.com/athelas-home | OPENED. The FDA cleared the CLIA-waived Athelas Home for point-of-care settings (ambulatory providers, pharmacies) on 12 Feb 2026. - **Solution, concretely (thread C):** Athelas RPM uses a SIM-connected BP cuff and weight scale; a nurse team reviews readings and escalates concerns; practices bill the US RPM codes. Do differently: device and strip cost must fit a PHC budget, and results should go by SMS or WhatsApp to a named medical officer, not into an EHR.

### D. Smart ICU and hospital deterioration

### HealthLeap (US): nightly AI screen of every inpatient for missed conditions
- **What it is:** an AI layer inside the hospital EHR that finds inpatients with under-diagnosed conditions. It started with malnutrition. Founded 2022 by Josiah Meyer (CEO) and Jemima Meyer (clinician).
- **Solution, concretely:** it takes in each patient's full EHR every day: progress notes, nursing assessments, lab panels, vitals trends, active medicine orders and problem lists. It screens the whole inpatient census each morning, ranks risk, and routes the list into the workflows of bedside clinicians and registered dietitians. Next targets: heart failure, pressure ulcers and hospital-acquired infections (sepsis, CAUTI, surgical-site). HIPAA, SOC 2. D7-03-F26 | https://hitconsultant.net/2026/10/07/healthleap-raises-38m-series-a-sequoia-first-round-clinical-ai-inpatient-screening/ | 2026-10-07 | OPENED. It flags patients for assessment and does not diagnose. The CEO also names delirium and aspiration pneumonia. D7-03-F28 | https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/strategy/healthleap-raises-38m-to-help-hospitals-find-patients-with-undiagnosed-conditions/ | SNIPPET.
- **Who confirms:** clinicians and dietitians assess each flagged patient and decide. Clinician-in-loop.
- **How paid:** effectively from recovered reimbursement. Severe malnutrition is a major comorbidity under the US MS-DRG rules, worth about $11,000 per qualifying stay (THEIR CLAIM). Pricing is not stated (F26, F27).
- **Sequoia round:** $38M total: an $8M seed co-led by Sequoia Capital and First Round Capital, then a $30M Series A led by Hummingbird Ventures, announced 7 Oct 2026. D7-03-F25 | same URL as F26 | OPENED.
- **Traction (THEIR CLAIM):** 39% more malnutrition cases identified and treated in one enterprise rollout; $11M extra reimbursement across one network; one Penn Medicine hospital about $23.8M a year from reimbursement and shorter stays. Hospital count not given. D7-03-F27 | same URL | OPENED.
- **Maps to:** D (hospital-wide early warning from records, not monitors).
- **India:** none found.
- **Borrow / do differently:** borrow the "morning census list". A once-a-day ranked list of who to see first suits a district hospital with one physician for 60 beds better than real-time alarms. Do differently: most district hospitals have no EHR, so the inputs must be a nurse's photographed vitals chart or a tablet form. The business case cannot be DRG upcoding, because PM-JAY pays fixed package rates; the pitch has to be deaths and transfers avoided.

## Gaps

- **No remote dosing for the high-stakes drugs in either portfolio.** Neither Sequoia nor Peak XV backs anything for warfarin/INR, hydroxyurea, antiepileptics, transplant immunosuppressants or oral chemotherapy dosing.   - Athelas Home supplies the safety lab value for clozapine and chemo, but has no dose rule (F13, F14).
  - Virta cuts insulin and sulfonylureas inside a diet programme (F03).
  - BiovitalsHF recommends heart-failure drug up-titration; it has Breakthrough designation, not clearance (F55).
  - OpenEvidence answers questions and never sees patient data (F18).
- **No low-cost shared vitals station or contactless sensor.** Sequoia has none. Peak XV's only wearable bet was Biofourmis (2019, now outside its new money). Dozee is not Peak XV (F69).
- **No camera or vision monitoring of beds at Sequoia.** In this family of funds, only Peak XV's Cloudphysician (NETRA) does it (D6-02-F01).
- **Sequoia's 2025-26 health money goes to rich-system software.** HealthLeap, Bunkerhill, Commure, Freed, Avelios and OpenEvidence assume an EHR and US or German payment rules (DRG recovery, RPM codes, ads to US doctors). None runs in India.
- **Peak XV's 2025-26 India health money goes to clinic chains, pharmacy, data and consumer AI** (Hoola, Truemeds, BioStack, August AI, Mosaic). Its last care-delivery-loop bet found was Cloudphysician in Jun 2024.
