Example night, times illustrative. Dosing based on the atropine trial, J Med Toxicol 2012

Say: Here is one night in a district ICU. A farmer who drank pesticide arrives at 2:10. The doctor on duty is not an ICU specialist. From 2:12 the system proposes each antidote dose from the written protocol, checking every five minutes and doubling the dose until the patient is stable. Each one is a routine step, so the on-site doctor approves it with one tap. The specialist is not called. At 3:40 the patient’s breathing slows. More doses can’t fix that: the patient may need a ventilator. The protocol marks this as a hard stop, so it goes to the specialist, who joins by video and has the patient moved to the nearest hospital with a free ventilator. By morning the doctor only signs notes the system has already written. One specialist call in the whole night. How to read the slide: - Top row: what the on-site doctor does. Every routine step is that doctor’s tap. - Orange card at 03:40: the one moment the written steps can’t handle. That is why the specialist is called. - Bottom row: the remote specialist. Dashed means idle. The only call is the orange card under 03:40. Notes: - EXAMPLE from the idea check, section 4. No real patient and no doses shown. The times are illustrative; only the 5-minute check comes from the trial. - The on-site doctor is a general duty medical officer, not an intensivist. The nurse gives each dose and taps in pupils and chest sounds from a checklist. - Dose stepping follows the trial regimen: atropine doubled every 5 minutes until signs of atropinisation, then an infusion. D6-03-F02 (one open-label trial, Bangladesh, 156 patients). - Hard stops in the poisoning pack: breathing failure and signs of atropine toxicity (idea check, section 5). Routine steps are the on-site doctor’s order; the specialist handles exceptions and the on-site doctor confirms (idea check, section 7). That is what makes 80+ patients per specialist possible. - Transfer: the system lists the nearest centres with a free ventilator, warns the chosen one with the full case and logs departure and arrival.
