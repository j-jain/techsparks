## Appendix D. Every player in detail

This appendix holds the detail behind chapter 2.4: who works in chronic-disease care in India and abroad, what each sells, how deep each goes, who failed and why, which companies abroad were funded in 2025-2026, and where nobody was found. It joins the old map of the players and the old funded-abroad tab, so each company is described once; a company named in more than one table points to the section that describes it.

How to read it:
- **The adaptive depth scale:** 0 = uses no patient data, 1 = shows data, 2 = reminders, 3 = coaching personalised from the patient's own data, 4 = changes the therapy itself.
- **Claims:** every traction figure (users, patients, outcomes a company reports about itself) is THEIR CLAIM, not checked against a trial. "(search summary only)" or * means the source was not opened. "YC mirror" means a secondary copy of the Y Combinator directory. A fund that "took part" put money into a round another investor led, a weaker signal than a fund that "led".
- **Dates:** Indian players as found on 13 Nov 2026; figures for companies abroad are for the quarter to 30 Sep 2026 unless dated.

### D7. Why companies died

- **Pear** (addiction and insomnia apps): that Medicare did not pay and half of revenue came from three payers could not be confirmed; bankrupt April 2023; assets sold for just over $6M against $130M of liabilities [D1-05-F20, D1-02-F42]. Its products had randomised trials [D1-02-F17]. A panel said it tried to change care and payment at once [D1-05-F21].
- **Akili** (ADHD game): $523,000 of prescription revenue in 2023, net loss $59.5M; sale agreed for about $34M in May 2024 [D1-02-F44]. Staff cuts conflict: 40% (search summary only) [D1-05-F22], or a third in Jan 2023 and 46% in Apr 2024 [D1-02-F44].
- **Better Therapeutics** (diabetes app): its trial showed HbA1c 0.39 points lower than a control app (Appendix B) [D1-02-F21]; expected no revenue in 2023 (revenue not reported), deficit $134.3M, weighed winding down in Mar 2024 [D1-02-F46].
- **Teva Digihaler**: US distribution and app support stopped 1 June 2024; the US notice gave no reason and a UK recall cited "commercial reasons" [D1-02-F41]; its trial's interval crossed 1 [D1-02-F02].
- **Propeller**: bought by ResMed for $225M in 2019. Sources conflict: still a ResMed company in 2023 [D1-05-F63]; an undated ResMed page says it "has ceased operations" [D1-02-F40].
- **Proteus** (ingestible sensor): bankrupt June 2020, reason NOT FOUND [D1-05-F56].
- **Babylon**: lost NHS contracts; $221M loss on about $1B revenue; UK business sold for just over $600,000 in 2023 [D1-05-F54].
- **Livongo**: bought by Teladoc for $18.5B in 2020 [D1-05-F7]; Teladoc booked a $13.4B goodwill write-down for the whole company in 2022 after its share price fell; its annual report does not name Livongo for the charge [D1-05-F53].
- **Onduo**: Sanofi said it "over-invested" (2019); Verily began phasing it out in 2024 [D1-05-F57].
- **In India:** PhableCare, Wellthy and ConnectedH (D3).

Common causes:
- No one paid for the app alone [D1-05-F20, D1-02-F44, D1-05-F23].
- Too few prescribers [D1-05-F21].
- Trials that payers did not accept as outcome proof [D1-05-F21].
- Sensors with no therapy change [D1-02-F41, D1-02-F02].
- Valuations ahead of revenue, then no cash [D1-05-F55, D1-04-F23].
- Drop-off: the median of 93 mental-health apps kept 3.3% of users at 30 days [D1-02-F26].

### D9. The closest relatives to our loop

These are the four funded companies nearest to our product: a home reading drives a protocol-based medicine change that a clinician approves. Plainly: the research found no funded startup in 2025 or 2026 whose funding page describes home blood-pressure dose steps by protocol, with or without a clinician signing. Cadence is the nearest: its funding page says only that its AI agents "support" medicine changes [D2-06-F1], but its own August 2026 page describes protocol-bound titration software that is in a US regulator's pilot and not cleared [D3-06-F31].

#### Cadence (US)

- **What.** Remote vital signs for older adults between visits; supervised AI agents flag risk and support medicine changes under the partner medical group's protocols; clinicians keep judgement calls; health systems pay. How far the AI decides alone is not stated [D2-06-F1].
- **Round.** $100M Series C, June 2026 (pages differ by a day, 23 or 24 Jun), led by Spark Capital; Thrive Capital, General Catalyst, Coatue, Duke Health and others took part [D2-06-F1]. No valuation is stated on the pages the claims check opened [D2-06-F1].
- **Regulator pilot.** On 13 Aug 2026 Cadence said its HypertensionOS, prescription software for clinician-supervised, protocol-bound initiation and titration of blood-pressure medicines, was selected for the FDA's TEMPO pilot; the company says this "does not constitute FDA approval or clearance" (THEIR CLAIM) [D3-06-F31].
- **Reports (THEIR CLAIM, Jun 2026).** 100,000+ active patients (older adults) [D2-06-F2, D1-05-F46]; 55% of alerts (vital-sign alerts in general, not medicine changes) resolved with no human adjustment [D2-06-F2]; blood-pressure control up 70% relative [D2-06-F2, D1-05-F46]; admissions down 27% [D2-06-F2]. It also claims $1,302 lower yearly cost per patient. Its claims on control and cost do not say who changes the medicine [D1-05-F46].
- **Independent view.** The US review body PHTI (Oct 2024) named Cadence among programmes where a team adjusts medicine, the only kind with clinically meaningful falls in systolic pressure (D6) [D1-05-F1].
- **India differs.** No Medicare-type payer; 79.9% of required posts in four specialities were unfilled at rural community health centres in March 2023 [D1-03-F16], so the approver is a general doctor or health worker, not a pharmacist.
- **Borrow.** Report the share of readings needing no clinician touch, and clinician minutes per patient, as in the HyperLink pharmacist trial [D1-01-F04].

#### Closed Loop Medicine, WeDosify (UK, launched in the US)

- **What.** Personalised dose options for GLP-1 weight-loss medicines, shown to clinicians, from tolerability, goals, clinical data and response. Built on 29 studies and 15,000+ patient profiles with PK/PD modelling (how a drug moves through the body and acts). FDA status not stated [D2-06-F24].
- **Round.** NOT FOUND. US launch announced 18 Sep 2025 [D2-06-F24].
- **Status, Nov 2026.** Closed Loop Medicine Ltd has been in members' voluntary liquidation (a solvent winding-up by shareholders) since 28 Apr 2026 [D3-06-F07]. Its website now points to Morchella Medical, a 2026 company that licenses the patents and dosing models (THEIR CLAIM) [D3-06-F08].
- **Reports (THEIR CLAIM).** 91% of surveyed GLP-1 patients would stay on treatment longer with support [D2-06-F25].
- **Evidence.** Its one blood-pressure trial (UK, published 2024, authors from the company and a university) titrated amlodipine through an app in 205 adults over 14 weeks, with no comparison group; systolic pressure fell 11 mmHg [D3-06-F09].

### D14. Eight ideas to borrow for a home blood-pressure step loop

OUR VIEW, most useful first.

1. Protocol held as data, options with reasons shown to the clinician, response fed back (Closed Loop Medicine) [D2-06-F24].
2. Before any step up, a call in the patient's language asks whether the tablet was taken, as the Gujarat protocol requires (Tucuvi) [D2-06-F10, D1-06-F14].
3. Report readings closed with no clinician touch and clinician minutes per patient (Cadence) [D2-06-F2].
4. Log every accept, edit and reject as the safety and training record (Counsel Health) [D2-06-F11].
5. A one-page staged safety ladder: supervised first, wider authority after testing (Hippocratic AI) [D2-06-F20].
6. Alert on drift of the 7-day mean, not single readings (Prana) [D2-06-F13].
7. One reading schema (value, time, device, quality) for any cuff or watch (Junction) [D2-06-F30].
8. Cuffless or 24-hour readings as an input or one-off check only; dose decisions stay on cuff readings (Aktiia, Biobeat) [D2-06-F37, D2-06-F36].

### D17. So what for us

OUR VIEW.
- **Stand where nobody was found.** India has many depth-3 sellers and one depth-4 product of unknown status; the step from a reading to a dose change is empty on what we found. Our place is a home BP loop that proposes the next protocol step for a doctor to approve, the only kind of programme PHTI passed [D1-05-F2]. A payer reading PHTI asks first whether a prescriber changes the medicine; our pitch should answer that in its first line.
- **The gap is real, and nobody has priced it.** The pattern exists abroad inside broad virtual clinics and for weight-loss medicines, but nobody funded sells it as a blood-pressure product for a public programme run by health workers. Only three of 32 rows in D10 are clearly at depth 4 (Cadence, Twin Health, WeDosify), and the one built most like our engine has no funding on record and its company is in liquidation [D3-06-F07]; most money sits at depths 1 to 3. Neither Brook.ai nor Embla showed a medicine change in the later search [D3-06-F37], so Cadence stays the nearest funded relative. Because no investor has priced this gap, the pitch must carry its own trial evidence (Appendix B).
- **Sell to a payer, not a patient.** The doctor controls the prescription and the payer controls the money; a product that leaves either out has no user or no buyer. Lasting businesses are paid for an outcome a payer counts, or for hardware that delivers the dose. Pear, Akili and Better Therapeutics had trials yet failed for lack of payment [D1-05-F20, D1-02-F44, D1-02-F46]. Public payers will pay for prescribed apps, around EUR 227 a course in Germany, but increasingly for results.
- **Build the prescriber in.** Clinician referral adds 40 days of retention [D1-02-F27]; Pear lacked prescribers [D1-05-F21].
- **Never sell the sensor or the reminder as the product,** and keep spending in line with revenue [D1-02-F41, D1-04-F23]. India's best-funded chronic-care app ran out of money within a year of its largest round; money raised is not proof that anyone will pay.
- **Measure what payers count from day one:** share under 140/90, and how many lost patients return [D1-05-F31].
- **Supply the mechanism the funds name.** The funds name the goal (act on numbers, cheaper clinician time) but not the mechanism; our thesis page can supply it: the dose step.
- **Do not sell explanation.** The labs are taking the "explain my numbers" job, so a reading app with a chat on top is a weak product. They stop before the regulated step, a medicine change approved by a named clinician, which is where our loop sits. Writing readings to Health Connect keeps our data open to whatever assistant the patient uses.
