# D4-05 Biomarkers and adherence signals (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-14)

## Digest

The 12 facts that matter most for the biology of the loop:

1. By urine or blood drug assay, about 1 in 3 people with apparent resistant hypertension are not taking their drugs (pooled 31.2%, range 3% to 86%) (D4-05-F01); 25% to 42% in UK and Czech LC-MS/MS series (F02, F03).
2. In primary care it is far lower: 4.7% of older UK patients (F12). Belgian "resistant" referrals were mostly under-dosed, not non-adherent (F44).
3. Odds of non-adherence rise 77% to 85% per added drug (F03; OR 1.57 in Brazil, F07). Each step-up raises the risk.
4. Self-report fails: 90.1% adherent by questionnaire vs 32.4% by urine assay (F07). In India, 99DOTS phone self-report had sensitivity 70%, NPV 21% against urine tests (F23).
5. Feeding assay results back raised adherence (70% to 92.5%) but did not lower BP in two RCTs (RHYME F05; OUTREACH -5.1 mmHg, NS, F11). Big BP falls come only from uncontrolled before-after data (F04).
6. Amlodipine and telmisartan leave no pulse signature (F16, F18); beta-blockers do (F41), diltiazem slightly (F42).
7. ARBs raise renin (losartan 2 to 3 times, F19); a 48% fall in aldosterone-to-renin ratio from baseline flagged adherence with 90% sensitivity, 75% specificity (one 40-patient study, F43).
8. Chlorthalidone lowers potassium and raises uric acid, dose-related and in step with its BP effect (F21, F22): a weak intake sign.
9. Recheck creatinine and potassium 2 to 4 weeks after starting or raising an ARB; continue unless creatinine rises over 30% (KDIGO 2024, F27, F28). eGFR change over 20% (F29) or ACR doubling (F33) is beyond noise.
10. HbA1c moves over 2 to 3 months; it cannot judge a 30-day step (F37).
11. India's free public list: dipstick at village level; creatinine, urea, uric acid at PHC; potassium and urine ACR sent to a hub; NT-proBNP only at district level (F39).
12. Long half-lives blur single assays: amlodipine 30 to 50 h (F17), chlorthalidone 40 to 60 h (F21); a dose just before a test hides gaps (Hawthorne effect, F06).

Surprises: cheap reminder devices did nothing in a 53,480-person RCT (F24), but reminder packaging raised pills taken 11% (F46); no Indian drug-assay study for hypertension exists that we found (F15); a smartphone home urine ACR test is FDA-cleared (F49).

Against lead 1: non-adherence grows with each added drug (F03), and objective adherence feedback has not yet lowered BP in RCTs (F05, F11).

## What this suggests for the product

- Suggestion: before any step-up, use signals beyond the patient's word, but do not expect a cheap body signal for the protocol drugs: amlodipine and telmisartan leave no pulse change (F16, F18). Pill counts or photos of the strip and refill dates are the practical first layer (F46, F47, F48).
- Suggestion: weight the adherence question by the number of drugs; risk rises about 1.8 times per added drug (F03, F07), so steps 3 to 5 need a stronger check than steps 1 to 2.
- Suggestion: borrow the Lane trigger as a rule: if systolic falls less than 10 mmHg after a new drug is added, flag possible non-adherence before the next step (F14).
- Suggestion: schedule creatinine and potassium 2 to 4 weeks after telmisartan starts or doubles, with a 30% creatinine-rise rule for doctor review (F27, F28); this fits inside the 30-day cycle.
- Suggestion: at chlorthalidone start, a baseline and 2 to 4 week potassium and uric acid gives both a safety check and a weak sign of intake (F21, F22). Potassium needs a hub sample in the public system (F39).
- Suggestion: do not use HbA1c or NT-proBNP as step-cycle signals; HbA1c is a 3-monthly check (F37); NT-proBNP is district-level and ₹1,200 to ₹3,520 privately (F35, F36), for suspected heart failure only.
- Suggestion: treat a drug assay as a referral-level test, not a loop feature: no Indian assay service or price was found (F15, NOT FOUND below), and RCT benefit on BP is unproven (F05, F11).
- Suggestion: if the loop wants an ARB-specific lab sign, a baseline renin or aldosterone-to-renin ratio before telmisartan and a repeat at 2 weeks is the only tested option found (F43); evidence is one 40-patient study.

### Q7. Body signals that show whether a drug was taken, drug by drug

- D4-05-F16 | Amlodipine does not change heart rate on chronic dosing | Norvasc label: chronic dosing lowers supine and standing BP "not accompanied by a significant change in heart rate or plasma catecholamine levels". Acute IV amlodipine raises heart rate; chronic oral use in angina trials changed heart rate by +0.3 bpm. Mechanism (common explanation, not stated in the label): slow onset of this long-acting dihydropyridine blunts reflex sympathetic activation. So heart rate cannot show whether amlodipine was taken. Year 2025. https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=abd6a2ca-40c2-485c-bc53-db1c652505ed | source dated 2025-06 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-05-F17 | Amlodipine half-life and steady state | Terminal half-life about 30 to 50 hours (56 hours in hepatic impairment); steady state after 7 to 8 days of daily dosing. Implication for assays: a dose taken the day before a test still shows in blood or urine, so a single test cannot separate steady daily use from a dose taken just before the visit. Year 2025. https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=abd6a2ca-40c2-485c-bc53-db1c652505ed | source dated 2025-06 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-05-F18 | Telmisartan: no heart-rate change; renin and angiotensin II rise | Micardis label: "There were no changes in the heart rate of patients treated with telmisartan in controlled trials." So a raised renin is a lab sign the ARB is being taken, but renin is not a routine Indian primary-care test and no adherence cut-off was found. - D4-05-F21 | Chlorthalidone: potassium falls and uric acid rises, dose-related; very long action | Chlorthalidone label: "Increases in serum uric acid and decreases in serum potassium are dose-related over the 15 to 100 mg/day range." Diuresis starts about 2.6 hours after a dose and lasts up to 72 hours; mean half-life 40 hours (53 hours after 50 mg, 60 hours after 100 mg); binds red-cell carbonic anhydrase. ### Q5. HbA1c

### Q7 (continued). Heart rate, renin and wearables
