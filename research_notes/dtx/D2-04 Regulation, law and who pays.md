# D2-04 Regulation, law and who pays (agent: Opus 5.5, 2026-11-13)

## Digest

Twelve facts that matter most:
1. CDSCO's MDSW guidance exists: CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026, 21 Jul 2026, opened on cdsco.gov.in (F01).
2. Its standalone-software matrix: Critical D/C/B, Serious C/B/A, Non-serious B/A/A across "treatment or diagnosis / drive / inform clinical management" (F03).
3. "Drive clinical management" includes information "to guide... next treatment interventions" and "safe and effective use of medicinal products" (F04). A clinician-approved BP step proposal reads as drive x serious = Class B, or Class A if non-serious. Patient-facing use without professional support can move the row up to critical (F03, F49).
4. CDSCO names "digital therapeutics platform... (e.g., Type 2 diabetes, hypertension...)" and a Bluetooth BP-tracking app as medical device software. Wellness carve-out is barred once a disease is named (F07, F09). No clinician-facing CDS exemption like the US one appears in the text (F10).
5. Pre-market patient studies need CLA permission (MD-22/MD-23). The MD-13 test licence covers "Test or Evaluation or Demonstration" (F13, F14, F39).
6. Telemedicine Guidelines 2020: AI platforms "cannot counsel patients or prescribe". Only a registered doctor prescribes (F17, commentary; primary blocked).
7. DPDP: Rule 4 (consent managers) starts one year after 13 Nov 2025. Consent and fiduciary duties start at 18 months (13 May 2027; AZB says 12 May). No general health-data exemption (F19, F20, F40).
8. The 1954 Act Schedule lists "Diabetes" and "High or low blood pressure" (F31).
9. US: insulin-titration software is a Class II 510(k) device (product code NDC; My Dose Coach K163099, K171230; d-Nav K181916) (F24). FDA's 6 Jan 2026 CDS guidance extends enforcement discretion to clinician-facing single recommendations (F23).
10. Medicare 2027 final rule (4 Nov 2026): RPM needs a face-to-face initiating visit from 1 Jan 2027. Direct-employee staff rule from 2029 (F26).
11. Germany: at least 20% of a DiGA price must be outcome-dependent from 1 Jan 2026 (s.134 SGB V). Average negotiated price €227 (F29, F42).
12. Japan: CureApp HT is billed at 90 points in person, 78 points online (new 1 Jun 2026), plus ¥7,010 material. It was capped at 6 months in the 2022 review (F36, F43).

Surprises:
- India's 2026 guidance has no carve-out for clinician-facing decision support.
- Medicare pays digital therapy codes only for behavioural health.
- The DiGA outcome-linked share is now statute.

Contradictions to the starting leads:
- Lead 5 ("decision support with clinician sign-off first") has no Indian exemption behind it. Sign-off lowers the matrix column; it does not take the software out of MDR.
- Lead 2: no Indian legal basis was found for a nurse or CHO changing doses (F18).

## Findings

Label key: [LAW] = statute or statutory rule; [GUIDANCE] = regulator guidance, not itself binding law; [COMMENTARY] = law firm, trade press, blog. "Q for counsel" marks open legal questions. This note never says a design is compliant.

### Q1. India: medical-device law for software (MDR 2017 + CDSCO 2026 MDSW guidance)

- D2-04-F01 [GUIDANCE] CONFIRMED: CDSCO "Guidance Document on Medical Device Software", Doc No. CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026, 62 numbered pages (92 PDF pages with annexures), PDF modified 21 Jul 2026. Covering circular F. No. MED-16028/2/2025-eoffice dated 21 Jul 2026 (per Nishith Desai note, 3 Aug 2026). Draft was issued 21 Oct 2025 (SNIPPET). Source: https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/Guidance-document-on-Medical-Device-Software-under-MDR-2017.pdf (2026-07-21, OPENED, text extracted locally). Circular number: https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623 (2026-08-03, OPENED). Pure Global summary quotes CDSCO saying it "should not be misconstrued as a new regulatory control" (https://www.pureglobal.com/news/india-cdsco-medical-device-software-guidance-2026, OPENED; I did not find that sentence myself in the extracted text, so treat as COMMENTARY).
- D2-04-F02 [LAW, restated in GUIDANCE s.7.0]: MDR 2017 Rule 4 classes: "Low risk Class A; Low moderate risk Class B; Moderate high risk Class C; High risk Class D" (Table 1) [meaning: four risk classes, A lowest]. Class "fundamentally based on the intended use of the software and the applicable rules in First Schedule" (s.7.0) [meaning: what the label, IFU, website and promotion say the software is for decides the class]. Source: CDSCO PDF p.16 (OPENED).
- D2-04-F03 [GUIDANCE] Standalone software matrix, Table 2 (s.7.1, p.17). Rows = healthcare situation; columns = significance of information:

| Situation | Treatment or diagnosis | Drive clinical management | Inform clinical management |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | D | C | B |
| Serious | C | B | A |
| Non-serious | B | A | A |

  Note under Table 2: software "intended to be used by non-clinical users in a 'serious situation or condition'... without the support from specialized professionals, may be considered as... 'critical'" [meaning: a patient-facing tool used without a clinician can be moved up one row]. (OPENED)
- D2-04-F04 [GUIDANCE] Definitions (s.7.1 a, p.18). "Treatment or diagnosis": information used "to take an immediate or near-term action to: Treat/prevent or mitigate by connecting to other medical devices, medicinal products..." "Drive clinical management": information used "to guide next diagnostics or next treatment interventions", including "To aid in treatment by providing enhanced support to safe and effective use of medicinal products". "Inform clinical management": "will not trigger an immediate or near term action" [meaning: a tool that proposes the next drug step for a clinician to approve reads most naturally as "drive clinical management"; one that only lists options reads as "inform". Which column a dose-proposal tool falls in is a Q for counsel / CDSCO, because "near-term action... medicinal products" also fits "treatment"]. (OPENED)
- D2-04-F05 [GUIDANCE] Situation definitions (s.7.1 b, pp.18-19). "Critical": disease "life threatening..., requires major therapeutic interventions, and/or time critical", population fragile, "Intended for use by specialized trained users". "Serious": disease "moderate in progression (often curable), does not require major therapeutic interventions... not expected to be time critical... providing the user an ability to detect erroneous recommendations". "Non-serious": "slow with predictable progression disease states (e.g., minor chronic illness...)... can be managed effectively, requires only minor interventions". [Meaning: where hypertension or type 2 diabetes sits (serious vs non-serious) is not stated in the guidance. Insulin, with hypoglycaemia risk, could be argued toward serious or critical. Q for counsel.] (OPENED)
- D2-04-F06 [OUR ARITHMETIC from F03] Reading the matrix: BP step-protocol proposal approved by a doctor = "drive clinical management" x "serious" = Class B, or x "non-serious" = Class A. If read as "treatment" x serious = Class C. Insulin dose proposal: drive x serious = B; treatment x serious = C; treatment x critical = D. CDSCO confirms the class: "The risk class shall be confirmed by CDSCO (CLA) upon review" (p.19). These are readings of a table, not determinations.
- D2-04-F07 [GUIDANCE] In-scope examples (s.5.1, pp.9-10): "Example (2): An embedded software that controls or drives an insulin pump to deliver a calculated dose of insulin." "Example (5): A software application that connects via Bluetooth to a blood pressure cuff to obtain readings in order to track blood pressure... for medical purposes." "Example (9): A digital platform, using Internet-of-Things (IoT)... with connected devices (like smart glucometers) to track chronic conditions... to offer real-time AI-driven data analytics and interventions". "Example (10): A behavior-change and digital therapeutics platform intended to mitigate the progression of chronic diseases (e.g., Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, etc.) through remote coaching and digital tracking." [Meaning: CDSCO names hypertension DTx, even coaching-only, as medical device software; a BP-cuff tracking app is in scope.] (OPENED)
- D2-04-F08 [GUIDANCE] s.5.1 note iii (p.8): "Software, including mobile apps/cloud software, that can control or adjust a medical device through a connection... Bluetooth or Wi-Fi... shall be considered as medical devices." s.7.1: software "driving or influencing the use of a hardware medical device shall be classified in the same risk class as the hardware" (p.16). (OPENED)
- D2-04-F09 [GUIDANCE] Out of scope (s.5.2, pp.11-13): ERP, data transfer/storage/"simple search", HIS/CIS for admission, scheduling, billing, communication; general communication systems sending "prescription, referrals, images, patient records"; general wellness software. Limits: HIS with "quantification of physiological parameters for clinical decision-making, real-time patient monitoring" "may be considered as a medical device"; wellness software "should not be making any reference to diseases"; software "to measure, estimate, or report physiologic values for... monitoring, alerting, or management of a disease... shall NOT be considered as General Wellness Software". [Meaning: a BP app that names hypertension cannot use the wellness carve-out.] (OPENED)
- D2-04-F10 [GUIDANCE] s.6.0 (p.13): intended use should state "whether it is a decision support software, driving a medical device, providing definitive diagnosis/treatment recommendation". The guidance has no separate carve-out for clinician-facing decision support comparable to the US 520(o)(1)(E) test; I found no such exemption in the text (grep for "decision support" returns only s.6.0 and an adverse-event term). (OPENED)
- D2-04-F11 [GUIDANCE] Classification lists: CLA has published MDSW risk lists under Rule 4(3), at https://cdscomdonline.gov.in/NewMedDev/ListOfApprovedRiskDevice; if not listed, apply on the portal for a class; "the strictest rules resulting in the higher classification shall apply" (p.19-20). (OPENED)
- D2-04-F12 [GUIDANCE restating LAW] Licensing authorities, Table 5 (s.11, p.29): test licence CLA all classes; manufacturing licence SLA for A and B, CLA for C and D; import CLA; clinical investigation CLA all classes. "Class A (non-sterile and non-measuring) medical device is exempted from the Licensing requirements, only registration is required as per Chapter IIIB". "The time line required for processing various license applications is mentioned in the MDR-2017" (no timelines in the guidance). (OPENED)
- D2-04-F13 [GUIDANCE restating LAW] Test licence (s.12.1, p.30): Form MD-12 application on NSWS for Form MD-13 test licence "to manufacture small quantities of MDSW for the purpose of Clinical Investigations or Test or Evaluation or Demonstration or Training", fee per Second Schedule; quantity can be stated as "number of installations/number of copies". SaaS: say whether hosted on a MeitY-empanelled cloud. (OPENED)
- D2-04-F14 [GUIDANCE restating LAW] Clinical investigation (s.12.2, p.32): "No person or sponsor shall conduct any Clinical Investigation of an Investigational Medical device (IMD)... except in accordance with the permission granted by the CLA" (Form MD-22 application / MD-23 permission, Chapter VII MDR). Exemptions from clinical investigation possible if CLA satisfied and no "theoretical possibility... of any difference in the behaviour and performance... in the Indian population". s.12.3: Form MD-26/MD-27 permission to manufacture an IMD before a marketing licence. [Meaning: a software with no predicate is an IMD; pre-licence pilots that test its clinical performance on patients need CLA permission. Whether a pilot where the clinician makes every decision unaided by the software output counts as a clinical investigation is a Q for counsel.] (OPENED)

- D2-04-F15 [COMMENTARY] Lupin Lyfe and Huma Class C (2024): NOT SEARCHED in this note (carried from D1-04-F15, F30). The CDSCO risk lists (F11) were not opened.
- D2-04-F16 [LAW] Fees and statutory timelines: the guidance says fees are "as specified in the Second Schedule" and timelines "mentioned in the MDR-2017" (F12, F13). I did not open the MDR 2017 Second Schedule. NOT FOUND (not opened): rupee fees and day counts.

### Q2. India: practice of medicine

- D2-04-F17 [GUIDANCE with regulatory status; text via COMMENTARY] Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 25 Mar 2020, issued by the Board of Governors in supersession of MCI as Appendix 5 to the IMC (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations 2002 (SNIPPET for the Appendix 5 status). ### Q9. Blood-pressure loop and insulin guidance (readings of the texts, not conclusions)

- D2-04-F49 [OUR ARITHMETIC from F03-F07] India class readings under Table 2: BP step proposal to a doctor who approves = "drive clinical management". Hypertension as "serious" -> Class B (SLA licence, MD-3 -> MD-5); as "non-serious" -> Class A. If a patient-facing app tells the patient to change a dose without a clinician, the Table 2 note can move "serious" to "critical" -> Class C. Insulin dose proposal: drive x serious = B; if CDSCO treats it as "treatment" or the population as fragile/critical -> C or D. If the software connects to and reads a cuff, the "same class as the hardware" rule (F08) can also apply. CDSCO's published MDSW risk list may already assign a class (not opened). The US comparator puts insulin titration software in Class II (F24). Class is confirmed by CDSCO (F06).
- D2-04-F50 [READING of F13, F14, F17, F35] What the texts allow before a marketing licence: a Form MD-13 test licence covers manufacture "for the purpose of Clinical Investigations or Test or Evaluation or Demonstration"; clinical investigation on patients of an investigational device needs CLA permission (MD-23) and EC approval; CTRI registration before enrolment. A desk test on open data, simulators or team members' readings is not a clinical investigation on patients. Whether a pilot where software drafts and a doctor decides each change counts as a clinical investigation is a Q for counsel.
