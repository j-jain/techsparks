### 10. Treatment delay in Indian diabetes care

- D3-07-F27 | AIIMS Bhopal diabetes clinic records Jan 2019 to Sep 2022, n=950: mean HbA1c (SD) of insulin users 8.92 (1.83); "insulin inertia" group 8.43 (1.63); insulin-naive 7.29 (0.88), P<0.001. ### 11. Indian trials of home BP monitoring or health-worker decision support

- D3-07-F29 | SMARThealth India (Peiris D et al., PLoS ONE 2019;14(3):e0213708). Stepped-wedge cluster RCT, 18 primary health centre clusters, West Godavari, Andhra Pradesh, 2 years; ASHA tablet risk assessment + doctor decision support + tracking. 62,254 assessed at baseline (84% of eligible; 18.4% high CVD risk); 11,484 high-risk, 8,642 followed up. Primary outcome systolic BP below 140: 41.2% vs 39.2%, adjusted OR 1.01 (95% CI 0.76-1.35), null. Subgroup ASHA-classified high risk: BP medicine use 54.3% vs 47.9%, OR 1.22 (95% CI 1.03-1.44), no BP control gain. Limits: only about 37% of the evaluation cohort exposed (risk-score discordance), heatwave during step 2, control-period improvement. Funder NHMRC (Australia); the George Institute disclosed a social enterprise using parts of the intervention. | CITED | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435227/ | 2019 | OPENED (via Europe PMC)
- D3-07-F30 | EASE-BP (CTRI/2023/02/049949), AIIMS New Delhi, WHO SEARO funded. Protocol: open-label randomised trial, two populations (self-reported non-hypertensive planned n=400; known hypertensive planned n=332); intervention arm gets a validated electronic BP device, advised to self-monitor at least twice a month; primary outcomes: new hypertension at 3 months (non-hypertensive part); systolic BP difference at 3 months, medicine adherence at 3 months co-primary (hypertensive part).
