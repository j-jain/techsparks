Why new blocks first: 97% aren’t built yet, so we’re written into the plans.

Sources: ICU beds estimate, CDDEP 2020 · Lok Sabha, Dec 2024 · Lok Sabha, Dec 2025 · IMPRI 2026 · Values are our arithmetic

Say: Every ICU bed in India is a potential customer. At our test price of 15,000 rupees per bed a month, that is a 1,710 crore rupee a year market. The part we can actually sell to, public ICU beds including the new district blocks, is 860 crore a year. We start narrower: 60 of the 621 new district ICUs by year three, about one in ten, which is 17 crore a year. We start there because 97% of those blocks are not built yet, so our cameras, network points and server can go into the building plans instead of a costly retrofit. The maths (all OUR ARITHMETIC at the ASSUMPTION price of Rs 15,000 per bed a month = Rs 1.8 lakh a year): - TAM: about 95,000 ICU beds in India (2020 modelled estimate: 59,262 private + 35,699 public; ICU beds assumed at about 5% of hospital beds). 95,000 x Rs 1.8 lakh = Rs 1,710 crore a year. Source: CDDEP / Princeton (now One Health Trust) preprint, medRxiv 2020 (search summary only). - SAM: 37,834 ICU beds in public facilities (Lok Sabha answer, Dec 2024; D6-01-F07, search summary only) + 621 new Critical Care Blocks x 16 monitored beds = 9,936 (D6-01-F03; 16 is the monitor count in the 50-bed block guideline, D6-01-F01; most blocks are 50-bed). Total 47,770 x Rs 1.8 lakh = Rs 859.9 crore, rounded to Rs 860 crore. Blocks finished before Dec 2024 may already sit in the 37,834 (only about 3% are complete), so the overlap is small. - SOM: TARGET of 60 new blocks by year 3 (about 1 in 10 of 621) x 16 monitored beds = 960 beds x Rs 1.8 lakh = Rs 17.3 crore, rounded to Rs 17 crore.
