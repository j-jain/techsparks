# D3-04 Patient and doctor voice (agent: Sonnet 5.5, 2026-11-13)

Honesty label: everything here is public voice and published interview studies. It is NOT interviews done by our team.

## Digest

What this note is: public voice and published interview studies, plus public app reviews and forum posts. It is NOT interviews done by our team and not our own customer evidence. Labels: PUBLISHED STUDY, PUBLIC REVIEW, FORUM POST. Fact IDs point into Findings below. Reviews are self-selected (and Fitterfly's look prompted), and the Europe PMC records were read as abstracts only.

The 12 facts that matter most:
1. Lost-to-follow-up interviews, coastal Karnataka, 32 hypertensive patients: "symptom-driven healthcare seeking, medication non-adherence, occupational responsibilities, and competing family priorities"; costs, travel, wage loss, rushed consultations (D3-04-F01).
2. Rural Tamil Nadu, 15 interviews: medicine stops in three ways, structural, unintentional (work, travel, caregiving) and intentional ("perceived harm, symptoms or treatment need"); family both helps and hinders (D3-04-F02).
3. Home BP monitor use among clinic-attending people with hypertension and their caregivers, North India, 2,588 people: 18.1% used one (urban 20.5%, rural 8.5%); 75.2% taught themselves; 24.7% were trained by a health worker (F06).
4. 832 Indian doctors: about 88% recommend home monitoring, but they estimate only 30.4% of patients measure at home; only 31.3% tell patients to measure before taking tablets (F07).
5. GRAND, 1,920 treated patients, 7 days of home readings: controlled 77.7% by clinic BP, 55.9% by clinic plus home BP; 21.8% had masked uncontrolled BP (F09). Patient knowledge of home monitoring was low, median 1 of 7 (F10).
6. A WhatsApp reminder with myth-busting before the follow-up visit did not work: attendance 21.8% against 19.6%, 388 patients, Punjab (F50).
7. Private doctors in Bhopal: 19 of 30 did not follow the state protocol, partly from "fear of either losing patients due to a lack of immediate blood pressure control or causing drug-related adverse effects" (F15).
8. Kolar doctors: "Doctors face very high workloads, especially in the public sector"; care is "very doctor-centred" (F16). ASHAs with a decision tool under physician supervision in diabetes: physician visits 85.0% vs 29.8% (F17).
9. Old women and shared phones: caregivers enable access for older adults, devices are shared (F05); NFHS-5: 54% of women 15-49 use a phone of their own, rural 46.6% (F23); rural elders: 50% own a phone, 11% digital literacy (F56).
10. Reviews of Indian chronic-care apps: biggest complaint themes are spam calls, no reachable doctor or helpline, coaching that runs dry, medicine or refund problems; the praise is for a named coach (F26 to F47).
11. Fear and traditional medicine: concern about long-term treatment and a preference for traditional medicine in Meghalaya (F12); Ayurvedic treatment then restarted tablets in a family post (F52).
12. Census 2011: about 10.6% of Indians speak English (F57). No review of the 300 read mentions language.

Three surprises:
- The WhatsApp myth-debunking trial found no effect (F50), while a Delhi voice-call plus SMS plus flipchart study without a control reported 14.7% to 52.9% control (F51).
- Under clinic BP alone, 77.7% looked controlled; adding home readings dropped it to 55.9% (F09).
- A stated "under 5% of households own a validated monitor" (F22) has no source, and the 18.1% figure comes from a clinic sample, so no household-level figure exists here.

Against the brief's starting leads: lead 2 (workload and the visit gap) is supported by F15, F16, F01. No Indian doctor or nurse voice was found on home readings driving dose steps, and no patient voice on a dose increase (see NOT FOUND).

### Q1 Published interview studies, India (all PUBLISHED STUDY)
- D3-04-F01 PLoS One, published 2026-10-27, Coastal Karnataka tertiary hospital: 32 hypertensive patients lost to follow-up, interviewed Feb-Apr 2026. Themes: illness perception, financial burden, wage loss, rushed consultations, paper records. Authors: "Participants described symptom-driven healthcare seeking, medication non-adherence, occupational responsibilities, and competing family priorities contributing to irregular follow-up." Also "Several participants perceived that integrated digital health information systems, decentralised services, appointment reminders, and improved counselling could strengthen long-term hypertension follow-up." (second quote is 24 words). DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0359571; PMID 42945358; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42945358 OPENED.
- D3-04-F02 BMJ Open, 2026-09-23, rural Tamil Nadu (Thiruvallur): 15 adults with diabetes and/or hypertension, in-depth interviews in Tamil. Interruption routes: structural (supply, affordability), unintentional (work, travel, caregiving), intentional ("perceived harm, symptoms or treatment need"). Quote: "Family relationships could both support and hinder medication-taking". DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2026-118921; PMID 42778240; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42778240 OPENED. Small sample (15).
- D3-04-F03 Cureus, 2025-11-17, Tehri district, Uttarakhand: 40 people (20 diabetes, 20 hypertension), semi-structured interviews in Garhwali. Themes include "the use of alternative medicine", and "lack of self-management guidance from their physicians". DOI 10.7759/cureus.97128; PMID 41426819; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41426819 OPENED.
- D3-04-F04 J Health Serv Res Policy, 2026-04-03, rural Meghalaya: 29 women aged 60+ with diabetes. Themes: "weak social support which hindered diabetes care", "caregiving roles as barriers to care", "feelings of guilt due to financial dependency". DOI 10.1177/13558196261437023; PMID 41930620; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41930620 OPENED.
- D3-04-F05 BMC Public Health, 2026-03-14, DIG-EQUITY, Chennai and Chunampet (Tamil Nadu): 54 participants (4 focus groups, 12 key informant interviews: people with diabetes, family caregivers, providers, policymakers). Quotes: "Interpersonal support from caregivers enabled access, particularly for older adults and children." and "shared device ownership" listed under organisational factors; "data privacy, security, and app reliability affected trust and continued engagement". DOI 10.1186/s12889-026-26999-w; PMID 41832485; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41832485 OPENED.

### Q2 Home BP monitors (all PUBLISHED STUDY)
- D3-04-F06 North India, 3 centres, 2,588 people with hypertension plus caregivers (2,070 urban, 518 rural), published 2024-11-22: 468 (18.1%) used home BP monitoring; urban 20.5% (424/2070), rural 8.5% (44/518). Only 24.7% of the 468 recalled training from a health worker; 75.2% "learning HBPM themselves through observation, videos, and reading" (source wording: "reported learning HBPM themselves through observation, videos, and reading"). Technique excellent in 15.9% of patients vs 5.9% of caregivers. PMID 39579976; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39579976 OPENED. Sample is clinic attenders, not households.
- D3-04-F07 Asia HBPM survey, India arm: 832 physicians, Jun 2020 to Jun 2021, published 2023-04-23. About 88% recommend home BP monitoring; "only 34.9% of their patients own HBPM device and 30.4% of the patients measure their own BP at home" (physician estimates, not patient counts). 31.3% tell patients to measure before taking tablets; about 30% give no instructions. PMID 37088141; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37088141 OPENED. SOURCE ESTIMATE (doctors' perceptions).
- D3-04-F08 Asia HBPM survey, 11 Asian places, 7,945 physicians, 2023-04-11: 95.9% recommend home BP monitoring but fewer than half of patients measure; "concern with the accuracy and reliability of HBPM devices" a key barrier. PMID 37041412; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37041412 OPENED.
- D3-04-F09 GRAND study, 18 centres in 12 states, 1,920 treated hypertensive patients aged 20-70 (all on drugs 3+ months and regular clinic attenders), 7 days home BP. Controlled by clinic BP alone 77.7%; by clinic plus home BP 55.9%; masked uncontrolled 21.8%; white-coat 5.1%; sustained uncontrolled 17.2%. Published 2026-11-06. PMID 42995223; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42995223 OPENED.
- D3-04-F10 GRAND knowledge paper, 1,920 patients, published 2026-08-21: "median knowledge score was 1 (range 0-7), indicating generally low knowledge"; "particularly poor understanding of BP thresholds". PMID 42629489; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42629489 OPENED.
- D3-04-F11 Digital-twin RCT India (319 people with type 2 diabetes, 2024-08-14): home BP monitoring system "guided anti-HTN medication adjustments"; systolic -7.6 vs -3.2 mmHg at 1 year. Only 59 on BP drugs. PMID 39372467. OPENED. (Context for dose changes by home reading in India; the doctors and company run it.)


## Batch 2 (Europe PMC abstracts, OPENED, PUBLISHED STUDY)

### Q1/Q6 more interview studies
- D3-04-F12 BMC Health Serv Res, 2026-04-03, West Garo Hills, Meghalaya: 52 participants (7 in-depth interviews, 6 focus groups: providers, frontline workers, community members, village health council). Barriers to hypertension/diabetes screening: "Work obligations, fear of diagnosis, and concern about long-term treatment discouraged participation in screening." and "family members - particularly elders - played a key role in determining participation"; "a preference for traditional medicine constrained screening efforts". PMID 38330094; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38330094 OPENED.
- D3-04-F14 Ludhiana urban health centre, 2023-08-29, 40 people with diabetes, interviews: "lack of knowledge, financial problems, familial issues, misconceptions regarding the disease, and side effects of taking medications daily" were the main reasons for non-adherence; "about 50% patients were adherent". PMID 37767450; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/37767450 OPENED.

### Q3 Doctors and health workers
- D3-04-F15 Private doctors on the India Hypertension Control Initiative protocol, Bhopal, 2020-07-20, 30 private physicians interviewed: only 11 followed the state protocol. Reasons for the other 19 included "fear of either losing patients due to a lack of immediate blood pressure control or causing drug-related adverse effects" and preference for fixed-dose combinations. PMID 33289944; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33289944 OPENED.
- D3-04-F16 Kolar district, rural Karnataka, 2019-01-18, doctors in 3 private clinics and 3 public primary health centres plus 24 patients: "Doctors face very high workloads, especially in the public sector" and "the care processes are very doctor-centred, with little room for other health centre staff". Also: "The patient is largely left on their own in trying to make sense of their condition" (source wording: "The patient is largely left on their own in trying to make sense of their condition and in finding their way..."). PMID 30658641; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30658641 OPENED.
- D3-04-F17 IMPACT Diabetes, cluster trial, 2025-10-21, 16 villages/8 primary health centres in two states, 418 people with diabetes found by ASHAs: ASHAs gave nine months of decision-support care under physician supervision. HbA1c fall of 0.5 or more in 21.8% vs 10.3% usual care (p<0.05); physician visits 85.0% vs 29.8%; adherence 63.0% vs 43.1%. Authors: "acceptable and feasible for patients, ASHAs, and physicians". PMID 41120559; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41120559 OPENED. Diabetes, not blood pressure; ASHA used a tablet decision tool.
- D3-04-F18 Indian physicians on home BP (Asia HBPM India arm, F07): doctors' own reference values for home BP diagnosis correct in only 15.1%; "HBPM instructions to the patients are either lacking or not well aligned with the local hypertension guidelines". OPENED, PMID 37088141.
- D3-04-F19 COVID-era web survey of 2,545 Indian health practitioners (2021-05-01, conference abstract): challenges included "dose modification (41%)" and "advising treatment via teleconsultation (48%)"; 32% thought "self-monitoring of BP" could improve adherence. PMC8136034. OPENED (abstract only; sponsor not stated; low weight).
- D3-04-F20 Chhattisgarh Health and Wellness Centres, 2024-03-19, 14 focus groups: "People appreciated the supportive behaviour of health workers in HWCs." Hypertension and diabetes services "seen as a key value addition". PMID 38504174; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38504174 OPENED. Note: Meghalaya (F12) reports "workforce vacancies and limited training".
- D3-04-F21 Community health officers on eSanjeevani telemedicine, 120 CHOs, 2026-09-01 (PMID 42690874): found by search, abstract only partly read. NOT used for BP follow-up claims.

### Q6 Trust and fear
