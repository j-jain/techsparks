Say: We are asking for one thing: a district ICU, or a small private ICU, where we can run a three-month pilot. With it we need a clinical lead who signs every protocol file, and an introduction to a state health mission so the pilot can turn into a contract. We will call it a success if a specialist decision takes under two minutes, more than nine in ten timed checks happen on time, and one specialist can cover more than 80 patients. Plan behind the ask (idea check, section 9; all OUR DESIGN): - Before the pilot, build three parts and bench-test them on open ICU data: a camera that reads any monitor, the protocol engine with a snakebite file and a sepsis file, and the specialist’s one-tap card. Replay data: eICU-CRD Demo, over 2,500 ICU stays from 20 hospitals, open licence. D6-02-F22. - Pilot: one district ICU (about 10 beds) or one small private ICU, three months, before and after. - Pass bars are TARGETS: median under 2 minutes per specialist decision; above 90% of timed checks (such as the snakebite 6-hour recheck) done on time; above 80 patients per specialist, beating Cloudphysician’s reported 60 to 80 (2020, search summary only, D6-02-F04). Also logged: time from alert to action, transfers and time to departure, ICU deaths (reported only), and a staff survey. - One ICU cannot show an effect on deaths. TELESCOPE needed 30 ICUs and found none for remote rounds (D6-02-F12). An outcome study comes later, across many blocks. - Before signing any site: check that nurses and duty doctors are actually in post. With no one to carry out a step, there is nothing to run. - The Ask asks for no money. A grant or seed figure can be added later.
