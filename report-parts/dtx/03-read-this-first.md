## A. Read this first

### The answer in six lines

1. **What the fund is asking for.** Care that changes the treatment itself between appointments, from a device or biomarker reading, for people who cannot see a specialist often. Part B decodes the wording.
2. **What the evidence says.** Outcomes move when a home reading leads to a changed treatment. They mostly do not when the reading is only shown or a reminder is sent. Self-monitoring of blood pressure alone moved pressure by 1.0 mmHg; with intensive support (dose changes by doctors, pharmacists or patients, plus education or counselling) it moved 6.1 mmHg [D1-01-F05]. An independent US review backed only the programmes that adjust medication [D1-01-F39]. Part D has the trials, including the ones that failed.
3. **Where India's gap is.** The government's hypertension programme controls pressure well in patients who come back, and about half do not come back [D1-03-F10]. In its first nine districts 21.2% of people with high blood pressure are controlled [D1-01-F11]. Parts C, C2 and D2.
4. **What to build tonight.** A home blood-pressure step loop: a checked home reading, the state's published step protocol run from a data file, a question on whether the medicine was taken, a doctor's one-tap approval, and the change sent in the patient's language. Part J is the build pack.
5. **Who else does it.** No Indian service was found that changes a dose from home readings (a search result, not proof of absence). The nearest products abroad are a Japanese cuff maker's UK software that drafts a medication plan for the clinician (search summary only) [D3-06-F35] and US programmes that use their own clinicians [D1-05-F1]. A UK company that dosed one blood-pressure medicine by app was wound up in April 2026 [D3-06-F07]. Parts F1 and H.
6. **Who you are pitching to.** The mentor's fund led a 2023 round in Wellthy, an Indian digital-therapeutics company, and its first investment in 2015 was the Japanese company whose blood-pressure app is covered by insurance there [D3-06-F01, D3-06-F26]. That app coaches lifestyle and does not change medicines [D3-06-F19]. Your loop is the step neither had. Part K.

### The three weak spots to own before someone else raises them

1. **Who pays.** Our price of ₹149 a patient a month is an ASSUMPTION. Public-sector medicines cost about ₹200 a patient a year [D2-01-F12]. At our price the US$100 million-a-year test fails in India alone (part F2).
2. **It will be regulated**, and a study on patients needs permission and ethics approval first [D2-04-F13, D2-04-F14].
3. **An Indian trial of software for clinicians found nothing** [D1-01-F46]. The loop must earn its value between visits, among the patients who do not return.
