## D2. How care has been scaled without specialists

### What this part shows

India and other low-income settings have raised blood-pressure control without specialists by giving routine work to nurses and health workers under a written protocol, backed by free drugs and regular refills. In India the dose change still waits for a doctor's visit, and that is where a home step loop could fit.

### India's hypertension programme in practice

The India Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI) shows what a protocol programme does well, and where patients drop out.

**Design choices**
- A protocol of 3 to 4 steps, amlodipine first. Punjab: amlodipine 5 mg, then 10 mg, then add telmisartan 40 mg (to 80 mg), then add chlorthalidone 12.5 mg (to 25 mg). Maharashtra orders the steps differently [D3-05-F01].
- Free drugs, given as 30-day refills at every facility level from health and wellness centres (HWC) to district hospitals; sub-centres are not named [D3-05-F02].
- The open-source Simple app: about 80 seconds to register a patient, 15 seconds for a follow-up, overdue lists with high-risk patients first, one-click nurse calls, automated reminders [D3-05-F03].

**Who does what.** Medical officers start amlodipine and review at 30 days; nurses register patients and record follow-up; Senior Treatment Supervisors support training. Who steps the dose in Maharashtra is not stated [D3-05-F06].

**Starts and losses.** Two cohorts give different loss figures; they differ in sites, years and definition:

| Cohort | Started | Not seen again | Control among those seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 sentinel sites, 4 states, registered 2018-19 | 21,895 | 49% (51% returned by Jul-Sep 2019) | 26.3% at registration, 59.8% at follow-up [D1-03-F10] |
| Punjab and Maharashtra, started 2018-2021 | 281,209 | 36% (no visit Jan-Mar 2022) | 70% and 76% on amlodipine 5 mg alone; 75% and 81% after step 2 [D3-05-F04] |

District surveys found 14.5% of people with hypertension controlled in 2018-19 and 21.2% in 2023-24, with no control districts [D1-01-F10, D1-01-F11]. The programme's own deck claims 75 million people with hypertension and/or diabetes on standard care by December 2025 (THEIR CLAIM) [D3-05-F10].

**Why patients stopped.** A Bathinda (Punjab) phone survey of 157 people who had missed visits for over 3 months, a small convenience sample: no sense of being ill 24.8%, distance 22.2%, no guidance or reminder 15.2%, acute illness 8.2% [D3-05-F07].

**The refill rule.** A doctor dispenses the first medicine. Trained paramedical staff dispense the monthly refill only when pressure is controlled; uncontrolled patients are sent to a doctor for a dose change [D3-05-F08].

**Cost.** Amlodipine for the first two steps cut drug cost about 20%, from US$2.50 to US$2.05 per patient per year [D3-05-F05]. A 2022 WHO release gives "as low as Rs 200" per patient per year, a modelled drug cost (SOURCE ESTIMATE) [D3-05-F09]. Neither includes staff; a full cost was not found.

**So what for us.** OUR VIEW: IHCI already has the protocol, free drugs, a record and an overdue list. It lacks a dose decision for the uncontrolled patient who does not go back to the doctor.

### Who may change a dose in India

- Opened source: in Bathinda, paramedical staff refill only when pressure is controlled; uncontrolled patients go to a doctor [D3-05-F08].
- Search summary only: state protocol text has the medical officer start and raise the dose; once controlled, the ANM (auxiliary nurse midwife) at the HWC or sub-centre measures pressure and gives a month's medicines [D3-05-F31].
- Search summary only: community health officer (CHO) duties are screening, referral and follow-up; starting treatment sits with the medical officer and staff nurse [D3-05-F30, D3-05-F13].
- NOT FOUND: any order letting a CHO or nurse start or change an antihypertensive dose. The 2023-2030 national NCD guidelines could not be opened.

We cannot say they may, nor that they are barred. It remains a question for state health departments and a lawyer.

**So what for us.** OUR VIEW: design for a doctor approving every dose change.

### Tamil Nadu's doorstep scheme

Makkalai Thedi Maruthuvam (MTM) brings screening and medicines to the home [D3-05-F15].
- Scale: launched August 2021; by August 2024, 1,86,13,872 beneficiaries, with 92,59,821 people with hypertension given medicines (government figures, not audited; THEIR CLAIM) [D3-05-F14].
- Women health volunteers deliver drugs to registered patients over 45 or with poor mobility (search summary only) [D3-05-F16].
- A 2025 survey of 6,856 adults: over 95% of those diagnosed on treatment, but a hypertension control rate of only 35.4% (the abstract does not state the denominator) [D1-03-F09].
- **It refills; it does not step.** No source says the dose is changed at the door; delivery continues the existing prescription [D3-05-F15].

**So what for us.** OUR VIEW: the drugs already reach the door at the same dose; high treatment with low control is the gap a dose step addresses.

**So what for us.** OUR VIEW: the largest controlled gains came where a non-doctor could step the dose, with free drugs or one pill; monitoring, counselling or prompts gained less or nothing.

### What the fixes are worth

| Fix | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Longer refills | Uganda 3-month refills: 96.4% retained, control 73.5% to 78.3%, no control group [D3-05-F36] |
| Single-pill combinations | Systolic 3.99 mmHg lower at week 12 vs separate pills; mostly observational (search summary only) [D3-05-F39] |
| Home delivery | IMPACT-BP home arm 7.9 mmHg lower [D3-05-F27] |
| Phone follow-up | Calls beat generic reminders, "moderately effective" (search summary only) [D3-05-F41]; no Indian trial isolates calls |

**So what for us.** OUR VIEW: each fix keeps patients on drugs; none changes the dose.

### What these programmes changed that software alone could not

- Drug stock: Nigeria [D3-05-F22]; IHCI [D3-05-F02]; China [D1-01-F38].
- Authority for a non-doctor to change drugs: China [D1-01-F09]; Lesotho [D3-05-F24]; PAHO roadmap [D3-05-F28].
- A person carrying medicine to the home: IMPACT-BP [D3-05-F27]; Tamil Nadu [D3-05-F14].
- Pay linked to control and physician audit: China [D3-05-F21].
- A person who phones and books visits: CARRS [D3-05-F18]; IHCI nurses [D3-05-F03].
- Software alone, mWellcare, added nothing [D1-01-F46].

**So what for us.** OUR VIEW: software was part of every working programme and the whole of none.

### Where a home step loop fits (OUR VIEW)

**What it adds to IHCI today**
- The dose step for patients who do not return (36-49% [D3-05-F04, D1-03-F10]), who now need a doctor's visit to step up [D3-05-F08]. The loop sends the doctor a proposed step from home readings, approved without a visit.
- A reading trusted enough to act on, so the "controlled or not" rule [D3-05-F08] can run at home.
- It answers distance and missing guidance, not "no sense of being ill" [D3-05-F07].

**What it must borrow:** free 30-day refills [D3-05-F02] or doorstep delivery [D3-05-F14]; a person who follows up [D3-05-F03]; the protocol [D3-05-F01]; a doctor's sign-off [D3-05-F30].

**Closest precedent:** IMPACT-BP, with home cuffs, delivered medicine and remote nurse decisions [D3-05-F27]. Warnings: visit-time prompts added nothing [D1-01-F46], and China's gain came with drugs and incentives [D1-01-F38].

### What we could not find

- Any order letting a CHO or nurse change a dose; the national NCD guidelines text.
- IHCI full cost per patient; Maharashtra's titrator; an effect size for nurse calls.
- Tamil Nadu's delivering cadre and refill interval; who exactly changed doses in IMPACT-BP; Kerala control outcomes.
- An Indian decentralised-refill pilot with numbers; China's programme cost; INTE-COMM results; an ECHO India effect on control [D3-05-F33].
