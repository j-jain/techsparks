### Q1 India devices: software as a medical device (CDSCO)

Status legend: LAW = statute/rules in force; GUIDANCE = regulator explanatory text; DRAFT.

- KEY GUIDANCE (final, 2026): CDSCO "Guidance Document on Medical Device Software", Doc No. CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026, 62 pp, PDF metadata modified 21 Jul 2026. Replaces the draft of 21 Oct 2025 (draft PDF: cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Draft%20guidance%20document%20on%20Medical%20Device%20Software%2021%2010%202025.pdf; draft date from Nishith Desai Associates, 31 Oct 2025, SNIPPET/secondary). Final reported 30 Jul 2026 by Emergo by UL. https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/Guidance-document-on-Medical-Device-Software-under-MDR-2017.pdf OPENED (text extracted). The notice on p.1 says it is "not meant to be used for legal or professional purposes": guidance, not law. Quote p.3: "reflects current practices under the MDR-2017 and should not be misconstrued as a new regulatory control".
- KEY Scope (guidance p.3, s.1-2): software intended for medical purposes that meets the Medical Devices Rules 2017 (MDR-2017) device definition is regulated as a medical device. Excluded: "merely intended for general wellness, promoting a healthy lifestyle, or measure body parameters only for regular tracking and fitness purposes". Same URL, OPENED.
- KEY Wellness line (guidance s.5.2, p.13-14): wellness software "should not be making any reference to diseases or disorders or pathological conditions"; software that measures or reports physiologic values for "screening, diagnosis, monitoring, alerting, or management of a disease" "shall NOT be considered as General Wellness Software". It also allows software relating healthy lifestyle to reducing risk of chronic disease where lifestyle role is well accepted. Same URL, OPENED. (Our reading, not legal advice: a lifestyle coach that names "hypertension" or "diabetes" as the thing it treats sits on the wrong side of this line.)
- KEY Risk class table for standalone software (guidance Table 2, p.17): rows = healthcare situation; columns = Treatment or diagnosis / Drive clinical management / Inform clinical management. Critical: D / C / B. Serious: C / B / A. Non-serious: B / A / A. Same URL, OPENED.
- Definitions (p.18-20): "Treatment or diagnosis" = output used for immediate or near-term action to treat or mitigate by connecting to devices, medicinal products or other means of providing therapy. "Drive clinical management" = aid in treatment, "guide next diagnostics or next treatment interventions", incl. "enhanced support to safe and effective use of medicinal products". "Inform clinical management" = no immediate action, informs options. Non-serious condition example given: "minor chronic illness or states ... may not be curable but can be managed effectively". Same URL, OPENED.
- KEY Non-clinician note (p.17, p.19-20): standalone software for non-clinical users in a "serious" condition "without the support from specialized professionals, may be considered" as used in a "critical" condition and so can move up a class. Same URL, OPENED. (Our reading, not legal advice: a patient-facing app that changes a dose with no clinician is most likely Class C or D; a clinician-facing draft-for-approval tool for hypertension or diabetes likely sits at Class B or C depending on whether the condition is judged serious or non-serious. CDSCO fixes the class; the guidance says the class "shall be confirmed by CDSCO (CLA)".)
- Example in guidance (p.9ish, line "Example (2)"): embedded software that drives an insulin pump to deliver a calculated dose takes the hardware's class. Same URL, OPENED.
- Class confirmation route: CDSCO publishes a dynamic list of classified software at cdscomdonline.gov.in/NewMedDev/ListOfApprovedRiskDevice; if not listed, apply on the MD Online portal for a risk class. Same URL, OPENED.
- KEY Licensing authorities (guidance Table 5, p.29): test licence = Central Licensing Authority (CLA) for all classes; manufacturing licence = State authority for A and B, CLA for C and D; import = CLA all classes; sale and distribution = State. Note in guidance: Class A (non-sterile, non-measuring) is exempt from licensing, "only registration is required" under Chapter IIIB. Same URL, OPENED. Timelines: guidance only says they "are mentioned in the MDR-2017"; fees are in MDR-2017 Second Schedule. NOT PULLED: the numbers.
- KEY Test licence route for a pilot (guidance s.12.1, p.30-31): manufacture test licence Form MD-13, applied via Form MD-12 on the NSWS portal (nsws.gov.in), documents per Rule 31 + fee per Second Schedule; import test licence Form MD-17 via Form MD-16 (Rule 40). Purpose covers "Clinical Investigations or Test or Evaluation or Demonstration or Training", "Not for Commercialization". Quantity may be stated as "number of installations/number of copies/number of intended deployments", must be justified; test-licence application for multi-site deployment may run in parallel with the clinical-investigation application. SaaS: say whether hosted on a MeitY-empanelled cloud. Same URL, OPENED.
- Clinical investigation (s.12.2-12.3): software that is an "investigational medical device" needs CLA permission in Form MD-23 (application on CDSCO MD Online portal, Chapter VII MDR-2017) before testing on people; then MD-26 application / MD-27 permission to manufacture or import the investigational device before a marketing licence. Exemption from trial possible under Chapter VII if CLA is satisfied with safety/performance data and no expected difference in Indian population. Same URL, OPENED.
- AI/ML: guidance has an Algorithm Change Protocol (ACP) section (s. near line 1858) and lists "Incorrect diagnosis, treatment recommendations, clinical decision support errors" under algorithmic risks. Emergo summary: applicants must disclose training/validation/test dataset composition and "data related to model bias, generalizability and robustness" across Indian sub-populations. https://www.emergobyul.com/news/india-cdsco-finalizes-guidance-medical-device-software dated 30 Jul 2026, OPENED (secondary); guidance text OPENED for ACP.
- Draft history: Oct 2025 draft (21 Oct 2025) reported by IANS and Nishith Desai; MTaI welcomed it. https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/the-next-frontier-in-medtech-regulation-dissecting-cdscos-draft-guidance-on-medical-device-software-15465 OPENED (secondary, dated 31 Oct 2025).
- Cybersecurity and QMS: manufacturers need QMS (MDR-2017 Fifth Schedule, ISO 13485 basis) and post-market duties; per Nishith Desai summary. SECONDARY.


### Q2 India prescribing: Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (TPG) 2020

- KEY GUIDELINES in force (as far as found): "Telemedicine Practice Guidelines", 25 March 2020, issued by the Board of Governors in supersession of the Medical Council of India with NITI Aayog; "Appendix 5 of the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics Regulation, 2002)". Primary text OPENED (48-page PDF, downloaded from https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf). The mohfw.gov.in copy returned 403/HTML. Whether the guidelines are binding as regulation: the text calls itself Appendix 5 of the 2002 regulations (so enforceable as professional conduct through medical councils); a successor to the 2002 regulations (NMC RMP Professional Conduct Regulations 2023) was reported held in abeyance, NOT VERIFIED here.
- KEY AI clause (TPG s.5.4, p.33): "Technology platforms based on Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning are not allowed to counsel the patients or prescribe any medicines to a patient." Same clause: decision-support systems "could assist and support a RMP on patient evaluation, diagnosis or management, the final prescription or counseling has to be directly delivered by the RMP". OPENED, primary. (Our reading, not legal advice: this squarely allows software that drafts a dose change for a doctor to approve; it bars a patient-facing system that prescribes by itself. It sits in the telemedicine guidelines; whether it reaches an app that adjusts a dose inside an in-person care programme is untested.)
- KEY Who may prescribe: only a Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP). Quote s.3.5: "Prescribing Medicines without an appropriate diagnosis/provisional diagnosis will amount to a professional misconduct". OPENED.
- KEY Medicine lists (s.3.6.4, p.20-21): List O = medicines safe through any tele-mode (OTC type, e.g. paracetamol, ORS, emergency medicines). List A = prescribable at first video consult and for refill at follow-up (relatively safe, low abuse potential). List B = prescribable only at follow-up, in addition to those prescribed in person for the same condition. Prohibited = Schedule X drugs and narcotic/psychotropic substances. The lists are "as notified" and can be modified by the BoG (s.6.1). I did NOT find the content of Annexure 1 drug names in this pass (the annexure is in the PDF; not read). The guideline text does not itself name antihypertensives or insulin in the body text checked. NOT CHECKED: Annexure 1 contents.
- Follow-up definition (s.3.6.2): same RMP, within 6 months of the previous in-person consultation, same condition; not a follow-up if new symptoms or the RMP "does not recall the context". First consult includes more than 6 months since last visit. OPENED.
- Health worker role (s.4.2, 5.2): "Health worker" = nurse, allied health professional, mid-level practitioner, ANM or other designated worker. The worker starts a tele-consult with an RMP, obtains consent, provides the medicine "prescribed by the RMP", reinforces counselling. The RMP may prescribe medicines "as prescribed for use in guidelines from time to time for a particular cadre of Health Workers". The text gives no independent dose-change power to the health worker. OPENED.
- NMC changes since: found NO 2026 revision of the TPG and none naming AI. Found only: Feb 2025 NMC RTI reply that the TPG do not allow remote authentication of lab reports (Medical Dialogues, SNIPPET), a 13 Oct 2026 NMC advisory on remote authentication of records (Medical Dialogues, SNIPPET: https://medicaldialogues.in/health-news/nmc/nmc-issues-advisory-for-doctors-on-remote-authentication-of-medical-records-via-digital-signatures-181173), a 6 Oct 2026 NMC public notice on ethical advertising incl. a limit on AI in promotion (Telangana Today, SNIPPET), and 11 Aug 2026 draft amendments to the RMP registration regulations (SNIPPET). The advertising notice matters for Q4.
- Practice: India Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI) uses state-specific, drug-and-dose-specific protocols. Punjab and Maharashtra analysis: step 1 amlodipine 5 mg; step 2 raise amlodipine to 10 mg or add telmisartan 40 mg depending on state; 70% (Punjab, n=33,450 at follow-up) and 76% (Maharashtra, n=125,842) controlled at step 1 (per search summary, SNIPPET of PMC10959138; Global Heart, Mar 2024). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959138/ . A state protocol letter (Telangana, 4 Sep 2018, Resolve to Save Lives) says the Medical Officer starts and escalates and the ANM dispenses and refers back if BP is 140/90 or above (SNIPPET): https://resolvetosavelives.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/telangana-a4-letter-hypertension-protocol_04092018-1.pdf . CHOs at Health and Wellness Centres run NCD screening and follow-up (SNIPPET). I found NO written standing order that lets a nurse or CHO change a dose independently; the documents found keep escalation with the Medical Officer. This is a gap, not proof that none exists.


### Q4 India claims: Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954 (DMRA) and ASCI

- KEY LAW in force (as amended 1963): DMRA Schedule, "See sections 3(d) and 14", lists 9. "Diabetes", 26. "Heart diseases", 27. "High or low blood pressure" (also cancer, cataract, glaucoma, etc.). Primary text OPENED: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/1412/1/A1954-21.pdf (Act 21 of 1954; Schedule substituted w.e.f. 17 Dec 1963). Item numbers 9, 26, 27 read from the extracted Schedule.
- KEY s.3(d): "no person shall take any part in the publication of any advertisement referring to any drug in terms which suggest or are calculated to lead to the use of that drug for ... the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention of any disease, disorder or condition specified in the Schedule". OPENED. Penalty s.7: first conviction up to six months, or fine, or both; later conviction up to one year, or fine, or both; offences cognizable (s.9A). OPENED.
- KEY Reach to software: "drug" in s.2(b) includes "any article, other than food, intended to affect or influence in any way the structure or any organic function of the body" and "any substance intended to be used for or in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention of disease". OPENED. (Our reading, not legal advice: an app is not obviously a "drug" or "article"; no case found applying the Act to software. The risk is real for any product sold with a pill or device and for "cure / reverse / treat diabetes or high blood pressure" claims. Treat "reversal" and "cure" wording on diabetes or blood pressure as high risk; "helps manage" with clinician sign-off is lower. NOT FOUND: any court ruling on apps.) The Act's text is old (1954 and 1963); a 2020 draft amendment bill was reported in the press, not verified here.
- ASCI annual complaints report 2025-26 (published May 2026): food and beverage sector 376 cases; 88% of ads processed needed modification; names "The Dangerous Illusion of Disease Reversal": ads "promising to 'reverse fatty liver' ... or 'reduce HbA1c' within fixed timelines". The report says ads on diabetes cure were flagged to the Ministry of AYUSH under the DMR Act (274 ads flagged; "diabetes cure" listed among recurring violations). OPENED: https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/annual-complaints-report-25-26-final.pdf (dated May 2026 by URL). ASCI is self-regulation, not statute. I did NOT locate a standalone ASCI healthcare-claims guideline text (NOT FOUND in this pass).
- NMC public notice 6 Oct 2026 on ethical advertising by doctors, incl. a limit on AI in promotion (Telangana Today headline "No more guaranteed cures", SNIPPET: https://telanganatoday.com/no-more-guaranteed-cures-nmc-issues-new-guidelines-to-curb-misleading-medical-ads-on-social-media). Relevant if doctors endorse the product.
