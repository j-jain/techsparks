# D4-02 How fast a dose change works (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-14)

## Digest

12 facts that matter most:

1. Steady state: amlodipine 7 to 8 days (F01); telmisartan about 4 to 5 days (F05, our arithmetic); chlorthalidone about 7 to 12 days (F08, our arithmetic); HCTZ 1 to 3 days (F11).
2. Across 18 trials, half of the full BP fall arrives in about 1 week, whatever the class (F36); if exponential, about 78% at 2 weeks and 95% at 4 (F37, our arithmetic).
3. Telmisartan: US label, most effect within 2 weeks, maximum by about 4 (F06); EU-style SmPC, maximum at 4 to 8 weeks, a caution before raising the dose (F43).
4. Amlodipine label: "wait 7 to 14 days between titration steps" (F03); a 1987 trial titrated every 2 weeks (F35).
5. Thiazides: early fall from salt and water loss, lasting fall from lower vessel resistance by 6 to 8 weeks (F39). In an Indian ambulatory trial chlorthalidone's week-4 effect was about 56% of week 12 (F44); office data show most by week 2 (F41, F10).
6. HCTZ 12.5 mg did not lower 24-h BP in the Indian trial; it wears off within the day (F42).
7. WHO 2021: monthly until target, low certainty (F18). ESH 2023: monthly, but 2-weekly visits gave earlier control (observational plus 1 small RCT); no "rigid rules" (F20). ISH: control within 3 months (F19). NICE: no interval (F22).
8. India: the Punjab IHCI sheet allows titration "at 15 days frequency if required" (F15); Madhya Pradesh says 30 days only (F16).
9. IHCI Punjab and Maharashtra: 70 to 76% controlled on amlodipine 5 mg; control plateaued at 77 to 82% from step 3 or 4 (F17).
10. Faster titration: 2-weekly protocol visits beat usual care at 12 months in a 156-patient RCT (F24); 2-week encounter intervals normalised BP in 0.7 vs 1.9 months (observational, F25).
11. Safety data on faster loops are thin: similar adverse events with monthly home-guided titration (F28); no falls or hypotension in a 42-patient pilot (F31); no stops for adverse events with accelerated titration in Nigeria (F34).
12. Once controlled, 6-monthly review equalled 3-monthly (F27).

Surprises: India's Punjab sheet already permits 15-day steps (F15). US and EU telmisartan texts disagree, 4 vs 4 to 8 weeks (F06, F43). Chlorthalidone's ambulatory effect kept growing after week 4 (F44).

Against the starting leads: the evidence that faster stepping helps is mostly observational (F25, F26), and later protocol steps added few controlled patients in IHCI (F17).

## What this suggests for the product

- Suggestion: keep 30 days as the default step. Readings on days 24 to 30 sit at about 93 to 96% of the expected full effect (OUR ARITHMETIC from F36: 1 - 0.5^(3.43/0.91) = 0.93; 1 - 0.5^(4.29/0.91) = 0.96).
- Suggestion: a 14-day step (readings days 8 to 14) would see only about 58 to 78% of the full effect (OUR ARITHMETIC, same model, F37). The loop would then step up some patients whose current dose would have worked. Offer it only where a label or an Indian sheet supports it: amlodipine 5 to 10 mg (F03, F15, F35).
- Suggestion: no fast track after starting or raising chlorthalidone or HCTZ (F39, F44), or telmisartan 40 to 80 mg (F43). If the reading is "still high" but close to 135/85, allow one more 30-day cycle at the same dose.
- Suggestion: start counting the reading window only after steady state. That is about day 8 for amlodipine and day 12 for chlorthalidone (F01, F08).
- Suggestion: store a predicted response curve for each step (t50 about 1 week, F36). A day-14 home mean with almost no fall should trigger the "was the medicine taken" question early, without stepping up early.
- Suggestion: if HCTZ replaces chlorthalidone, record the time of dose and reading. HCTZ's short action (F11, F42) can make morning pre-dose readings look high.
- Suggestion: once controlled, drop to sparse home checks (F27, F19).
- Suggestion: log side effects at every step. IHCI could not (F17), so the product's own data would be new evidence on faster stepping (F31, F34).
