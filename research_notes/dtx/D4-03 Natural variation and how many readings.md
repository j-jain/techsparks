# D4-03 Natural variation and how many readings (agent: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-14)

## Digest

1. Error of a home mean (untreated adults): week-to-week SD of mean SBP 6.37 mmHg for 3 days, 4.36 for 7 days (D4-03-F33). 95% band about +/- 8.8 mmHg (3-day) and +/- 6.0 (7-day) (F34, OUR ARITHMETIC).
2. Smallest real change at 95%: 8.5 mmHg between two 7-day means, 12.5 between two 3-day means (F34); 13 to 14 between monthly means on stable drugs, where month-to-month SD is 4.7 to 5.1 (F11, F61).
3. Single readings in treated older patients: within-week SD 11.8 mmHg (F10, F62).
4. 3 days suffice for most; extend to 7 near the threshold (F02, F03, F04). In treated patients a 3-day mean is off the 7-day mean by 3.7 mmHg on average (F06).
5. Day 1 runs 2.8/1.4 mmHg high (F01); the effect fades after 6 months of self-monitoring (F16). Sources disagree on discarding it (F17).
6. The second reading of a pair averages 2.3 mmHg lower than the first, with wide swings (F36); both predict outcomes equally (F37).
7. UK titration trials changed drugs only after "two consecutive months" above target (F13, F56).
8. On unchanged drugs, a raised 6-month retest reflects a true rise only 25% of the time (F11); a >10 mmHg clinic rise gave 6 false alarms per true one (F15).
9. Only 3% of the apparent spread in individual systolic response to treatment is real (F55).
10. Treated Indians (GRAND, Nov 2026): 21.8% masked uncontrolled, 5.1% white-coat uncontrolled (F40). Untreated (India Heart Study): 23.7% white-coat, 18.2% masked (F21).
11. India winter minus summer: 9.0/5.6 mmHg in Punjab (F31), 11.1/6.8 in Delhi (F32), both visit readings; Japanese home readings 3 to 4 mmHg (F28).
12. Regression to the mean in home BP: a group starting at 156 fell to 143 in a year (F26).

Surprises: individual response is nearly all noise (F55); India's seasonal swing is about one drug step (F31, F32); masked uncontrolled is four times white-coat uncontrolled in treated Indians (F40).

Against the leads: ESH 2021 says dose self-adjustment is "to be avoided, if not under guidance by the physician in charge" (F07), which fits doctor sign-off. The digital titration trial gained only 3.4 mmHg over usual care (F56).

## What this suggests for the product

- Suggestion: treat 135/85 as a band, not a line. With 7 valid days, call "still high" without doubt at a mean of about 141 or more. For a mean between 135 and 141, ask for a second cycle before stepping up, as TASMIN-SR and HOME BP did (F13, F34, F56).
- Suggestion: if only 3 to 4 days are valid and the mean is within about 9 mmHg of 135, extend measuring rather than decide (F02, F04, F34).
- Suggestion: drop day 24 (the first day of the window) when at least 3 other valid days remain, at least in the first 6 months; average both readings of each pair (F01, F16, F36, F37).
- Suggestion: never act on one reading except at the 180/110 safety level, and then repeat it after a rest (F53, F54, F62, F13).
- Suggestion: report a change as "real" only if two 7-day means differ by 8.5 mmHg or more; label smaller changes "within normal variation" (F34, F61).
- Suggestion: add a season flag for India (November to February rise, April to June fall) so winter rises are not over-read and summer lows are checked for over-treatment (F28, F31, F32).
- Suggestion: offer home cycles to patients who look controlled in clinic, since about 1 in 5 treated Indians are high only at home (F40).
- Suggestion: do not credit or blame the drug on one cycle's change after a high starting value; expect regression to the mean (F26, F55, F57).

## Findings

### Q5. How many home readings or days give a reliable mean; error of a 3-day against a 7-day mean

- D4-03-F01 | Optimal and minimum home schedule (pooled 2,122 people, Finland, Greece, UK) | Home BP fell over successive days and reached a plateau after day 3. Day 1 was higher than later days by about 2.8/1.4 mmHg (P < 0.001). In a 3-day schedule, dropping day 1 changed the hypertension diagnosis in 120 people (6%) and gave a clinically important fall in 115 (5%); with more than 3 days, dropping day 1 had negligible impact. The 3-day mean correlated strongly with awake ambulatory BP, with little gain after. Authors: 7 days recommended, minimum 3; discard day 1, especially for a 3-day schedule (average days 2 and 3). Mechanism: not stated beyond the day-by-day decline (habituation). Kyriakoulis, Stergiou et al., J Hypertens 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35762478/ | source dated 2022-07 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F02 | Systematic review of self-monitoring schedules (37 studies) | Prognostic power: 72% to 91% of the theoretical maximum predictive value reached by 3 days, 86% to 96% by 7 days. Going beyond 3 days did not improve correlation with ambulatory monitoring. No convincing evidence that time of day, number of readings per day, or ignoring the first day mattered. Authors' rule: measure for 3 days, extend to 7 only when the mean is close to a diagnostic or treatment threshold. Hodgkinson, McManus et al., Am J Hypertens 2019 (updates a NICE review). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30668627/ | source dated 2019-03 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F03 | Number of readings for a reliable home mean (IDH study, USA, untreated, n = 316, 14 days) | Mean home SBP and DBP showed excellent reliability with 2 morning plus 2 evening readings for at least 2 days, or 1 morning plus 1 evening (or 2 morning) readings for at least 3 days. For diagnosing home hypertension, good agreement (kappa) needed at least 3 days of 2 morning + 2 evening or 1 morning + 1 evening readings. Conclusion: 3 days of morning and evening readings are needed. Bello, Shimbo et al., J Am Heart Assoc 2018. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30371272/ | source dated 2018-10 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F04 | Sequential stopping rule with upper and lower thresholds (IDH, untreated, n = 361) | Using thresholds with >90% positive or negative predictive value against a separate 7-day reference week (high = >=130/80), 63.7% could stop after 1 day, 17.1% after 2, 10.5% after 3, 3.3% after 4, 3.6% after 5, 1.4% after 6. Conclusion: in most people high home BP can be identified or excluded with 3 days or less. Mechanism: people far from the threshold need few readings; only those near it need more. Bradley, Shimbo et al., Hypertension 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37577827/ | source dated 2023-10 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F05 | Reliability of 1 week of home BP against office and ambulatory (IDH, n = 408, untreated, USA) | Reliability coefficient for 1 week of home BP 0.938 systolic and 0.918 diastolic, against 0.894/0.847 for 3 office visits and 0.846/0.843 for one 24-h ambulatory recording. 10 mmHg higher home SBP was associated with 5.07 g/m2 higher left ventricular mass index after adjusting for office and ambulatory BP. Schwartz, Shimbo et al., J Am Coll Cardiol 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33334418/ | source dated 2020-12 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F06 | 3-day against 7-day home mean in treated patients (TASMINH4 post hoc, UK, subgroup n = 84) | Reference = 7 days with day 1 excluded. At month 1 of monitoring, a 3-day mean was on average 1.08 mmHg systolic higher (95% CI 0.03 to 2.13) and its mean absolute difference from the reference was 3.66 mmHg (2.92 to 4.39) systolic, 1.97 mmHg (1.67 to 2.27) diastolic. 5 days: mean absolute difference 2.19 mmHg systolic (all days) or 1.83 (day 1 excluded). At month 6 the 3-day mean absolute difference fell to 2.91 mmHg (2.41 to 3.41) systolic. Morris, McManus, Stevens et al., J Hypertens 2025 (Table 4). https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12237135 | source dated 2025-08 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F07 | Long-term schedule recommendation (ESH Working Group position paper 2021) | Box 15: 7-day monitoring, not fewer than 3 days; duplicate morning and evening readings after 5 min sitting rest and 1 min between readings; before drug intake if treated; average all readings ("the need of discarding the first day is matter of debate"). Treated and controlled: 7 days before each clinic visit, at least 1 week within 3 months. Daily monitoring discouraged. Self-adjustment of dose "to be avoided, if not under guidance by the physician in charge". The 7-day schedule is recommended in the titration phase too. Parati, Stergiou et al., J Hypertens 2021. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9904446 | source dated 2021-09 | OPENED | CITED

Associations with ambulatory BP, LV mass and urine albumin improved mostly within the first 4 days (16 readings); discarding day 1 gave no improvement; morning and evening home BP had similar associations with organ damage. A Finnish study found home BP lower at weekends than on workdays. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9904446 | source dated 2021-09 | OPENED | CITED

### Q1. How much home readings vary (within-person SD)

- D4-03-F10 | Within-week SD of single home SBP readings in treated patients (TASMINH4, UK, n = 225, mean age about 67) | Mean within-person SD of systolic readings within one patient-week was 11.8 mmHg (95% CI 11.7 to 11.9) at month 1, 11.8 at month 3 and 11.0 (10.9 to 11.1) at month 6 of monitoring; coefficient of variation 12.7% at month 1. Proposed reason: familiarity with self-measurement. Note: this SD pools morning, evening, first and second readings, so it includes the morning-evening difference. (The text gives the month-6 reduction as both -0.8 and -0.08 mmHg; the table supports about -0.8.) Morris et al., J Hypertens 2025, Table 3. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12237135 | source dated 2025-08 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F11 | Month-to-month variation of the home mean on stable treatment (HOMEBP n = 232, TASMINH4 n = 582, UK) | With home BP <=135/85 and unchanged medication, mean SBP drift was -0.1 mmHg per month (SD 0.6) and -0.2 (SD 0.7). Intra-individual systolic SD across monthly means was 4.7 and 5.1 mmHg. From a starting home SBP of 130, retesting at 6 months gave an 18% chance of a mean >=135, with only a 25% chance that this was a true rise; at 12 months, 26% chance of >=135 with 65% probability of a true rise. Rose, Stevens, McManus et al., J Hypertens 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40838357/ | source dated 2025-11 | OPENED | CITED

### Q6. Published rules for "still high" or "changed"

- D4-03-F13 | TASMIN-SR self-titration rule (UK trial protocol) | Self-monitoring in the first week of each month, mornings; two seated readings 5 minutes apart, and "The second of these readings will be used" to decide. "Four or more above target readings in two consecutive weeks of measurement will require a change in medication." Each of 3 plan steps was one medication change after "two consecutive months of raised readings". Very high or very low readings persisting on a third reading 5 min later meant contacting the practice. McManus et al., BMC Cardiovasc Disord 2013 (protocol). https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC3623796 | source dated 2013-03 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F14 | HOMEBP schedule-reduction rule (UK) | Seven consecutive days of home readings; if mean home BP was controlled for 3 consecutive months (100 to 134 systolic, <=84 diastolic), monitoring dropped to once every 8 weeks, reverting to monthly if the mean rose to >=135/85. Described in Rose et al., J Hypertens 2025. https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12517721 | source dated 2025-11 | OPENED | CITED
- D4-03-F15 | False alarms when looking for a rise in BP on stable treatment (PROGRESS trial clinic data, n = 1,709, Asia, Australasia, Europe) | Six months after BP stabilised on fixed perindopril plus indapamide, an observed systolic rise of >10 mmHg gave 6 false positives for every true rise of >=10 mmHg; for a 20 mmHg rise the ratio was over 200. For diastolic rises of 5 and 10 mmHg: 3.5 and 39. True and false positives reached parity only when measurements were 21 months apart. Mean cohort BP did not change over 33 months. (Clinic readings, mean of 2, not home.) Keenan, Irwig et al., BMJ 2009. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19406886/ | source dated 2009-04 | OPENED | CITED

### Q6 (continued). Rules used in named titration trials
